\begin{theorem}[The whole source initial ideal uses the same reduced canonical components]
\label{hir:actual-reduced-canonical-homogeneous-initial-span}
\uses{hir:actual-reduced-basis-entire-filtered-division,hir:actual-homogeneous-initial-ideal-hs-equality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualPointOrderRetainingHS.actualHomogeneousInitialIdeal_eq_reduced_component_span}
\leanok
For an actual formal ideal $I$, let $G_a$ be its canonical reduced
vertex series and $d_a=|a|$. Its entire homogeneous initial ideal is
exactly the whole span of the actual components $(G_a)_{d_a}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Degree-first leading exponents put each $G_a$ in
$\mathfrak m^{d_a}$ and identify $d_a$ as its least nonzero degree.
For $F\in I\cap\mathfrak m^q$, the genuine reduced canonical division
gives $F=\sum_aQ_aG_a$, with each
$Q_a\in\mathfrak m^{q-d_a}$. If $d_a\le q$, the actual degree-$q$
component of this product is $(Q_a)_{q-d_a}(G_a)_{d_a}$, by the genuine
finite convolution and the two lower order bounds. If $d_a>q$, that
component vanishes because the whole product lies in
$\mathfrak m^{q+1}$. Thus the degree-$q$ component of every such $F$
lies in the displayed whole span. Apply this to the least degree of
each nonzero member of $I$ to obtain the first ideal inclusion.
Conversely each $G_a$ is an actual nonzero member of $I$ with least
degree $d_a$, so its displayed component is its actual homogeneous
initial form and belongs to the entire initial ideal. Taking the
whole span proves the reverse inclusion.
\end{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous chart polynomials are the actual sheared source forms]
\label{hir:actual-point-homogeneous-dehom-shear-identity}
\uses{hir:actual-point-blowup-homogeneous-polynomial-factor,hir:actual-formal-blowup-all-directions-whole-power-reflection}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualPointOrderRetainingHS.centerShear_eq_dehom_of_homogeneous_dehom}
\leanok
Let $f$ be an actual homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$ and let
$\delta_bf$ be its literal point-chart dehomogenization. If this
actual polynomial is homogeneous of the same degree $d$, then
$\sigma_b(f)=\delta_bf$ as entire formal series.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The literal dehomogenization contains no pivot variable, because
that variable was replaced by $1$. Its zero-direction
dehomogenization is therefore the same whole polynomial. Genuine
homogeneous blowup factorization gives
$\pi_b^*f=x_e^d\delta_bf$ and
$\pi_0^*(\delta_bf)=x_e^d\delta_bf$. The actual all-direction
factorization also gives
$\pi_b^*f=\pi_0^*\sigma_b(f)$. These identities identify the actual
zero-chart blowup images of $\sigma_b(f)$ and $\delta_bf$.
The proved injectivity of that actual whole formal blowup therefore
gives the asserted entire series identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The same point family identifies the whole source initial ideal by a genuine shear]
\label{hir:actual-point-order-retention-whole-source-shear-dictionary}
\uses{hir:actual-reduced-canonical-homogeneous-initial-span,hir:actual-point-homogeneous-dehom-shear-identity,hir:actual-point-canonical-quotient-pure-coefficient}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualPointOrderRetainingHS.canonical_point_order_retention_shadow_span}
\leanok

reduced generators in an actual point chart. If each $H_a$ retains its
original vertex order $d_a$, the actual shear maps the ENTIRE source
homogeneous initial ideal exactly onto the whole literal span
$(\rho_e(\widehat H_a))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

$\rho_e(\widehat H_a)=\delta_bf_a$, where $f_a$ is the degree-$d_a$
polynomial component of the SAME original reduced canonical
$\widehat G_a$. Indeed it gives every coefficient with zero pivot
exponent, and both sides have zero coefficient at every other exponent.
The actual chart polynomial has degree at most $d_a$, because every
variable was replaced by a polynomial of degree at most one. The
assumed current order puts $\widehat H_a$ and its pivot projection in
$\mathfrak m^{d_a}$. Consequently the chart polynomial has no
coefficients below $d_a$ and none above $d_a$, so it is homogeneous
of degree $d_a$. The preceding actual shear identity identifies it
with $\sigma_b(f_a)$ as a whole series. The first theorem identifies
the entire source initial ideal with the span of these SAME reduced
canonical components. The actual ideal image of that whole span is
the span of their actual shear images, now exactly the indicated
literal pivot shadows. This proves both whole ideal directions.
\end{proof}
