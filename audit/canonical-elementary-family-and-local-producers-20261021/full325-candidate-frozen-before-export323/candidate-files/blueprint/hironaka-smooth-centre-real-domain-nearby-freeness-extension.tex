constructed quotient domain. One denominator is chosen for the whole finite

\begin{theorem}[One actual nonvanishing open makes every entire original nearby associated module free]
\label{hir:real-domain-original-nearby-associated-free-open}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\uses{hir:real-domain-original-common-domain-coefficient-initial-family,hir:relative-normal-coefficient-uniform-denominator-free-images,hir:relative-normal-graded-internal-finite-relations}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothCentreRealDomainNearbyFreeness.exists_original_regular_real_nearby_associated_free_open}
\leanok
ONLY original regular-centre geometry and original ideal coherence internally
supply ONE genuine flattening and ONE actual real open $U$ meeting every
neighbourhood of zero. Its tangent inclusion lies in the actual target and
coordinate centre plane. At EVERY $a\in U$, the ENTIRE original associated
graded module of the actual translated original flattened family is free over
the actual base holomorphic germ ring. No caller denominator, free open,
finite family, rank or flatness witness is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original real-domain coefficient theorem supplies one finite type domain
$D$, a real coefficient neighbourhood $W$ containing zero, actual nearby
coefficient maps, and one finite homogeneous polynomial ideal presenting
every ENTIRE original nearby initial ideal. The genuine uniform polynomial
construction over the fraction field of $D$ supplies a single nonzero leading
denominator $b\in D$ whose unit coefficient images make every entire
polynomial quotient free. Choose a representative $c$ of $b$ in the actual
coefficient-function subalgebra and let $g$ be its actual holomorphic function.
Its whole origin germ is nonzero: a zero germ would put $c$ in the complete
origin coefficient kernel, making its quotient class $b$ zero.
Put $U=\{a\in W:g(a)\ne0\}$. At every included point actual holomorphicity
makes $g$ continuous, so $U$ is open. If an origin neighbourhood missed $U$,
$g$ would vanish on its intersection with the genuine neighbourhood $W$,
contradicting its nonzero whole origin germ. Thus $U$ meets every origin
neighbourhood. The actual factorisation formula identifies the nearby image
of $b$ with the whole translated germ of $g$. Its value at zero is $g(a)$,
which is nonzero on $U$, so this image is a unit. The uniform polynomial
coefficient-image theorem gives the whole polynomial quotient free over the
actual base germ ring. Its entire ideal is the ORIGINAL nearby initial ideal.
The genuine original whole symbol quotient equivalence therefore transfers
freeness to the ENTIRE associated graded module. Restrict the original target
and coordinate-plane dictionary along $U\subset W\subset B$. Every point
uses the same denominator, ideal, flattening and actual open.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The same real open makes every original canonical centre degree finite free and flat]
\label{hir:real-domain-original-all-degree-canonical-centre-free-open}

\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothCentreRealDomainNearbyFreeness.exists_original_regular_real_all_degree_canonical_centre_free_open}
\leanok

centre action. The centre action is the genuine action descended from the

truncations is introduced.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At each actual included point the preceding theorem gives freeness of the
entire original associated direct sum over the base holomorphic germ ring.

inclusion and projection, hence projective and flat. The whole homogeneous

module is free. This works for every degree on the SAME open, without any
further shrinking. The independently descended original centre action is

and the actual identity semilinear map on EVERY original degree element.
Its inverse sends a tangent basis to a basis for the actual centre action,
and sends finite tangent generators to finite centre generators. Thus each
original degree is finite free over the genuine centre ring and therefore
flat. The whole original associated direct sum has the same genuine identity

All actual included points remain in the true target and centre plane. This
proves the coordinate original normal-module result; the original nonlinear

\end{proof}
