\begin{lemma}[The conventional strict transform from a dense preserved open]
\label{hir:original-strict-transform-dense-preserved-open}
\uses{mc:first-centre-exists,mc:first-centre-smooth-part,hir:embedded-resolution-local}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalStoppedPrefixSmoothComparison.strictTransform_eq_closure_preimage_inter_of_centresAvoid}
\leanok
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
Let $T:D\to X$ be an actual finite blowup tower, $O\subseteq X$ open,
and $W\subseteq X$ with $W\subseteq\overline{W\cap O}$.  If every actual
centre of $T$ avoids the inverse image of $O$, then
\[
 \operatorname{Str}_p(W)=\overline{p^{-1}(W\cap O)},\qquad p=T.\mathrm{proj}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are no exceptional points of $p$ over $O$.  Thus
$p^{-1}(W\cap O)$ is contained in $p^{-1}W\setminus\operatorname{Exc}p$,
which gives one inclusion after taking closures.  Conversely take
$y\in p^{-1}W\setminus\operatorname{Exc}p$ and a neighbourhood $V$ of $y$.
The differential of $p$ at $y$ is invertible.  The local inverse theorem
therefore gives $p_*(\mathcal N_y)=\mathcal N_{p(y)}$, so $p(V)$ is a
neighbourhood of $p(y)$.  Original density supplies a point of
$p(V)\cap W\cap O$.  A representative of that image point lies in
$V\cap p^{-1}(W\cap O)$.  Hence $y$ belongs to the latter closure.
That closure is closed, so it contains the closure defining the entire
conventional strict transform, which proves the other inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole strict-transform equality on an actual smooth Cartesian tower]
\label{hir:original-stopped-strict-transform-smooth-square}
\uses{hir:original-strict-transform-dense-preserved-open,hir:smooth-original-ambient-canonical-history-values,hir:submersion-relative-control-joint-tower}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalStoppedPrefixSmoothComparison.IsActualTowerPullback.centresAvoid_preimage,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalStoppedPrefixSmoothComparison.IsActualTowerPullback.strictTransform_preimage_of_centresAvoid}
\leanok
\source{bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
Let $b:Y\to X$ be a holomorphic submersion and let
$(b,Q,T,g)$ be an actual Cartesian pullback of finite blowup towers, with
projections $q:C\to Y$ and $p:D\to X$.  Suppose the original $O,W,T$
satisfy the preceding lemma.  Then the actual source centres avoid
$b^{-1}O$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Str}_q(b^{-1}W)=g^{-1}\operatorname{Str}_p(W).
\]
No equality of strict transforms or exceptional sets is a premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the actual pullback tower.  At an added step the source centre
is the literal inverse image of the target centre.  If a source point in
this centre projected into $b^{-1}O$, the actual projection square would
put its target point in the target centre over $O$, contradicting target
avoidance.  This proves source avoidance, including empty centres.

The submersion $b$ is continuous and open.  Consequently inverse image
under $b$ commutes with closure, and original density gives
$b^{-1}W\subseteq\overline{b^{-1}(W\cap O)}$.  Apply the preceding lemma
to both towers.  The actual upper map $g$ is also a continuous open
submersion, so its inverse image commutes with the target closure.
Finally $pg=bq$ identifies the sets inside those closures.  These three
equalities give the claimed equality of whole strict transforms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An original embedded stopped prefix and its genuine arbitrary smooth source]
\label{hir:original-canonical-embedded-stopped-smooth-comparison}
\uses{hir:original-stopped-strict-transform-smooth-square,hir:smooth-original-compact-canonical-terminal-object,hir:embedded-from-resolution,hir:embedded-resolution-snc,hir:submersion-relative-regular-geometry,hir:submersion-relative-nc-transversality,hir:submersion-relative-control-joint-tower,hir:smooth-original-ambient-canonical-history-values}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalStoppedPrefixSmoothComparison.exists_original_smooth_embedded_stopped_prefix}
\leanok
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold, $V\subset X$ a closed irreducible
analytic subvariety with $\dim V<n$ and coherent whole vanishing ideal.
Let $b:Y\to X$ be any holomorphic submersion, without compactness of $Y$.
For the literal $M=(X,X,\varnothing,I_V,1)$, internally construct its compact
canonical terminal tower $T$.  There are an actual canonical prefix $T_0$
and actual remaining tail $R$ with $T_0R=T$, and an internally constructed
actual Cartesian smooth pullback $Q_0$ of this same prefix, such that:
the centres of $T_0$ avoid $O=X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}V$, its next
actual maximal centre meets the inverse image of $O$, and the centres of
$Q_0$ avoid $b^{-1}O$.  Write $p=T_0.\mathrm{proj}$, $q=Q_0.\mathrm{proj}$,
$g$ for the actual upper submersion and $E$ for the actual transformed
boundary on the target prefix.  Both projections are proper surjective
holomorphic admissible blowup sequences, with centres over the original
singular set $\operatorname{Sing}V$ and its inverse image $b^{-1}\operatorname{Sing}V$.
Their boundaries are exactly $E$ and $g^{-1}E$, and both are SNC.

The target prefix is canonical for $M$, the source prefix is canonical
for the literal marked pullback $b^*M$, and both are actual geometric test
towers.  The square $pg=bq$ has the holomorphic Cartesian universal
property.  Its whole strict transforms satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Str}_q(b^{-1}V)=g^{-1}\operatorname{Str}_p(V).
\]
They are closed smooth submanifolds, regular respectively of dimensions
$\dim V$ and $\dim V+(m-n)$ at all their points, and have normal crossings
with their exact accumulated boundaries.  Both maps are isomorphisms over
their stated good open sets, and their exceptional images lie outside
those opens.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The dimension bound and coherence put $M$ in the invariant domain.  The
compact driver internally supplies an actual canonical terminal tower.
Some actual centre meets $O$: otherwise the nonempty original regular
locus would remain in the final cosupport, contrary to terminality and
surjectivity of the tower projection.  Take the first such centre.  The
first-centre theorem retains the actual prefix $T_0$, tail $R$ and
factorization, its canonicality, avoidance of $O$, and a point of the
actual next maximum over $O$.

The identity principle for this maximum says that the strict transform
of $V$ is locally a component of that smooth admissible centre.  It
therefore is closed smooth, has dimension $\dim V$ everywhere, and has
normal crossings with the actual accumulated boundary $E$.  Actual
testness also gives that $E$ is SNC.  The centres-avoidance dictionary
places every actual target centre over $\operatorname{Sing}V$.

Construct the actual smooth pullback tower of this very $T_0$.  The
original canonical transport theorem gives source canonicality for
$b^*M$, and the whole transformed-record dictionary gives actual source
testness and its literal boundary $E$ pulled back through $g$.  Original
density $V\subseteq\overline{V\cap O}$ and the preceding theorem give
source avoidance and the whole conventional strict-transform equality.
Smooth submersion pullback transfers closed smoothness, its exact fibre
dimension, SNC of the boundary, and normal crossings of the strict carrier
with that boundary.  Both projections are proper holomorphic surjections
because they are actual finite blowup towers.  Testness and the centres
over the stated original singular sets give the two admissible-sequence
conclusions.  Finally avoidance puts each exceptional image outside its
good open set, and the finite blowup-sequence isomorphism theorem gives
the two good-open isomorphisms.  These arguments retain the selected
stopped prefix and every actual source position, including empty ones.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The source canonical marked input here is exactly $b^*M$, whose ideal is
the whole pulled-back ideal $b^*I_V$.  No equality
$b^*I_V=I_{b^{-1}V}$ for singular $V$ is assumed or concluded.  Comparing
this constructed source with an independently selected stopped source
prefix is a further statement, especially when target centres become
empty under a nonsurjective base map.  Neither a common stopping index
nor the full embedded or boundary functoriality assertion follows here.
\end{remark}
