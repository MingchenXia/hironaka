\section{The whole actual homogeneous initial ideal is the extension of its literal polynomial contraction}

For any genuine formal ideal $K\subset\mathbb C[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$,
write $J=\operatorname{actualHomogeneousInitialIdeal}(K)$ for the
existing whole ideal generated by the true lowest homogeneous forms
of all nonzero members of $K$. Let $\iota$ be the existing literal
\texttt{MvPolynomial.coeToMvPowerSeries.ringHom}. The polynomial ideal
used below is exactly $\iota^{-1}(J)$, not a chosen initial family or
new proxy for $J$.

\begin{theorem}[Every actual homogeneous series component is the inclusion of its genuine finite polynomial truncation]
\label{hir:actual-every-homogeneous-component-finite-polynomial}
\uses{hir:actual-homogeneous-initial-form-coefficients}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualInitialPolynomialContraction.homogeneousComponent_truncTotal_coe}
\leanok
For every actual formal series $F$ and every degree $q\ge0$, the
literal inclusion of the polynomial
$\operatorname{truncTotal}_{q+1}(\operatorname{HC}_q(F))$ is exactly
the entire unchanged series $\operatorname{HC}_q(F)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At an arbitrary exponent $\alpha$, genuine total truncation retains
the coefficient if $|\alpha|<q+1$. In that case the equality is
literal coefficient equality. Otherwise $q+1\le|\alpha|$, so
$|\alpha|\ne q$; the original homogeneous-component coefficient at
$\alpha$ is therefore zero, exactly as is the truncated polynomial
coefficient. Genuine coefficient extensionality proves the equality
for the whole series. Finiteness comes from the original polynomial
truncation over the finite variable set, not a supplied support
certificate. The proof includes $q=0$ and the zero series.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Both whole ideal directions identify the unchanged actual initial ideal with the extension of its literal polynomial contraction]
\label{hir:actual-whole-initial-ideal-polynomial-contraction-extension}
\uses{hir:actual-every-homogeneous-component-finite-polynomial,hir:actual-homogeneous-initial-form-coefficients}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualInitialPolynomialContraction.actualHomogeneousInitialIdeal_eq_map_polynomial_comap}
\leanok
For every actual entire formal ideal $K$,
\[
 J=\operatorname{Ideal.map}(\iota)
       \bigl(\operatorname{Ideal.comap}(\iota)(J)\bigr).
\]
Both maps are the existing genuine polynomial-to-series homomorphism,
and $J$ is the literal whole actual homogeneous initial ideal of the
same $K$. No finite selected initial family or derivative-stability
certificate is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Extension of the literal contraction is contained in $J$ by the
actual map/comap adjunction. For the opposite inclusion, apply the
actual span description of the unchanged $J$. Every span generator
is the true lowest homogeneous form $\operatorname{HC}_q(F)$ of an
arbitrary nonzero member $F\in K$. The preceding theorem identifies
this entire series with the inclusion of its genuine finite
polynomial truncation. That polynomial belongs to the literal
contraction, because its inclusion is exactly this actual generator
of $J$. Consequently its inclusion belongs to the extension of the
contraction. Ideal span closure gives the whole opposite inclusion,
for every original formal generator and arbitrary formal ideal
coefficients. No convergence of formal coefficients or polynomial
replacement of the ambient series ideal is used.

This identity supplies the genuine whole-series dictionary for the
separate polynomial tangent-derivative/cylindrical criterion. The
criterion itself, normal-coordinate extension, normal flatness,
compatible analytic vertex families and general Bennett remain
separate conclusions.
\end{proof}
