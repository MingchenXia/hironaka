\section{An actual new initial exponent forces a finite strict Hilbert--Samuel drop}

Write $\Gamma(I)$ for the actual degree-lex initial diagram of a whole
formal ideal $I\subset\mathbb C[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$. The inclusion
$\Gamma(I)\subseteq\Gamma(J)$ below is an explicit mathematical
hypothesis. It is not inferred from equality of a normalized ideal
with a geometric strict ideal. No normalized-order or history tracking
criterion is claimed here.

\begin{theorem}[Every actual added initial exponent forces a specific strict drop]
\label{hir:actual-new-diagram-exponent-strict-hs-drop}
\uses{hir:actual-formal-and-original-hs-standard-monomial-count}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualDiagramStrictHSDrop.genuineFormalHilbertSamuelValue_strict_lt_of_new_diagram_exponent}
\leanok
Let $I,J$ be whole formal ideals with
$\Gamma(I)\subseteq\Gamma(J)$. If
$\beta\in\Gamma(J)\setminus\Gamma(I)$, then
\[
 H_J(|\beta|)<H_I(|\beta|).
\]
These are the genuine entire finite-jet Hilbert--Samuel dimensions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual standard monomials of total degree less than
$|\beta|+1$ for $J$ form a subset of those for $I$, since diagram
inclusion reverses the complements. The literal identity on exponent
vectors gives an injective map between their genuine finite index types.
The vector $\beta$ belongs to the $I$ type, by its degree and
nonmembership in $\Gamma(I)$, but cannot lie in that map's image:
its membership in $\Gamma(J)$ contradicts the defining condition of
every source element. The map is not surjective, so the source finite
cardinality is strictly smaller. The proved actual whole standard
monomial bases identify these two cardinalities with the stated
original finite-jet dimensions, proving the strict inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The first extra degree is the first strict value drop, and whole profiles detect diagram equality]
\label{hir:actual-diagram-first-strict-hs-drop-and-rigidity}
\uses{hir:actual-new-diagram-exponent-strict-hs-drop,hir:actual-formal-and-original-hs-standard-monomial-count}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualDiagramStrictHSDrop.exists_first_hilbertSamuel_drop_of_diagram_ne,Hironaka.HironakaActualDiagramStrictHSDrop.genuineFormalHilbertSamuelValue_eq_iff_diagram_eq_of_subset}
\leanok
Under the same actual diagram inclusion, if the diagrams differ,
there is an actual integer $d$ such that $H_J(d)<H_I(d)$ and
$H_J(\ell)=H_I(\ell)$ for every $\ell<d$. Consequently their entire
Hilbert--Samuel profiles agree if and only if their whole actual
initial diagrams agree.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A proper inclusion has a genuine exponent in the difference.
Choose the least natural degree of such an exponent; the natural
well-ordering supplies an exponent of that exact degree $d$.
The preceding theorem gives the strict value inequality at $d$.
For $\ell<d$, every exponent of degree less than $\ell+1$ has degree
strictly less than $d$. If it belongs to $\Gamma(J)$ but not
$\Gamma(I)$, its degree contradicts the chosen least degree.
Thus the two whole diagrams have exactly the same membership on
these bounded exponent vectors. The actual identity of exponents
therefore gives an equivalence of their finite standard index types;
their cardinalities and genuine finite-jet dimensions agree.

If all values agree, a genuine exponent in the diagram difference
would contradict its specific strict inequality. Hence no difference
exponent exists, and the inclusion is equality. Conversely equality
of whole diagrams gives the same bounded index equivalence at every
order, and therefore all values agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The same finite strict drop concerns original analytic ideals]
\label{hir:actual-original-taylor-diagram-strict-hs-drop}
\uses{hir:actual-diagram-first-strict-hs-drop-and-rigidity,hir:actual-original-formal-entire-hs-transfer}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualDiagramStrictHSDrop.hilbertSamuelValue_strict_lt_of_new_taylor_diagram_exponent,Hironaka.HironakaActualDiagramStrictHSDrop.hilbertSamuelValue_eq_iff_taylor_diagram_eq_of_subset}
\leanok
Let $A,B$ be arbitrary original holomorphic origin ideals. Use their
literal whole Taylor extensions $\widehat A,\widehat B$, and assume
$\Gamma(\widehat A)\subseteq\Gamma(\widehat B)$. Every actual exponent
$\beta$ in the diagram difference gives the strict original analytic
inequality $H_B(|\beta|)<H_A(|\beta|)$. Their entire original analytic
profiles agree exactly when their whole Taylor initial diagrams agree.
No formalization or diagram identification is supplied by the caller.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The genuine original Taylor homomorphism sends each entire original
ideal to its whole formal extension. Its proved whole-jet quotient
algebra equivalence identifies every original analytic
Hilbert--Samuel value with the formal value of that very extension.
Apply the preceding actual formal strict inequality and rigidity
theorems to these two literal extensions, then use those same
whole-jet value identities on both sides. Thus the strict inequality
and every profile identity concern the original analytic ideals,
not an unrelated finite-dimensional model.
\end{proof}
