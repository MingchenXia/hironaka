\section{Effective gluing over the actual complex point}

Let $D$ be genuine native locally ringed open gluing data with pieces
$X_i$ and overlap maps $f_{ij},t_{ij}$. Let
$\sigma_i:X_i\to\mathrm{pt}_{\mathbb C}$ be their actual structural maps.
Assume the usual overlap equality
$f_{ij}\sigma_i=t_{ij}f_{ji}\sigma_j$.

\begin{theorem}[The actual complex structural map descends uniquely]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-complex-structural-map}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-complex-point-models}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedPointMap,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedPointMap_ι,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedPointMap_unique}
The actual native glued locally ringed space has an internally
constructed, unique map to the complex point whose restriction to
each genuine chart inclusion is $\sigma_i$.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The overlap equalities are precisely the two-arrow compatibility
conditions for the native gluing multicoequalizer. Its actual descent
map constructs the structural morphism. The multicoequalizer's actual
chart equations prove the prescribed restrictions, and its morphism
extensionality proves uniqueness. No global structural map is supplied.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Countably many actual analytic charts give second countability]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-second-countable}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-space-category}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.secondCountable_glued}
If the index type is countable and every original piece over the
complex point is an object of the actual analytic category, the
genuine glued topological space is second countable.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The native chart inclusions are actual open immersions, hence open
embeddings. Each is a homeomorphism onto its actual open range. These
ranges inherit second countability from the original analytic pieces.
They form a countable open cover by genuine joint surjectivity of the
native chart inclusions. The countable open-cover theorem proves
second countability of the actual glued space.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual local analytic models glue to an analytic object]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-object}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-complex-structural-map,hir:reduced-analytic-glue-second-countable,hir:reduced-analytic-space-category,hir:reduced-analytic-abstract-stalks-reduced}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.hasAnalyticCharts_glued,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedAnalyticSpace}
If the index type is countable, the original pieces are actual analytic
objects, and the genuine glued topological space is Hausdorff, the
constructed glued space over the complex point is an object of the
reduced complex analytic category. Its stalks are reduced local rings.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Second countability is proved above; Hausdorffness is the stated
topological gluing condition. Every actual glued point comes from a
point of an original piece. Choose a genuine closed analytic local
model at that original point and compose its actual open immersion
with the native chart inclusion. Composition is an open immersion,
and the proved structural-map descent equation makes it a chart over
the complex point. Its actual image contains the given glued point.
Thus the required analytic local-model property is derived from the
original pieces. Bundle this property in the native full subcategory.
Its stalks are reduced by the already proved analytic-model stalk
theorem, rather than by a new reducedness assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The original pieces remain actual open charts]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-chart-morphisms}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-object,hir:reduced-analytic-glue-complex-structural-map}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedChartMap,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedChartMaps_properties}
The native inclusions are genuine morphisms of the analytic category,
their underlying locally ringed maps are actual open immersions, and
their images cover the entire actual glued space.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each structural-map descent equation is exactly the commutative
triangle defining its slice morphism. The native full subcategory
therefore gives the genuine analytic morphism. Its underlying map is
the unchanged native chart inclusion, so actual open immersion and
whole-space coverage are the native gluing theorems themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual compatible complex-structural maps descend effectively]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-effective-map-descent}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-object,hir:reduced-analytic-glue-chart-morphisms,hir:reduced-analytic-glue-complex-structural-map}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.gluedAnalyticMap,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.exists_unique_glued_analytic_map}
Actual locally ringed maps from the original pieces to an analytic
object, compatible on the genuine overlaps and over the complex point,
internally construct a unique morphism from the glued analytic object
with exactly these actual chart restrictions.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the actual native multicoequalizer to construct the global locally
ringed map from the given compatible piece maps. On every original
chart, its composition with the target structural map equals the
source structural map, by the given local triangles and the proved
descent equations. Multicoequalizer extensionality therefore proves
the actual global structural triangle. It is a genuine slice morphism,
hence an analytic-category morphism. Its actual chart restrictions
are the original maps by the native descent equations. If another
analytic morphism has those same restrictions, native multicoequalizer
extensionality makes its underlying locally ringed map equal to this
one. Slice and full-subcategory extensionality prove uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Original two-model holomorphic data construct an actual analytic space]
\label{hir:reduced-analytic-original-two-model-gluing}
\uses{hir:reduced-analytic-glue-object,hir:reduced-analytic-glue-chart-morphisms,hir:actual-two-model-native-glue-data,hir:actual-two-model-complex-structural-compatibility,hir:reduced-analytic-complex-point-models}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.actualTwoModelAnalyticSpace,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.actualTwoModelChartMap,Hironaka.HironakaReducedComplexAnalyticGluing.actualTwoModelChartMap_properties}
Let the input consist of two original closed analytic models, original
open overlaps, actual holomorphic transition functions, and their
inverse identities on the original subsets. If the actual constructed
glued topological space is Hausdorff, these ordinary data internally
construct an object of the analytic category. Its underlying locally
ringed space is the actual native gluing, and the unchanged original
model inclusions are genuine analytic open immersions covering it.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual quotient-model construction produces the native overlap
maps, inverse isomorphisms and gluing data from the original functions.
Its proved structural-map compatibilities provide precisely the
descent equations above. Each original closed analytic model has the
proved actual analytic local-model property. The two-element index
type is countable. Apply the proved actual gluing construction with
the stated Hausdorff condition. Its underlying object is literally
the native glued locally ringed space, and its chart maps are the
unchanged native inclusions by definition. Their structural triangles,
open immersion properties and joint coverage have already been
proved. Thus no locally ringed gluing datum, local-model certificate,
or analytic-space object is supplied by the caller.
\end{proof}

Hausdorffness is an explicit condition: gluing Hausdorff pieces along
opens can produce a non-Hausdorff space. These results construct an
actual scalar-compatible analytic atlas gluing and its effective map
descent. Compatibility and descent of resolution centres, blowups and
terminal resolution histories remain separate geometric obligations.
