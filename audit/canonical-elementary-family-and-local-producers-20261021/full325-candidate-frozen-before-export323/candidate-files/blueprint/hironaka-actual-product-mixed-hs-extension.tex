\section{The genuine mixed maximal-power quotient of a smooth product}

All ideals and scalar actions are the original ones. Write
$B=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^d,0}$, $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{d+t},0}$,
$m=\mathfrak m_B$, $J=(z_1,\ldots,z_t)$, and let $I\subset B$ be arbitrary.
The actual ambient maximal ideal is $mR+J$. This computation retains its
mixed powers throughout, using genuine original monomials.

\begin{lemma}[Genuine monomial coordinates preserve every whole normal-power filtration image]
\label{hir:actual-product-monomial-normal-power-filtration}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-coefficient-separation,hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-entire-quotient-basis}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualNormalMonomial_mem_power,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductNormalTail,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualNormalPowerImage_coordinates}
\leanok
For $r\le s$, in the genuine monomial coordinates
$R/J^s\simeq B^{A_s}$, the actual image of $J^r$ is exactly the families
whose coefficients of every degree strictly below $r$ vanish.
Each original monomial $z^\alpha$ belongs to $J^{|\alpha|}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the monomial assertion, each factor $z_i^{\alpha_i}$ belongs to the
actual $\alpha_i$th power; multiply these genuine ideal-power memberships
and add their exponents. If a class has a representative $g\in J^r$,
its genuine monomial representative $P$ differs from $g$ by a member of
$J^s\subset J^r$. Thus $P\in J^r$, and the original whole coefficient
separation theorem makes every lower coefficient zero. Conversely a
family with lower coefficients zero has its original polynomial sum
supported only in degrees at least $r$. Each such original monomial is
in $J^{|\alpha|}\subset J^r$ by the first assertion; multiplying by its
whole original coefficient and summing gives a genuine representative
in $J^r$. These are both directions of the full image equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual mixed maximal powers have precisely the genuine coordinate weights]
\label{hir:actual-product-monomial-mixed-maximal-power-coordinates}
\uses{hir:actual-product-monomial-normal-power-filtration,hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-maximal-power}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductMixedCoordinates,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductMixedTailSum_eq_coordinates,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualMixedPowerImage_coordinates}
\leanok
In the SAME actual coordinates of $R/J^s$, the image of
$(mR+J)^s$ is precisely the families with
$a_\alpha\in m^{s-|\alpha|}$ for every $\alpha\in A_s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the true ideal power as
$\sum_{r=0}^s(mR)^{s-r}J^r$. The actual quotient and original $B$-linear
monomial maps carry each term to $m^{s-r}$ times the genuine degree-at-least
$r$ tail. Any such multiple has zero lower coordinates; a coordinate of
degree $q\ge r$ lies in $m^{s-r}\subset m^{s-q}$, proving one inclusion.
Conversely decompose an arbitrary finite coefficient family into its
single-coordinate vectors. The vector at index $\alpha$, of degree $q$,
is its coefficient $a_\alpha\in m^{s-q}$ times the unit coordinate vector
of that SAME original monomial. The unit vector lies in the true tail
of degree $q$, so this term belongs to the $r=q$ summand of the actual
mixed image. Their finite sum proves the other inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole original smooth-product ambient jets retain all actual weighted coefficient quotients]
\label{hir:actual-product-whole-ambient-jet-weighted-coefficient-quotient}
\uses{hir:actual-product-monomial-mixed-maximal-power-coordinates,hir:actual-product-entire-extension-ideal-module-quotient}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductCoordinateIdealSup,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductWholeIdealImage_coordinates,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualRingIdealModuleQuotientEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductMixedJetEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductMixedJetEquiv_monomial_sum,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductAmbientJetEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductAmbientJetEquiv_monomial_sum}
\leanok
The genuine original ambient quotient
$R/(IR+\mathfrak m_R^s)$ is $B$-linearly equivalent, and hence originally
complex-linearly equivalent, to
$\prod_{\alpha\in A_s}B/(I+m^{s-|\alpha|})$.
The class of EVERY original lower-degree monomial sum has exactly its
weighted whole original coefficient classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The full original normal power $J^s$ is a summand of the actual mixed
maximal power, so first quotienting by $J^s$ changes no final quotient.
In this true quotient, the extension ideal $IR$ has exactly the
coordinatewise ideal $I$ by the genuine original-scalar extension
comparison. The preceding lemma gives exactly the coordinate weights
of the whole maximal power. Their sum is coordinatewise $I+m^{s-|\alpha|}$:
one inclusion follows by addition; for the converse choose for each of
the finitely many coordinates a decomposition into these two ideals
and assemble its two families. The quotient of the finite coefficient
module by these actual coordinate ideals is its finite product of the
actual coefficient quotients. Every map uses the original monomial
combination and the actual quotient projections, proving the assertion
on every original numerator class and the original complex scalars.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The EXISTING intrinsic germ HS of the true smooth product is the actual all-order convolution]
\label{hir:actual-product-original-ambient-hs-convolution}
\uses{hir:actual-product-whole-ambient-jet-weighted-coefficient-quotient}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductMixedHS.actualProductHilbertSamuel_convolution}
\leanok
For EVERY original order $\ell$,
\[
 H_{IR}(\ell)=\sum_{\alpha\in A_{\ell+1}} H_I(\ell-|\alpha|).
\]
Here both sides are the existing actual whole local-ring HS functions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the preceding genuine complex-linear quotient equivalence with
$s=\ell+1$. Every index has degree at most $\ell$, hence
$s-|\alpha|=(\ell-|\alpha|)+1$. The corresponding coefficient quotient
therefore has exactly the EXISTING original value
$H_I(\ell-|\alpha|)$. All such original maximal-power quotients are
finite-dimensional. The finite product dimension formula sums these
actual dimensions, proving the literal asserted equality at EVERY order.
No product HS formula or desired quotient comparison is an assumption.
\end{proof}
