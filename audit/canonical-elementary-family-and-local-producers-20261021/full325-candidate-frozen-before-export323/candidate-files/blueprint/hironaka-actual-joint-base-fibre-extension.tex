\section{The actual zero-base fibre and original ambient germs}

Put $B=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^d,0}$, $S=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{d+n},0}$,
$R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,0}$. Write the joint coordinates as $(c,z)$,
let $M=S\mathfrak m_B$, let $N=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, and let
$\sigma:S\to R$ be actual pullback by $z\mapsto(0,z)$.
All ideals and maps concern whole holomorphic germs.

\begin{theorem}[The entire zero-base kernel]
\label{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-kernel}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseZeroTrace,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseZeroTrace_surjective,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.baseMaximalIdeal_eq_ker_jointBaseZeroTrace}
\leanok
\uses{hir:relative-normal-entire-plane-kernel}
The genuine zero-base trace $\sigma$ is surjective and has entire kernel $M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pullback by the actual last-coordinate projection gives a right inverse:
the last-coordinate projection of $(0,z)$ equals $z$ on the entire model,
so the corresponding composition is the identity on every holomorphic germ.
The maximal ideal of $B$ is generated by all actual coordinate germs,
by the existing zero-dimensional-base Hadamard theorem. Thus $M$ is the
entire ideal generated by the first $d$ coordinate germs of $S$.
Permute the two coordinate blocks from $(c,z)$ to $(z,c)$. The literal
coordinate pullback is an algebra isomorphism with its actual inverse;
it sends this whole ideal to the last-$d$ normal-coordinate ideal.
Under the same permutation $\sigma$ becomes the existing plane trace
retaining the first $n$ coordinates. The whole Hadamard kernel theorem
identifies its kernel with precisely the last-$d$ ideal. Pulling back
through the actual coordinate isomorphism proves the claimed entire
kernel equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole original joint ideals and powers]
\label{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-original-ideals}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseZeroTrace_jointPullback,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.map_jointIdeal_eq_original,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.map_jointNormalIdeal_eq_maximalIdeal,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.map_jointFibreTruncation_eq_original}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-kernel}
For $n=d+t$, let $L(c,z)=z+T(c)$ be the original actual joint map.
For every original ideal $I\subset R$, let $J=L^*I$. Then
$\sigma\circ L^*=\mathrm{id}_R$, $\sigma_*J=I$, and
$\sigma_*N=\mathfrak m_R$. For every $s\geq0$,
\[
 \sigma_*\bigl(J+M+N^s\bigr)=I+\mathfrak m_R^s.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual pointwise identity $L(0,z)=z$ gives the whole germ retraction.
Ideal extension composes, so the image of $L^*I$ is exactly $I$, for
every whole ideal $I$. Each normal coordinate maps to the same original
coordinate germ. Their span is the entire original maximal ideal by
Hadamard decomposition, proving $\sigma_*N=\mathfrak m_R$.
The preceding kernel theorem makes the whole image of $M$ zero.
Ideal extension preserves sums and products, hence also every power,
which proves the displayed equality, including $s=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Original-numerator fibre truncation dictionary]
\label{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-truncation-dictionary}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.baseFibreTruncationAlgEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseFibreTruncationAlgEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.baseFibreTruncationAlgEquiv_mk,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseFibreTruncationAlgEquiv_mk}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-kernel,hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-original-ideals}
For every whole joint ideal $K\subset S$ and every $s\geq0$, the
actual zero-base trace gives a complex-algebra equivalence
\[
 S/(K+M+N^s)\simeq R/(\sigma_*K+\mathfrak m_R^s),\qquad
 [g]\longmapsto[\sigma g].
\]
In particular, for every original ideal $I$ and $K=L^*I$ one obtains
\[
 S/(L^*I+M+N^s)\simeq R/(I+\mathfrak m_R^s),\qquad
 [g]\longmapsto[\sigma g].
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $D=K+M+N^s$. Surjectivity of $\sigma$ implies
$\sigma^{-1}(\sigma_*D)=D+\ker\sigma$. Here $M=\ker\sigma$ is
contained in $D$, and the preceding theorem identifies $\sigma_*D$
with $\sigma_*K+\mathfrak m_R^s$. Thus the composite of $\sigma$ with the
quotient map by $\sigma_*K+\mathfrak m_R^s$ has precisely kernel $D$.
It is surjective, since both factors are surjective. The first
isomorphism theorem therefore gives the stated algebra equivalence. Taking $K=L^*I$ gives the
original ideal $I$ by the preceding whole-ideal retraction.
Its quotient formula is the defining induced map on every original
numerator, not a comparison based only on dimensions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The entire layer and literal symbol dictionary]
\label{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-layer-symbols}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseFibreLayerMap,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseFibreLayerEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualJointBaseFibre.jointBaseFibreLayerMap_symbol}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-joint-base-fibre-truncation-dictionary,hir:actual-principal-parts-residue-symbol-square}
For every whole joint ideal $K$, the preceding equivalence restricts,
for every $q$, to a complex-linear
isomorphism between the entire image of $N^q$ in
$S/(K+M+N^{q+1})$ and the entire image of $\mathfrak m_R^q$ in
$R/(\sigma_*K+\mathfrak m_R^{q+1})$. It sends $[g]$ to $[\sigma g]$.
For every actual $v\in N$, this dictionary commutes with multiplication
by $v$ on the joint layer and multiplication by $\sigma v$ on the
original ambient layer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The whole image of $N^q$ under $\sigma$ is precisely
$\mathfrak m_R^q$, since ideal extension preserves powers and
$\sigma_*N=\mathfrak m_R$. Thus the quotient equivalence maps
one stated layer into the other. Conversely, every representative
$h\in\mathfrak m_R^q$ has a preimage $g\in N^q$ under the
surjective whole map $\sigma$, which supplies a preimage of its
layer class. Injectivity follows by restriction of the established
whole quotient isomorphism. These restrictions preserve complex
scalars. For a representative $g\in N^q$, both composites in
the symbol square have value $[\sigma(vg)]=[(\sigma v)(\sigma g)]$.
Every layer class has such a representative, proving the square
on the entire original layers.
\end{proof}

The target is now the actual original ambient germ quotient. Successive tangent
cuts leading to the original centre-normal filtration remain separate
steps before normal flatness or the general higher Hilbert--Samuel
transformation theorem can be asserted.
