\section{Literal complex structure-sheaf stalks and whole original relative modules}

Use the actual sheaf of complex-smooth functions with complex scalar
models: its sections are holomorphic functions, by complex differentiability
and holomorphic smoothness. Its stalk is the genuine neighbourhood colimit,
not a chosen chart ring by definition. The original germ-ideal membership
record supplies ALL original membership functions. The whole ideal in the
literal stalk is the span of their actual section germs. A bundled
normal-cone sheaf and its stalk identification remain separate from these
literal local-ring and relative-module dictionaries.

For every actual open $V$ of an original complex manifold, every actual

its total extension by zero represents a genuine original holomorphic germ
at $y$. Reading this representative in the actual preferred chart at $y$
gives a homomorphism from the section ring to the whole holomorphic germ
ring at zero. These homomorphisms are compatible with every actual open
restriction containing $y$. Conversely EVERY original function holomorphic
near $y$ is represented by a genuine complex-smooth section on a real open
neighbourhood of $y$, and all such section representations have the SAME
literal sheaf stalk germ.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the actual open $V$, the total extension agrees with $s$. Smoothness of
the section over COMPLEX scalar models gives complex manifold
differentiability at every point of $V$; the inclusion of this open has
its genuine local inverse, so the extension is holomorphic at every
included point. Only its germ at $y$ is used; no global holomorphic
extension is asserted. The genuine chart inverse, recentered at $e_y(y)$,
gives its whole original chart germ. Original equality of functions on

that neighbourhood, so the germ operation is a ring homomorphism. Under
restriction to a smaller actual open, both extensions agree at every point
of that smaller open; original eventual equality proves equality of their
whole germs. Conversely an original holomorphic function is complex-smooth
near every point of some real open neighbourhood, by holomorphic smoothness.
Choose that open and restrict the actual function, obtaining a genuine
section. For two such choices, intersect the opens; both restrictions are
literally the SAME original function there. The actual colimit restriction
identity makes their sheaf stalk germs equal. The same intersection
argument proves that eventual equality of original representatives gives
the same sheaf stalk germ, independently of every choice.
\end{proof}

EVERY actual section germ and on EVERY original holomorphic-function germ
is precisely the original translated chart germ. No stalk equivalence,
representative-surjectivity or inverse-chart certificate is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compatible section homomorphisms above form a cocone on the actual
neighbourhood restriction diagram. The genuine commutative-ring colimit
universal property gives the actual stalk homomorphism, with its stated
formula on EVERY section. Each stalk element is the germ of an actual
section. Suppose two stalk elements have equal images; choose real section
representatives on their actual opens. Equality of whole chart germs says
that their actual translated functions agree on a neighbourhood of zero.
The true original chart map sends a real neighbourhood of $y$ into that
neighbourhood, and its local inverse identities recover the SAME section
values there. Intersect this original neighbourhood with both actual section
opens and the true chart source. Their restrictions are equal on an actual
open containing $y$. The colimit germ restriction identity therefore makes

For surjectivity take an arbitrary whole germ represented by $F$ holomorphic

Its stalk image is the original $F$ germ, because the recentered actual
chart inverse composed with the chart map is the identity near zero in
its true target. Thus every whole germ has a genuine section preimage.
Bijectivity upgrades this actual homomorphism to the required ring
equivalence; all displayed representative formulas come from its original

\end{proof}
