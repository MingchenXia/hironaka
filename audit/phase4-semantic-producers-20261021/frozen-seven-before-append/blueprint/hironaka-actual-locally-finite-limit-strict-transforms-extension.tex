\section{Actual centre-relative strict transforms on a locally finite composite}

The locally finite composite in \cite{bierstone-milman97-invent-math},
Theorem 13.4 and Lemma 13.7 (printed pages 298--301), must carry the actual
strict transforms of the analytic chain. Throughout this chapter a strict
transform means the iterated centre-relative closure
$\overline{\pi^{-1}(A\setminus C)}$. No identification with an
exceptional-locus strict transform is used. The actual history and local
finiteness are genuine data for the general geometric construction; the
last theorem constructs its history internally from original data in the
stated discrete-upper-cut case. No unrestricted scheduler is asserted.

\begin{theorem}[An actual stable endpoint is biholomorphic to its whole limit window]
\label{hir:actual-lf-limit-stable-window-biholomorphism}
\uses{hir:actual-locally-finite-history-finite-stage-factors,hir:actual-tower-tail-holomorphic-lift-uniqueness,hir:actual-locally-finite-history-limit-complex-manifold}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.stableFactor_local,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.stableWindowDiffeomorph,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.isOpenHoloEmbedding_localInclusion}
\leanok
Let $s$ be an actual growing blowup history and suppose its actual centre
images are locally finite. Write $p:L\to X$ for its constructed complex
limit projection. For each chosen stable base window $U_x$ and its actual
index $N_x$, the actual endpoint inclusion induces a biholomorphism
\[
 (s_{N_x})^{-1}U_x\ \simeq\ p^{-1}U_x.
\]
Its inverse is the actual finite-stage factor to stage $N_x$, restricted
to this open. Both maps commute with their whole original projections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual factor to stage $N_x$, composed with the actual endpoint
inclusion, has the same projection as that endpoint. Its values lie in
the endpoint open by this projection equation. Regard it as a holomorphic
self-map of the endpoint over its restricted finite prefix. Apply genuine
finite-tower lift uniqueness to that prefix and its empty continuation:
the self-map is the identity. Thus the factor followed by the endpoint
inclusion is the identity on the endpoint. The entire range of the
endpoint inclusion is exactly $p^{-1}U_x$. Every point of this latter open
therefore has an endpoint preimage, and the identity just proved gives
the other inverse identity. The inclusion is holomorphic by the genuine
lifted-chart construction; the inverse is holomorphic by the proved
holomorphicity of the actual finite-stage factor. Restricting these maps
to their true opens gives the claimed biholomorphism and whole projection
equations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual infinite strict transform equals one finite prefix locally]
\label{hir:actual-lf-limit-centre-relative-strict-dictionary}
\uses{hir:actual-lf-limit-stable-window-biholomorphism,hir:actual-locally-finite-history-finite-stage-factor-compatibility}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.limitStrictTransform,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.localInclusion_preimage_limitStrictTransform,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.isClosed_limitStrictTransform,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.limitStrictTransform_mono}
\leanok
For a closed original subset $B\subset X$, define an actual subset of $L$ by
\[
 B_\infty=\bigcap_{j\geq0}p_j^{-1}(B_j),
\]
where $p_j:L\to s_j$ is the constructed actual stage factor and $B_j$ is
the iterated centre-relative strict transform along the actual prefix.
It is closed, is monotone in $B$, and its preimage under every actual
stable endpoint inclusion is exactly $B_{N_x}$ on that endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each finite strict transform is closed: an empty prefix retains the given
closed set, and a nonempty prefix ends in a closure. Each $p_j$ is
continuous, so the displayed intersection is closed. Monotonicity follows
at every finite stage from monotonicity of inverse image, set difference
with the same centre, and closure; intersecting preserves inclusion.

Membership in the intersection implies membership at $j=N_x$. The preceding
theorem identifies the stage-$N_x$ factor of an endpoint point with that
same endpoint point, proving one inclusion in the local equality.

Conversely take an endpoint point in $B_{N_x}$ and any stage $k$. Use the
actual factor at $J=N_x+k$. Its projection by the recorded tail from
$N_x$ to $J$ is the original endpoint point, by the actual factor
compatibility and the preceding theorem. On the inverse image of $U_x$
that tail has only empty centres, by actual stable-window avoidance.
The centre-relative strict transform of a closed set along an empty-centre
tower is its ordinary inverse image: at each step the inverse image is
closed, so the final closure changes nothing. Consequently the point at
stage $J$ belongs to $B_J$. The actual tail from $k$ to $J=k+N_x$ maps
$B_J$ into $B_k$, since the strict transform of a closed set lies in its
inverse image at each step. Actual factor compatibility identifies this
image with the stage-$k$ factor of the chosen limit point. It belongs to
$B_k$. Since $k$ was arbitrary, the limit point lies in the entire
intersection, proving the reverse inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Analytic chains have actual analytic transforms on the same limit]
\label{hir:actual-lf-limit-analytic-centre-relative-strict-transforms}
\uses{hir:actual-lf-limit-centre-relative-strict-dictionary,hir:actual-lf-limit-stable-window-biholomorphism}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.isComplexAnalyticSubset_strictAlong,Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.isComplexAnalyticSubset_limitStrictTransform}
\leanok
For every original analytic subset $B\subset X$, the actual subset
$B_\infty$ is analytic in the constructed complex limit. Thus every nested
chain of original analytic subsets yields a nested chain of actual analytic
subsets on that same limit, with the exact preceding finite-prefix dictionary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First prove analyticity of each finite centre-relative strict transform by
induction on the actual prefix. The empty prefix gives $B$. At the next
blowup the centre is a closed complex submanifold and hence analytic.
Both its holomorphic inverse image and the inverse image of the previous
analytic transform are analytic. The closure of their difference is
analytic by the proved closure-difference theorem. This is precisely the
centre-relative strict transform at that step.

At any point of the limit choose a genuine stable endpoint containing it.
The exact local dictionary identifies the pullback of $B_\infty$ with the
analytic finite-prefix transform restricted to that endpoint. The genuine
endpoint biholomorphism transports this analytic set to the actual limit
window. These windows cover the limit, so locality of analytic presentations
proves that $B_\infty$ is analytic. For a chain, apply the same construction
to each original member. The monotonicity proved above gives every required
inclusion; the histories and stage factors are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Original discrete-upper-cut data internally give analytic limit transforms]
\label{hir:original-discrete-cut-actual-analytic-limit-transforms}
\uses{hir:actual-lf-limit-analytic-centre-relative-strict-transforms,hir:actual-locally-finite-history-limit-proper,hir:original-canonical-discrete-full-cut-cleanup}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualLocallyFiniteLimitStrictTransforms.exists_original_discrete_cut_limit_transforms}
\leanok
Let $M$ be actual original invariant-domain data and let its literal initial
strict upper full-invariant cut at a threshold be discrete. There is an
internally constructed original-admissible locally finite growing history,
with every actual centre image contained in that cut, whose constructed
proper holomorphic limit carries the analytic centre-relative transform
of every original analytic subset and the exact stable-prefix dictionary.
The whole original cosupport is allowed to be noncompact and
positive-dimensional. No history or limit-space certificate is an input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the original discrete-upper-cut cleanup producer to the given original
data and threshold. It constructs one actual growing history, proves original
geometric testness at every prefix, proves that its actual centre images
lie in the initial strict cut, and proves their local finiteness. Apply the
genuine limit construction to this same history and this proved local
finiteness. Its projection is holomorphic and proper. Apply the preceding
analytic-transform theorem to each original analytic subset, and use the
proved stable-endpoint dictionary for this same history. These constructions
use the chosen original history throughout, so the required statements hold
simultaneously for every original analytic subset. Nothing in this argument
asserts existence of such a history for a nondiscrete initial cut.
\end{proof}
