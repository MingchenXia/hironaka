\section{Actual translated normal families with analytic tangent coefficients}

\begin{theorem}[A genuine common coefficient domain for a finite normal polynomial family]
\label{hir:actual-finite-normal-polynomial-coefficient-domain}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-entire-germ-retraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualTranslatedNormalFamilyHS.exists_actual_polynomial_coefficient_domain}
\leanok
Let $P_i$, for $i$ in a finite set, be genuine finite normal polynomials
with coefficients in the original holomorphic germ ring on $\mathbb C^d$.
There are actual representative functions $a_{i\gamma}$ of all their
coefficient germs and a single genuine open neighborhood $U$ of zero
on which every coefficient occurring in any $P_i$ is holomorphic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an actual holomorphic representative of each original coefficient
germ. Each representative is complex differentiable at all points in
some neighborhood of zero. For fixed $i$, the support of the actual
polynomial $P_i$ is finite. The pairs $(i,\gamma)$ with $\gamma$ in that
support therefore form a finite set. Intersect their neighborhoods and
choose an open neighborhood $U$ inside the intersection. Each supported
representative is differentiable throughout $U$, and its germ is still
the original entire coefficient germ. Unsupported coefficient
representatives impose no domain requirement. This also covers an empty
family and zero polynomials.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The genuine jointly holomorphic normal translation family and its entire original fibres]
\label{hir:actual-translated-normal-joint-family}
\uses{hir:actual-finite-normal-polynomial-coefficient-domain,hir:actual-normal-scaling-translated-polynomial}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualTranslatedNormalFamilyHS.exists_actual_joint_normal_ray_family}
\leanok
For any such finite family and any true normal vector $p$, there are an
actual open set $D\subset\mathbb C\times\mathbb C^{d+t}$ containing the
whole parameter axis and actual functions $G_i$ jointly holomorphic on
$D$, whose entire original fibre ideal at $c$ is precisely $J_{cp}$,
the ideal spanned by the original evaluations $P_i(u,x+cp)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the common representative domain $U$ from the preceding theorem.
Let $D$ be the inverse image of $U$ under the actual continuous linear
map sending $(c,z)$ to the tangent projection of $z$. This is open,
and contains every $(c,0)$ because $0\in U$. Define the actual function
\[
 G_i(c,z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}(P_i)}
 a_{i\gamma}(u(z))\prod_{j=1}^{t}(x_j(z)+cp_j)^{\gamma_j}.
\]
Each supported coefficient is holomorphic after composition with the
true tangent projection on $D$. Every translated normal coordinate is
an actual polynomial function of $(c,z)$, hence holomorphic. Finite
sums and products prove joint holomorphicity on this very domain.
For fixed $c$, expand the genuine finite polynomial evaluation as its
sum of monomials. The original germ sum, product, and pullback identities
identify the germ of each displayed summand with the actual tangent
pullback of its entire original coefficient germ times the same
translated normal monomial. Thus the germ of $G_i(c,\cdot)$ equals
exactly the original evaluation of $P_i$ translated by $cp$.
The actual fibre ideal is the span of these very germs. Replacing each
generator by that exact equality proves both entire ideal directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every translated homogeneous normal ideal has HS bounded by its ray origin]
\label{hir:actual-translated-normal-family-origin-hs-bound}
\uses{hir:actual-translated-normal-joint-family,hir:actual-translated-normal-family-nonzero-ray-hs,hir:actual-holomorphic-family-fibre-hs-closed-superlevels}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualTranslatedNormalFamilyHS.hilbertSamuelValue_actual_translated_normal_le}
\leanok
Suppose additionally that every $P_i$ is homogeneous in the normal
variables, with an arbitrary genuine degree depending on $i$.
For every actual normal vector $p$ and every order $\ell$, the unchanged
original Hilbert--Samuel value of $J_p$ is at most that of $J_0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the preceding actual jointly holomorphic family and its exact
entire fibre dictionary. For fixed $\ell$, the original fibre HS
superlevel theorem makes the set of parameters $c$ with
$H_{J_p}(\ell)\le H_{J_{cp}}(\ell)$ closed. Indeed the genuine whole
parameter axis lies in the actual domain, so the closed superlevel
theorem on the actual parameter open applies, and pullback along the
continuous inclusion of the complex parameter gives this closed set.
For every $c\ne0$, genuine partial-normal scaling maps the whole
$J_{cp}$ onto the whole $J_p$, so the preceding nonzero-ray equality
puts $c$ in this set. The complement of zero is dense in $\mathbb C$.
Its closure contains zero, and closedness gives the asserted inequality
at $c=0$. This works for every original HS order separately and hence
for all of them, without a supplied semicontinuity or numerical
certificate.
\end{proof}

The translated expressions are evaluations only of genuine finite normal
polynomials. Their coefficients remain entire original analytic tangent
germs. No formal power series is evaluated at a nonzero point. This
comparison concerns these original ideals; compatibility of a smooth
centre with the original vertex data remains a separate requirement in
the general positive-dimensional Bennett argument.
