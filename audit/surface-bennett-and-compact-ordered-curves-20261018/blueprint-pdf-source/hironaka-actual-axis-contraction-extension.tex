\section{The genuine original one-coordinate contraction}

\begin{definition}[Actual analytic contraction and its genuine nonzero-parameter inverse]
\label{hir:actual-axis-contraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContraction,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGerm,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGermEquiv}
\leanok
For the actual coordinate $e$ and $c\in\mathbb C$, let
$L_c(z)=z+(c-1)z_e\mathbf e_e$. This is a genuine continuous complex-linear
map, fixing the original origin. Let $L_c^*$ be its actual pullback on the
whole original analytic germ ring. For $c\ne0$, use the actual inverse
$L_{c^{-1}}$ to construct the original complex germ algebra equivalence.
At zero the true map sets exactly the selected coordinate to zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Literal coordinate values, original composition and identity]
\label{hir:actual-axis-contraction-whole-coordinate-dictionary}
\uses{hir:actual-axis-contraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContraction_apply,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContraction_comp,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGerm_comp,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGerm_one}
\leanok
The true coordinate values are $L_c(z)_e=cz_e$ and $L_c(z)_i=z_i$ for
$i\ne e$. The actual maps satisfy $L_cL_t=L_{ct}$, and their whole
pullbacks satisfy $L_c^*L_t^*=L_{tc}^*$ and $L_1^*=\mathrm{id}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Evaluate the genuine continuous-linear sum defining $L_c$ in each original
coordinate. The standard vector is one at $e$ and zero elsewhere, giving
the displayed values. Evaluate the composition in those same coordinates:
the selected coordinate is multiplied by $ct$ and all others retained.
Equality in every coordinate proves equality of the actual maps. Apply
these true function identities to each original holomorphic representative;
the actual whole germ pullback is precisely composition with those maps.
Every genuine whole germ has such a representative, proving the entire
pullback composition and identity claims. In particular $c^{-1}$ gives
both actual inverse identities when $c\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual polynomial dehomogenization is fixed by every original axis contraction]
\label{hir:actual-axis-contraction-fixes-dehomogenized-polynomial}
\uses{hir:actual-axis-contraction-whole-coordinate-dictionary,hir:actual-point-polynomial-dehomogenization}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGerm_coordinate,Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionGerm_dehomogenization}
\leanok
The whole analytic coordinate germ at $e$ maps to $c$ times itself and all
other coordinate germs are retained. For every actual complex polynomial
$P$, the original analytic evaluation of $\delta_{e,b}P$ is fixed by
every $L_c^*$, including the true coordinate-zero contraction $c=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The literal whole pullback of each coordinate function has the preceding
actual coordinate value, which gives the stated entire germ identities.
Both polynomial algebra maps in the second assertion preserve constants,
addition and multiplication. On variable $e$, actual dehomogenization is
one and is fixed; on every other variable it is the constant $b_i$ plus
that coordinate germ, both fixed. Polynomial algebra extensionality gives
the equality for every genuine finite polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The same original contraction is genuinely jointly holomorphic]
\label{hir:actual-axis-contraction-joint-holomorphic}
\uses{hir:actual-axis-contraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAxisContraction.actualAxisContractionJoint_contDiff}
\leanok
The actual map $(t,z)\mapsto L_{t_0}(z)$ on the true parameter/model product
is complex smooth, hence jointly holomorphic, and fixes every parameter-axis
point $(t,0)$ at the origin in the target.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Its literal formula is the model projection $z$ plus
$(t_0-1)z_e\mathbf e_e$. The parameter coordinate and original model
coordinate are genuine continuous complex-linear projections. Their
product and scalar multiplication by the fixed original standard vector
are complex smooth. Thus their sum is complex smooth on the actual whole
product. At $z=0$ both terms vanish, proving the stated axis identity.
\end{proof}

certificate. Their all-order Hilbert--Samuel comparison is the next step.
