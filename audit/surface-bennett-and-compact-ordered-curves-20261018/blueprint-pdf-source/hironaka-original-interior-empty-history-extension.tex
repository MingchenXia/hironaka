\section{An actual interior empty step with the original history retained}
\label{chap:hironaka-original-interior-empty-history}

The source-facing goal is the empty-step invariance used in W\l odarczyk's
resolution invariant: original boundary members remain original, whereas a new
exceptional member belongs to the later history.  This chapter supplies an
unrebased geometric prerequisite.  Throughout, the same initial marked ideal
and the same initial space are retained.  No value from the transformed initial
marked ideal is substituted for a value from the original history.

The step treated here is the genuine blowup of the empty centre with identity
projection.  It is sufficient to isolate deletion of a redundant interior stage
without a change of manifold.  Arbitrary empty-centre biholomorphisms and the
complete recursive invariant comparison still require further proofs.

\begin{definition}[The original history with an interior identity empty step]
\label{hir:original-interior-empty-history}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.interiorEmptyHistory,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.proj_interiorEmptyHistory,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.length_interiorEmptyHistory}
\leanok
For actual blowup towers $H:X\leftarrow A$ and $S:A\leftarrow Y$, let
\[
 H\epsilon S=(H\,\xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}_A}\,A)S,
 \qquad C_\epsilon=\varnothing.
\]
The identity is an actual blowup along the empty centre: the empty vanishing
ideal is the unit ideal, and the blowup universal property has the identity as
its unique lift.  The initial space, final space and all stages of $H$ and $S$
are retained.  The composite projection of $H\epsilon S$ is literally that of
$HS$, and its length is $|H|+1+|S|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The actual geometric dictionary without rebasing]
\label{hir:original-interior-empty-geometric-dictionary}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.geometric_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.whole_record_of_nonempty_tail}
\leanok
\uses{hir:original-interior-empty-history,bcalc:monomial-invariant-empty-member}
Let $J=(N,E,I,d)$ be well formed and let $H$ be an actual test tower of $J$.
For any actual tower $S$ starting at the final stage of $H$, put
$j=|E|+|H|$, $J^+=(J)_{H\epsilon S}$ and $J^-=(J)_{HS}$.
Then
\[
 N^+=N^-,\quad d^+=d^-,\quad E^+=(E^-).\operatorname{insert}_j(\varnothing),
 \quad\operatorname{cosupp}J^+=\operatorname{cosupp}J^-.
\]
Their codimension-one cosupport parts along $N^+=N^-$ also agree.
If $S$ has at least one blowup, the entire literal record satisfies
$J^+=\operatorname{insertEmpty}_j(J^-)$.
No admissibility hypothesis on the tail $S$ is required for these comparisons.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $K=J_H$, and let $B$ be the transform of $K$ by the identity empty
blowup with its appended empty boundary member removed.  Closedness of $K.N$
and of every member of $K.E$ implies that their strict transforms by this
identity map equal themselves.  The empty-step ideal comparison on $K.N$,
together with the identity pullback of its coherent ideal, gives restricted
equivalence $B\sim_N K$.  Both ideals are coherent: $K$ is a stage of a true
test tower, and the empty centre is admissible for $K$.

The actual transform definition, including its ordered boundary list, gives
\[
J^+=\operatorname{insertEmpty}_j(B_S).
\]
Restricted equivalence preserves cosupports along every tail and, after the
first blowup, the entire transformed records are equal.  The carrier and
boundary transforms along any tower depend only on their initial carrier and
boundary, so $(B_S).N=(K_S).N$ and $(B_S).E=(K_S).E$, also for an empty tail.
The mark is retained by every transform.  Inserting an empty boundary member
changes neither the carrier, ideal, mark nor cosupport.  Finally the
codimension-one part depends only on the carrier and cosupport, which have
just been proved equal.  If $S$ is nonempty, apply equality of transforms of
restricted-equivalent coherent marked ideals after its first blowup; this
proves the claimed literal record equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The original Step One members and all special branches]
\label{hir:original-interior-empty-step-one-dictionary}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.original_members_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.level_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.isSpecial_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.stepRegime_dictionary}
\leanok
\uses{hir:original-interior-empty-geometric-dictionary,hir:step-one-value-masked}
Let the original marked ideal $M$ be well formed, and let $H$ be an actual test
tower of the original coefficient-homogenized marked ideal $\overline M$.
For every actual tail $S$, the ordered list of original members, its indexed
membership count at every point, both branches of the Step One special locus,
and each fixed Step One regime coincide for $H\epsilon S$ and $HS$.
This includes the zero-member codimension-one branch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient-homogenized marked ideal is well formed and has the same
initial boundary list as $M$.  Apply the geometric dictionary to $\overline M$.
The first $|M.E|$ elements of its transformed boundary list are precisely the
original members.  Since $j=|M.E|+|H|\geq |M.E|$, insertion at $j$ leaves this
whole ordered prefix unchanged.  Consequently its membership count agrees
pointwise.  In the zero-member branch, the dictionary identifies the actual
carrier and codimension-one cosupport part.  In the positive branch it
identifies the actual carrier, ordered original-member list and cosupport;
these are all arguments of the member-intersection locus.  Both special
predicates therefore coincide.  A fixed regime is the conjunction of equality
to its chosen level and failure of the special predicate, so it also coincides.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual contact and member masks of the original history]
\label{hir:original-interior-empty-masks}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.maskA_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.maskB_dictionary,Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.lowerMask_dictionary}
\leanok
\uses{hir:original-interior-empty-history,hir:original-interior-empty-step-one-dictionary}
Under the preceding hypotheses, at each final point $p$ the actual open sets
$\operatorname{maskA}(M,H\epsilon S,p)$ and
$\operatorname{maskB}(M,H\epsilon S,p)$ equal their counterparts for $HS$.
Hence the actual lower mask agrees at every level.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The raw set whose interior defines the member mask uses only the ordered
original members, their levels and the special predicate at each point.
All of these agree by the preceding lemma, so its interior agrees.

For the zero-level contact mask, the initial marked ideal is literally the
same $M$.  Both composite projections equal that of $HS$.  Thus the chosen
tangent-direction pair is evaluated at exactly the same initial point; its
choice is not replaced or identified using a transport certificate.  The
remaining raw-set arguments are absence from each original member and
absence from the actual codimension-one cosupport part.  The geometric and
Step One dictionaries prove both equalities.  The raw sets and their interiors
therefore agree.  The lower mask selects the contact mask at level zero and
the member mask at every positive level, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The exact actual fixed-regime run across the interior insertion]
\label{hir:original-interior-empty-regime-run}
\source{wlodarczyk05-hironaka,bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaOriginalInteriorEmptyHistory.stepRegime_run_dictionary}
\leanok
\uses{hir:original-interior-empty-step-one-dictionary}
Fix any Step One level $s$ under the preceding hypotheses.  At $y\in Y$ put
$r=\operatorname{run}_{M,s}(HS,y)$, $t=|S|$, and
$b=\operatorname{stepRegime}_{M,s}(H,S.\operatorname{proj}(y))$.
Then the actual recursively computed run satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{run}_{M,s}(H\epsilon S,y)=
 \begin{cases}
 r,&r<t,\\
 r+1,&t\le r\text{ and }b,\\
 t,&t\le r\text{ and }\neg b.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is an equality of the literal original fixed-regime run, not a supplied
comparison of split times.  No final regime membership is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the actual tail $S$.  For the empty tail, its length is zero and its
projection is the identity.  The new last step is the identity empty blowup,
so the defining recursion gives $\operatorname{run}(H,y)+1$ precisely when
$H,y$ satisfies the chosen regime, and zero otherwise.  This is the stated
formula, including the third case when a run of $H$ itself is nonzero but its
current point fails the regime.

For a tail ending in $\pi$, the special-branch and level dictionary makes the
predecessor regime test identical in both histories.  If that test fails,
both runs are zero, which is shorter than the now positive tail length.
If it holds, both recursively computed predecessor runs increase by one.
Apply the induction hypothesis at $\pi y$.  The inequality $r<t$ is preserved
when both sides increase by one; in the other cases the old run, or the tail
length respectively, increases by one.  The base-point test $b$ is unchanged
because the tail projection composes with $\pi$.  These three cases give the
formula.
\end{proof}

These results give geometric producers and the actual fixed-regime run for retaining the original history when
an interior empty step is removed.  They do not yet identify the recursive
invariant's lower marked ideals or restricted tails, prove the whole higher value
unchanged, provide local regularity of its maximum locus, or construct global
maximum scheduling on a noncompact space.  The full G3 goal remains open.
The argument is an adaptation of the empty-step part of the marked-ideal
resolution invariant, using the existing literal controlled-transform and
original-member definitions; it does not supply an independent resolution
driver.  Source routes: \texttt{wlodarczyk05-hironaka}, resolution-invariant
empty-step property and original boundary histories; \texttt{bierstone-milman08-functoriality},
marked-ideal equivalence and blowup histories.
