\begin{lemma}[Locally finite analytic unions and their actual dimensions]
\label{hir:curve-predicates-locally-finite-union}
\uses{rem:global-components,hir:dimtwo-finite-dimzero-union,ana:analytic-preimage}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.isComplexAnalyticSubset_iUnion_of_locallyFinite,Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.analytic_dimzero_iUnion_of_locallyFinite}
\leanok
A genuinely locally finite family of closed analytic subsets has an
analytic union. If each member has dimension at most zero, so does its union.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The locally finite union is closed. At any point choose a genuine open
neighbourhood meeting only finitely many indexed members. On this same
neighbourhood the full union agrees exactly with that finite union, whose
analytic equations come from actual finite analytic union. Transfer its
analytic presentation using this local equality; outside the closed union
use the unit equation. In the zero-dimensional case the finite union has
actual dimension zero by the proved finite-union producer. Every regular
point of the full union transfers to that finite union by the same open
local equality, so its regular dimension is zero. This permits infinite,
empty and repeated families whenever their actual indexed family is locally finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A proper analytic intersection on a genuine curve component]
\label{hir:curve-predicates-proper-intersection}
\uses{rem:global-components,rem:thin-dimension-drop,hir:global-component-pure-geometric-dimension}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.analytic_dimzero_inter_globalComponent_of_not_subset}
\leanok
Let $A$ be analytic of dimension at most one, $B$ one of its genuine global
components, and $P$ analytic. If $B\not\subseteq P$, then $B\cap P$ is
closed analytic and has dimension at most zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual $B$ is an analytic closure of a connected component $C$ of the
regular locus of $A$, is contained in $A$, and inherits its dimension bound
by analytic dimension monotonicity. If a nonempty relatively open trace
of $B$ were contained in $P$, choose a genuine point of $C$ in that open
set using its density in $B$. The corresponding nonempty relatively open
trace of $C$ is contained in $P$. Analytic continuation on $C$ makes all
of $C$, and then its closure $B$, contained in the closed $P$, a contradiction.
Thus the actual intersection is thin in $B$. Genuine thin analytic
dimension drop gives dimension at most zero; intersection of the actual
analytic sets provides analyticity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Internally produced exception for finite analytic predicates]
\label{hir:curve-predicates-finite-exception}
\uses{hir:curve-predicates-locally-finite-union,hir:curve-predicates-proper-intersection}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.predicateException,Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.exists_analytic_dimzero_predicate_exception}
\leanok
For an actual analytic $A$ of dimension at most one and a finite indexed
family of actual analytic predicates $P_i$, there is a constructed closed
analytic zero-dimensional $Z\subseteq A$ such that, for every actual global
component $B$ and every $x\in B\setminus Z$,
\[
x\in P_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\subseteq P_i
\]
for all indices $i$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $i$, take the union of $B\cap P_i$ over precisely the genuine
components $B$ not contained in $P_i$. This actual subfamily is locally
finite: inject its component subtype into the genuinely locally finite
full global-component family, then take actual subsets. Every intersection
is analytic and zero-dimensional by the preceding lemma. The actual union
producer therefore makes it analytic and zero-dimensional. Take the finite
union over $i$, retaining its actual inclusion in $A$. At $x\in B\setminus Z$,
if $B\subseteq P_i$, membership is immediate. If $B\not\subseteq P_i$, membership
in $P_i$ would place $x$ in the corresponding actual intersection, hence in
$Z$, contradicting the hypothesis. This proves the literal predicate dictionary.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual zero-dimensional singular envelope of an analytic curve]
\label{hir:curve-predicates-singular-envelope}
\uses{hir:dimension-part-global-component-union,rem:pure-singular-locus,hir:dimtwo-finite-dimzero-union}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.eq_dimensionPart_zero_union_one,Hironaka.HironakaCurveAnalyticPredicates.exists_analytic_dimzero_regular_curve_envelope}
\leanok
For an actual analytic $A$ of dimension at most one, an internally
constructed analytic zero-dimensional $S\subseteq A$ contains all its
zero-dimensional components and all its nonregular points. Every point
of $A\setminus S$ is genuinely regular of dimension one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual global components have internally witnessed regular dimensions
zero or one. Their exact geometric dictionary with the dimension parts
therefore gives $A=A_0\cup A_1$. Both parts are analytic and genuinely pure
of their selected dimensions. Take $S=A_0\cup(A_1\setminus\operatorname{Reg}_1 A_1)$.
The first part has dimension zero by purity; the second is analytic and
zero-dimensional by the actual pure singular-locus producer. The finite
union remains analytic and zero-dimensional. Away from its closed $A_0$
part, $A$ has the same germ as $A_1$. Outside the second part the point is
actually regular of dimension one in $A_1$, hence also in $A$. Both parts
are subsets of $A$, so this is an actual exception producer.
\end{proof}

preceding internally constructed finite-predicate exception to this actual
family and this actual cosupport. Its literal membership dictionary gives
the claimed comparisons and retains every actual history stage and map.
\end{proof}
