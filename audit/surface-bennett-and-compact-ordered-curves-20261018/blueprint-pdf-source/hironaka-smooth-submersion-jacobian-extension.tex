\section{Actual Jacobian pivots of a holomorphic submersion}

The input is a genuine holomorphic submersion of possibly different dimensions,
with original atlas charts through the two original points. The local section,
the derivative right inverse and the nonzero holomorphic Jacobian pivot are
constructed internally. Derivative-ideal equality and the complete selected
canonical tower under smooth base change are subsequent obligations.

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-chart-derivative-right-inverse}
Let $f:Y^m\to X^n$ be holomorphic and a submersion at $y$, and let $e'$ and $e$
be original atlas charts through $y$ and $f(y)$. For the actual chart map
$F=e\circ f\circ(e')^{-1}$, the complex derivative $DF(e'(y))$ has a complex
continuous linear right inverse.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionJacobian.exists_chartDerivative_rightInverse}
\leanok
\uses{hir:crossdim-chart-map}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the genuine submersion charts to obtain an actual holomorphic local section
$s$ through the original point, with $s(f(y))=y$ and $f\circ s=\mathrm{id}$
near $f(y)$. Put $S=e'\circ s\circ e^{-1}$. The actual inverse-chart continuity,
the original point identities and holomorphicity of $s$ give a neighbourhood
of $e(f(y))$ on which all arguments stay in their true chart domains. There
$F\circ S=\mathrm{id}$ and $S(e(f(y)))=e'(y)$. Both chart maps are complex
differentiable at those points. Differentiate the actual neighbourhood identity
and apply the complex chain rule. The derivative $DS(e(f(y)))$ is the required
right inverse. No equality of Jacobians or chosen inverse is a hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-chart-derivative-surjective}
The derivative of the actual chart map $F$ at $e'(y)$ is surjective.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionJacobian.chartDerivative_surjective}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-chart-derivative-right-inverse}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any vector $v$ in the original target model, apply the internally produced
right inverse to $v$. The differentiated actual section identity shows that
its image under $DF(e'(y))$ is exactly $v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-jacobian-nonzero-minor}
There are actual original source-coordinate columns
$c:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}$ for which the square matrix
$A_{ij}=(DF(e'(y))\epsilon_{c(j)})_i$ has nonzero complex determinant.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionJacobian.exists_nonzero_chartJacobian_minor}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-chart-derivative-surjective}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $L_i$ be the $i$th target coordinate functional composed with the actual
derivative. A linear relation between the $L_i$ vanishes after evaluation at
each preimage of a target coordinate vector. Surjectivity supplies those
preimages, and evaluation gives every coefficient zero. Thus the actual rows
are linearly independent. The finite original-coordinate evaluation map is
injective. The resulting rectangular matrix has row rank $n$, and its columns
span the whole $n$-dimensional coordinate space. Select a genuine finite basis
from those original columns and reindex it by $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The square
matrix of that basis has nonzero determinant. If $n=0$, the chosen empty matrix
has determinant one; no positivity or nonempty column assumption is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{hir:submersion-holomorphic-jacobian-pivot}
Some actual original-coordinate Jacobian minor is a holomorphic scalar germ
at $y$, is nonzero there and stays nonzero on a genuine neighbourhood of $y$.
For any actual finite matrix of scalar holomorphic germs its determinant is
holomorphic, including the empty matrix.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionJacobian.holomorphicAt_matrix_det}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionJacobian.exists_holomorphic_nonzero_chartJacobian_minor}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-jacobian-nonzero-minor,hir:crossdim-chain-holomorphic}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Keep the actual columns supplied by the previous lemma. Every matrix entry is
one of the genuine chart chain coefficients for an original source coordinate
vector, so is holomorphic at $y$. Expand the square determinant as its finite
signed sum of products. Closure of holomorphic germs under products, sums and
complex constants makes this actual determinant holomorphic. Holomorphicity
gives continuity; because its value at the original point is nonzero, an open
neighbourhood of zero's complement pulls back to a genuine neighbourhood of
$y$ where this same internally selected minor stays nonzero. The empty
determinant is the constant one, so the zero-dimensional target is included.
\end{proof}
