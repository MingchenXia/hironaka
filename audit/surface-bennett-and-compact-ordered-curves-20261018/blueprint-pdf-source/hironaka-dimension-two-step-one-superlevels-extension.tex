\section{Whole maximal-order Step 1 thresholds on carriers of dimension at most two}

All dimensions below are genuine complex analytic dimensions. The construction
uses actual SNC intersections and the identity theorem on the genuine global
components, as in Demailly\cite{demailly-cadg}, II.5.3. It treats the full actual
masked Step 1 value, including its actual historical lower entry, rather than a
caller-supplied tuple. This does not yet establish the full general invariant's
dynamic companion/history gluing or the higher-dimensional global sequence.

\begin{lemma}[The genuine remainder of a smooth analytic carrier]
\label{hir:dimtwo-unselected-smooth-components}
\uses{hir:smooth-selected-components}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.remainingComponents,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.isClosedSubmanifold_remainingComponents,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.remainingComponents_eq_diff_selected,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.thin_inter_remainingComponents,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.analytic_dimzero_inter_remainingComponents}
\leanok
For a closed smooth analytic set $H$ and an analytic set $A$, let $H^-$ be
the union of the genuine global components of $H$ which are not contained
in $A$. Then $H^-$ is closed and smooth, equals $H$ minus the union of the
components contained in $A$, and $A\cap H^-$ is thin in $H^-$. If $H$
has dimension at most one, $A\cap H^-$ is analytic and has dimension zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The regular locus of $H$ is $H$, so its regular connected components are
its actual connected components. Each is closed, and at every one of its
points a genuine open neighbourhood meets $H$ only in that component.
The genuine global component family is locally finite. Every subfamily's
union is therefore closed and has, near each of its points, the same germ
as $H$, proving smoothness. Two such components meeting at a point are
equal, by the connected-component equality at a common point. Coverage by
the actual global components then proves the stated difference identity.
If an open $O$ meets $H^-$ and $O\cap H^-\subseteq A$, choose the actual
unselected component $B$ through a point of that intersection. Its nonempty
relatively open trace is contained in $A$. Analytic continuation on its
connected regular component makes the whole component, hence its closure,
contained in the closed analytic $A$, contradicting its unselected property.
Thus every such trace is empty, which is exactly thinness. If $H$ has
dimension at most one, the remaining union has the same bound by regular
submanifold inclusion. Its intersection with $A$ is analytic, and genuine
thin analytic dimension drop makes this intersection zero-dimensional.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Genuine dimension of a transverse boundary trace]
\label{hir:dimtwo-boundary-trace-dimension}
\uses{bcalc:snc-simultaneous,bcalc:member-intersection-along}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.dimLE_inter_get_of_dimLE_two}
\leanok
Let the closed smooth carrier $N$ have pointwise dimension at most two,
and let an actual SNC divisor $E$ be transverse to $N$. Each actual trace
$N\cap E_i$ is closed smooth and has dimension at most one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a point of the trace, genuine simultaneous adapted coordinates flatten
the trace and $N$ to coordinate planes of dimensions $k_C$ and $k_N$ and
make $E_i$ the hyperplane with coordinate $s_i=0$, where $s_i<k_N$.
The true dimensions of the planes are their ambient-truncated dimensions.
Since the trace lies in $E_i$, its free coordinate plane cannot contain
coordinate $s_i$: otherwise perturb the actual coordinate value in that
direction by a small complex scalar. The actual coordinate target is open,
so this perturbation remains in the target. The actual inverse chart then
produces a trace point whose $s_i$ coordinate is nonzero, a contradiction.
Hence $\min(k_C,n)\le s_i<\min(k_N,n)$. Actual uniqueness of the regular
dimension and the genuine dimension bound on $N$ give
$\min(k_N,n)\le2$, proving that the trace dimension is at most one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual isolated maximal-order remainder]
\label{hir:dimtwo-cosupport-codimtwo-remainder}
\uses{bcalc:codim-one-along,hir:analytic-dimzero-all-points-regular}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.isComplexAnalyticSubset_cosupport_diff_codimOnePartAlong,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.dimLE_cosupport_diff_codimOnePartAlong_of_dimLE_two}
\leanok
For a coherent positive-mark ideal of maximal order along a genuine closed
smooth carrier $N$ of pointwise dimension at most two, its actual cosupport
minus its actual codimension-one part is closed analytic and has dimension zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The genuine codimension-one part is closed and relatively open in the
actual cosupport. Its complement in the cosupport is closed; near any of
its points it agrees with the analytic cosupport on the open complement
of that closed codimension-one part, so it is analytic. At a regular point
of this remainder, choose the actual regular dimension of $N$, at most two.
If that dimension is zero, regular submanifold inclusion forces remainder
dimension zero. If it is one or two, the genuine maximal-contact filling
and codimension-one remainder theorem makes the remainder dimension
strictly less than respectively zero or one. The former case is impossible
and the latter forces dimension zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual finite unions in analytic dimension zero]
\label{hir:dimtwo-finite-dimzero-union}
\uses{hir:analytic-dimzero-all-points-regular}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.analytic_dimzero_union,Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.analytic_dimzero_finset_union}
\leanok
A finite indexed union of genuine closed analytic sets of dimension at
most zero is analytic and has dimension at most zero, including an empty union.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual zero-dimensional analytic sets are closed and discrete, and
every point is regular of dimension zero. Their finite union is analytic
by finite analytic union and is closed and discrete. At a point of the
union, choose an open neighbourhood in which the union is that singleton,
and a member containing the point. The member and union have identical
germs there, so its genuine zero-dimensional regularity transfers to the
union. Uniqueness of regular dimension excludes all other regular dimensions.
Induction on the finite index set proves the general assertion; the empty
union has no regular points and its analytic presentation is the constant
unit equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual zero-dimensional envelope for all nonspecial points]
\label{hir:dimtwo-stepone-nonspecial-envelope}
\uses{hir:dimtwo-unselected-smooth-components,hir:dimtwo-boundary-trace-dimension,hir:dimtwo-cosupport-codimtwo-remainder,rem:thin-dimension-drop,hir:member-inter-locus-analytic-components,hir:dimtwo-finite-dimzero-union}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.exists_analytic_dimzero_nonspecial_envelope}
\leanok
For an actual maximal-order well-formed positive-mark Step 1 datum on a
carrier of pointwise dimension at most two, and any actual geometric test
tower of its actual coefficient marked ideal, there is a genuinely closed
analytic zero-dimensional set $Z$ containing every actual nonspecial point
of its transformed cosupport.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Internally produce the actual transformed coherence, SNC divisor,
transversality, maximal order and dimension bound. At level zero, nonspecial
points lie in the actual isolated remainder of the preceding lemma. At
positive level, choose an actual original boundary member through the point
and its actual smooth trace $H_i$ on the transformed carrier. If its genuine
global component is contained in the actual cosupport $A$, a true local
component neighbourhood shows that the intersection of all original members
through the point, on the carrier, has germ contained in $A$. The point
is special, a contradiction. Hence it lies in $A\cap H_i^-$.
Each $H_i^-$ is actual closed smooth of dimension at most one, and its
intersection with $A$ is thin by analytic continuation. Genuine thin
analytic dimension drop makes this closed analytic intersection zero-dimensional.
Take the union of the actual level-zero remainder with the finitely many
actual original-member remainders. Each is genuinely closed and discrete;
a finite union remains closed and discrete, and is analytic. At each point
its singleton germ gives actual regular dimension zero. This is the required
actual envelope. No global finite component family or history certificate
is an assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every whole actual Step 1 superlevel is analytic in carrier dimension two]
\label{hir:dimtwo-whole-stepone-superlevels}
\uses{hir:dimtwo-stepone-nonspecial-envelope,hir:step-one-top-threshold-analytic,hir:analytic-dimzero-subsets-and-constancy}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDimensionTwoStepOneAnalyticSuperlevels.isComplexAnalyticSubset_stepOneInvM_superlevel}
\leanok
Let the lower invariant be any genuine constructed sequence-resolution
invariant. Under the preceding actual Step 1 hypotheses, every superlevel
of the whole actual masked Step 1 value, with finite or infinite lower
threshold and all actual historical entries retained, is globally analytic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the whole threshold $(s,\alpha)$. Its actual superlevel is the union of
the already internally produced whole threshold $(s,\infty)$ superlevel
and the points of the former superlevel outside the latter. Special points
have infinite lower entry and hence cannot lie in the difference. Actual
cosupport nonspecial points lie in the produced zero-dimensional envelope.
Every subset of a genuine closed zero-dimensional analytic set is closed
smooth and analytic, so this actual difference is analytic. Union with the
actual top superlevel proves the assertion. This argument uses the literal
actual historical value; it neither assumes local constancy nor replaces
that value with a proxy tuple.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
