\section{Genuine analytic monomial-invariant superlevels}

The monomial step in W{\l}odarczyk\cite{wlodarczyk05-hironaka},
Proposition \texttt{pr:resol}, Step 2b, uses the maximum of finitely many
feasible boundary-subset keys. Actual divisor exponents are locally
constant on the corresponding smooth boundary traces. These facts give
analytic, rather than merely topological, superlevels for that invariant.

\begin{lemma}[Actual finite-maximum threshold dictionary]
\label{hir:monomial-max-threshold-dictionary}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaMonomialAnalyticSuperlevels.le_inv_iff_exists_feasible}
\leanok
For fixed actual weights $a_i$, a finite set $S$ of member indices and
mark $d$, the genuine monomial invariant $\gamma$ satisfies
\[
 v\le\gamma(S,a,d)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists K\subset S:\ d\le\sum_{i\in K}a_i\ \text{and}\ v\le\operatorname{key}(K,a).
\]
Here $v$ is a genuine nonbottom key value; the key is the actual
lexicographic/colexicographic key used by the monomial algorithm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The invariant is the maximum of the actual finite image of the feasible
subsets. A nonbottom lower bound implies that this finite family is
nonempty. Its actual maximum is attained by one of its feasible subsets,
which witnesses the right side. Conversely the key of any feasible subset
is at most the actual maximum; transitivity proves the lower bound.
No optimal or feasible subset is supplied by the caller.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Produced local analytic monomial superlevel]
\label{hir:monomial-superlevel-local-analytic-dictionary}
\uses{hir:monomial-max-threshold-dictionary}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaMonomialAnalyticSuperlevels.exists_analytic_near_gammaAlong_superlevel}
\leanok
Let $I$ be coherent and nonzero along a genuine closed smooth $N$, and
let the genuine SNC boundary $E$ be transverse to $N$, in positive
ambient dimension. Near each $x\in N$, the actual locus
$\{y\in N:v\le\gamma_N(I,E,d,y)\}$ agrees with an internally produced
global analytic finite union of actual member intersections.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The genuine exponent local-constancy theorem and closedness of missing
members give one neighbourhood on which only the members through $x$
can occur and each occurring member carries its actual original exponent
at $x$. Freeze those genuine exponents as $a$. Select, from the actual
finite feasible subsets of members through $x$, those whose actual key
is at least $v$. For each selected $K$, take the genuine closed smooth
trace $N\cap\bigcap_{i\in K}E_i$. These are analytic by SNC transversality;
their finite union is the produced analytic set.

At any nearby $y\in N$, its actual invariant equals the fixed-weight
invariant on the actual members through $y$. The preceding finite-maximum
dictionary says the threshold holds exactly when one selected feasible
$K$ is contained in that member set, which says precisely that $y$
belongs to the corresponding actual intersection. Both containments
use the same actual neighbourhood and all actual stalk exponents.
The chosen finite analytic set can depend on $x$; no globally constant
coefficient on a disconnected original boundary member is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global analytic monomial superlevels]
\label{hir:monomial-superlevels-global-analytic}
\uses{hir:monomial-superlevel-local-analytic-dictionary}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaMonomialAnalyticSuperlevels.isComplexAnalyticSubset_gammaAlong_superlevel}
\leanok
Every actual monomial-invariant superlevel on $N$ is globally analytic,
including the bottom threshold, without compactness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the bottom threshold the locus is exactly $N$, hence analytic.
For a nonbottom threshold, genuine upper semicontinuity of the monomial
invariant and closedness of $N$ prove that the actual superlevel is closed.
At each point in that locus, the preceding producer supplies an actual
analytic set agreeing with it on a neighbourhood. Restrict that set's
actual holomorphic presentation to the common neighbourhood to present
the actual superlevel. At a point outside the closed locus, a neighbourhood
misses it and the constant function $1$ presents the empty germ. These
actual presentations prove global analyticity; topological upper
semicontinuity is used only for closedness, never for analyticity itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual canonical superlevels at monomial thresholds]
\label{hir:canonical-monomial-threshold-global-analytic}
\uses{hir:monomial-superlevels-global-analytic,hir:canonical-first-entry-analytic-superlevel,hir:canonical-centres}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaMonomialAnalyticSuperlevels.canonical_monomial_superlevel_eq_union,Hironaka.HironakaMonomialAnalyticSuperlevels.isComplexAnalyticSubset_canonical_monomial_superlevel}
\leanok
For the genuine constructed canonical invariant along any actual test
tower of a marked ideal in its invariant domain, every superlevel on
the actual transformed carrier at a monomial threshold $(0,\operatorname{inl}v)$
is globally analytic. It equals the union of the actual first-entry
superlevel at $1$ and the genuine monomial-invariant superlevel at $v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual transform remains in the invariant domain, so the genuine
closed smooth, coherent, SNC, transverse and nonzero-along hypotheses
needed above are internally available. At a point of positive nonmonomial
order, the first entry strictly exceeds $0$, so the lexicographic canonical
value exceeds the monomial threshold regardless of its companion entry.
At order zero, the canonical value is exactly $(0,\operatorname{inl}\gamma)$,
so the comparison is precisely $v\le\gamma$. This proves the exact union
dictionary. Both actual members of that union are globally analytic by
the two genuine producers, and finite union proves the result. No tuple,
coefficient or enhanced-invariant analyticity certificate is an input.
Companion/history thresholds and the auxiliary Hilbert--Samuel chain
engine of Bierstone--Milman, Lemma 13.7, remain separate open obligations.
\end{proof}
