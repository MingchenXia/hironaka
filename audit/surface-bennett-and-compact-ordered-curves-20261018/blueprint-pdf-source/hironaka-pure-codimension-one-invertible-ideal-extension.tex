\section{Original reduced ideals of arbitrary pure hypersurfaces}
\label{sec:hironaka-pure-codimension-one-ideal}

The local equations and Cartan coherence theorems of
\cite{demailly-cadg}, Chapter~II, give the complete original ideal of a
pure analytic hypersurface. The finite local collection used below consists
of the actual global irreducible components meeting a neighbourhood.
Reducible, disconnected and noncompact hypersurfaces are allowed.

\begin{lemma}[Actual finite original component equations]
\label{hir:pure-hypersurface-original-equation}
\uses{rem:global-components,hir:embedded-resolution-pure-dimensional,ana:actual-codimension-one-local-equation,lag:germ-domain}
\source{demailly-cadg:II.4.19,demailly-cadg:II.5.3}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaPureCodimensionOneInvertibleIdeal.exists_nonzero_local_equation_of_pure}
\leanok
Let $W$ be analytic in a complex manifold of dimension $m+1$, with
$W\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{Reg}_m W}$. At every ambient point $x$
there is a nonzero holomorphic germ whose zero set is the entire original
centred chart germ of $W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual global irreducible components of $W$ are locally finite and cover
$W$. Choose an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ meeting only finitely many of
them. A global component is the closure of an actual connected component
of a regular locus, generated at a point $c\in\operatorname{Reg}_k W$.
The original regular density implies that every point of
$W\setminus\operatorname{Reg}_m W$ is nonregular in every dimension:
a regular flattening of dimension $k$ gives a relatively open regular-$k$
piece, and density gives a regular-$m$ point in that piece, forcing $k=m$
by regular dimension uniqueness. Therefore $c$ is regular of dimension
$m$. The closure of its regular-locus component is an actual closed
irreducible analytic subvariety of dimension $m$, with the same genuine
generating regular point. Its codimension-one local-equation theorem
supplies a nonzero holomorphic equation for the entire component chart
germ at $x$, including when $x$ lies outside that component.

Apply this theorem to each of the finitely many components meeting $U$
and take the finite product of their equations. Holomorphicity and all
individual chart-germ equalities hold on a common neighbourhood. The
holomorphic germ ring is a domain, so the product germ is nonzero.
Its zero set is the union of those component zero sets. Local finiteness
and the actual global-component cover identify that union with the whole
original chart germ of $W$. If no component meets $U$, the product is $1$
and the original germ is empty. All components and equations are produced
from the original analytic set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual reduced generators of every nearby stalk]
\label{hir:original-reduced-generator-neighbourhood}
\uses{ana:actual-reduced-holomorphic-equation,oka:cartan-local,oka:function-ideals}
\source{demailly-cadg:II.4.22,demailly-cadg:II.4.29}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaPureCodimensionOneInvertibleIdeal.exists_reduced_generator_neighbourhood}
\leanok
A nonzero holomorphic equation $f$ near the origin internally produces a
smaller neighbourhood and a holomorphic equation $r$ generating the
complete vanishing ideal of $\{f=0\}$ at every point of that neighbourhood.
Their zero sets agree there. This includes empty zero germs and dimension
zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Factoriality and the radical Nullstellensatz produce a reduced germ $r$
with the original zero germ and original complete vanishing ideal
generated by $r$ at the origin. Local Cartan coherence supplies finitely
many holomorphic generators $s_i$ generating all nearby original ideal
stalks on one neighbourhood. At the origin each $s_i$ belongs to $(r)$,
so choose a holomorphic quotient $b_i$ with $s_i=b_i r$ as germs.
Shrink once so that all finitely many quotient identities hold, all
their functions are holomorphic, and the original zero-set equality
holds. Every nearby ideal germ is a finite holomorphic combination of
the $s_i$, and hence of $r$. Conversely every multiple of $r$ vanishes on
the original zero set. These two tests give the whole stalk equality at
every point of the same neighbourhood. The construction of the
neighbourhood uses only holomorphic germs and coherence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An original chart equation gives the entire nearby chart ideal]
\label{hir:original-principal-chart-neighbourhood}
\uses{hir:original-reduced-generator-neighbourhood,oka:cartan-chart-reduction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaPureCodimensionOneInvertibleIdeal.exists_original_principal_chart_neighbourhood}
\leanok
Suppose the centred chart germ of a set $W$ at $x$ equals the zero germ
of a nonzero holomorphic equation $f$. Then a smaller centred chart
neighbourhood has a holomorphic function $r$ generating every original
vanishing-ideal stalk there, and its zero set equals the original chart
germ there. The neighbourhood lies inside the actual chart target.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the preceding reduced-generator theorem to $f$. The original
chart-germ equality holds on a neighbourhood, and implies equality of
all vanishing-ideal stalks at nearby points. Intersect with that
neighbourhood and with the translate of the open actual chart target,
which contains the centred origin. A smaller ball lies in the
intersection. Restricting the holomorphic reduced equation and the
all-stalk generation tests to this ball proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Empty interior gives a nondegenerate original generator]
\label{hir:original-equations-empty-interior-invertible}
\uses{hir:original-principal-chart-neighbourhood,oka:cartan-chart-reduction,ana:ideal-sheaves}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaPureCodimensionOneInvertibleIdeal.isInvertible_vanishingIdeal_of_original_equations}
\leanok
Let $W$ have empty interior. If at every original ambient point its
entire centred chart germ has a nonzero holomorphic equation, then the
complete original vanishing ideal of $W$ is invertible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At an arbitrary point choose the original equation and the preceding
principal chart neighbourhood. Compose its reduced equation with the
actual centred chart map to get a holomorphic function $h$ on an
original manifold neighbourhood $U$. The chart and its inverse transport
each complete original ideal stalk in both directions: a germ vanishing
on $W$ transports to a chart ideal germ, is a multiple of the reduced
equation there, and its holomorphic quotient transports back to a
multiple of $h$. Conversely every multiple of $h$ vanishes on $W$ near
that point. The same actual chart identities give $\{h=0\}\cap U=W\cap U$.
If $h$ vanished on a nonempty open set $Z\subseteq U$, then
$Z\subseteq W$; each point of $Z$ would lie in the empty interior of $W$,
a contradiction. Thus the generator is nondegenerate on $U$ in the
sense required by an invertible ideal. These neighbourhoods at every
original point prove invertibility of the entire original ideal.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual original pure hypersurface ideal is invertible]
\label{hir:pure-hypersurface-original-invertible-ideal}
\uses{hir:pure-hypersurface-original-equation,hir:original-equations-empty-interior-invertible,hir:embedded-resolution-pure-dimensional}
\source{demailly-cadg:II.4.22,demailly-cadg:II.4.29}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaPureCodimensionOneInvertibleIdeal.isInvertible_vanishingIdeal_of_pure}
\leanok
For every analytic $W$ with $W\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{Reg}_m W}$
in a complex manifold of dimension $m+1$, its complete reduced vanishing
ideal is invertible, including at singular points. Compactness and
irreducibility are unnecessary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual finite component equations produce an entire original
nonzero chart equation at every point. Original pure codimension-one
regular density implies that $W$ has empty interior: a nonempty open
subset of $W$ would meet its dimension-$m$ regular locus, whereas a
regular flattening of dimension $m<m+1$ has empty interior.
Apply the preceding all-stalk invertibility criterion to these two
internally proved original geometric facts. Every original local
equation and reduced generator, including at a singular point, is
produced in the argument.
\end{proof}
