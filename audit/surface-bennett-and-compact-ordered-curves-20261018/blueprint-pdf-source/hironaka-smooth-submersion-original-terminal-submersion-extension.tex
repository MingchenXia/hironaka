\begin{lemma}[A genuine biholomorphism pulls every genuine maximal chart back to a maximal chart]
\label{hir:smooth-terminal-biholomorphic-maximal-chart-pullback}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalTerminalSubmersion.biholomorphic_chart_pullback_mem_maximalAtlas}
\leanok
\source{bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\uses{hir:submersion-composition-whole-holomorphic-chart}

with the SAME model dimension. For ANY actual maximal holomorphic chart
$a$ on $Z$, the actual chart $a\eta$ on $\eta^{-1}(\operatorname{source}a)$
is maximal holomorphic. Its inverse is $\eta^{-1}a^{-1}$ and it has the
SAME chart target as $a$. No chart-compatibility certificate is an input.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compose the global genuine homeomorphism underlying $\eta$ with the actual
partial homeomorphism $a$. Its true source is the displayed preimage and
its target is the original target: the inverse image lies in the global
homeomorphism target. The actual partial inverse is exactly
$\eta^{-1}a^{-1}$. Maximality of $a$ gives holomorphicity of $a$ on its
actual source and of $a^{-1}$ on its actual target. Compose these with
the genuine holomorphic $\eta$ and $\eta^{-1}$, on the respective true
domains. Both actual partial maps are therefore holomorphic. A partial
homeomorphism whose forward and inverse maps are holomorphic on their
actual domains belongs to the maximal holomorphic atlas: its two changes
to every original chart are compositions of those genuine holomorphic
partial maps with the original chart and its inverse. This proves actual
chart maximality, including empty charts and zero-dimensional manifolds.
\end{proof}
