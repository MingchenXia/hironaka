\begin{lemma}[The actual component germ at an actual regular point]
\label{hir:general-two-curve-component-germ}
\uses{ana:regular-component}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaGeneralTwoCompanionCurveLoci.eventually_mem_iff_globalComponent_of_regular}
\leanok
If $B$ is an actual global component of an analytic $A$, and $x\in B$ is an
actual regular point of $A$, then $A$ and $B$ have the same germ at $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $B=\overline C$, where $C$ is the genuine connected component of
the actual regular locus through its actual generating regular point.
An actual regular flattening at $x$ has a connected flat-ball neighbourhood
whose trace on $A$ lies in the component of the regular locus through $x$.
Since $x\in\overline C$, this open neighbourhood meets $C$. The common
regular point identifies the two genuine connected components. Thus $x$
belongs to $C$ and on a smaller actual open neighbourhood the whole $A$
lies in $C\subseteq B$. Conversely $B\subseteq A$ follows from the true
analytic component theorem. This gives equality of the actual germs.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A regular cosupport curve has an actual surface carrier]
\label{hir:general-two-curve-carrier-surface}
\uses{hir:one-dimensional-actual-cosupport-dimension,ana:component-local-filling}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaGeneralTwoCompanionCurveLoci.regularAtDim_carrier_two_of_cosupport_one}
\leanok
For an actual dimension-two invariant-domain marked ideal, an actual
regular dimension-one point of its positive-mark cosupport is an actual
regular dimension-two point of its carrier.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The genuine closed smooth carrier has an actual regular dimension $j\le2$
at the point. Inclusion of the real cosupport forces $1\le j$. If $j=1$,
the actual analytic filling lemma applied to the dimension-one cosupport
and this regular carrier makes the whole carrier lie in the cosupport on
a neighbourhood. This contradicts actual coherent nonzero along: choose
a real nonzero germ, express it using the actual finite coherent generators,
and shrink so that every generator belongs to the actual ideal at each
nearby point. Positive cosupport order makes every such generator zero
on the carrier there. Their actual finite holomorphic combination makes
the chosen germ zero at all nearby carrier points, contradicting its
genuine frequent nonzero points. Hence $j=2$. No global maximal-order
condition is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A genuine boundary-containing curve is in the member locus]
\label{hir:general-two-curve-boundary-special}
\uses{hir:general-two-curve-component-germ,hir:dimtwo-boundary-trace-dimension,bcalc:member-intersection}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaGeneralTwoCompanionCurveLoci.mem_memberInterLocus_of_regular_component_subset_member}
\leanok
Let an actual marked ideal be in the dimension-two invariant domain and
let $F$ be a finite list of genuine members of its actual SNC boundary.
If an actual global cosupport component $B$ contains an actual regular
curve point $x$ and $B$ is contained in one member of $F$ through $x$,
then $x$ belongs to the actual member-intersection locus for $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The preceding germ theorem makes $B$ an actual regular dimension-one set
at $x$. Its containing member has a genuine closed smooth trace $H$ on
the carrier, by the actual SNC transversality theorem. The true adapted
coordinate dimension-drop producer bounds this trace by one. Actual
regular dimension monotonicity and $B\subseteq H$ force the trace to have
dimension exactly one at $x$. The true analytic filling lemma therefore
gives $B=H$ as germs at $x$. Since $B$ lies in the real cosupport, the whole
trace $H$ lies in that cosupport near $x$. Every nearby carrier point on
all members of $F$ through $x$ lies in this one chosen member, hence in
$H$ and in the real cosupport. This is precisely the actual member locus.
\end{proof}
