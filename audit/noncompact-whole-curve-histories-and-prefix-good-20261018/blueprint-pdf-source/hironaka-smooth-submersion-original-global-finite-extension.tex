\begin{lemma}[Biholomorphic changes of final spaces preserve genuine submersions]

A genuine holomorphic submersion remains a genuine submersion after
precomposition and postcomposition with biholomorphisms of the source
and target final manifolds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The genuine precomposition theorem pulls back the submersion's actual
domain chart by the source biholomorphism, retaining the same fixed

submersion, it says that every actual biholomorphism is a submersion.
The genuine composition theorem therefore permits postcomposition by
the target biholomorphism. The complement is fixed over the entire map,
so this proves the stated global submersion and not merely pointwise
surjectivity of a differential.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual finite original smooth arrows descend to the original global terminal manifolds]
\label{hir:original-global-finite-terminal-smooth-map-cartesian}
\uses{hir:original-global-terminal-finite-whole-biholomorphism,hir:original-global-finite-submersion-biholomorphic-transport,hir:smooth-original-finite-terminal-intrinsic-smooth-map}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFinite.originalGlobalFiniteSmoothMap,Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFinite.originalGlobalFiniteSmoothMap_properties}
\leanok
\source{bierstone-milman08-functoriality,wlodarczyk05-hironaka}
Let $f:Y\to X$ be a genuine holomorphic submersion and let $M$ be an original
marked ideal in the invariant domain on $X$. Given actual finite canonical
terminal histories for $M$ and $f^*M$, construct a smooth arrow
$G:\widetilde Y_{f^*M}\to\widetilde X_M$ between their genuine original global
terminal manifolds. It is a holomorphic submersion, it satisfies
$P_MG=fP_{f^*M}$ at every point, and its square with these same entire
original projections is Cartesian among holomorphic maps of complex manifolds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original finite terminal producer supplies the actual finite smooth
Cartesian arrow $g:E\to D$. The preceding finite-identification theorem
constructs genuine biholomorphisms $C_Y:\widetilde Y_{f^*M}\to E$ and
$C_X:\widetilde X_M\to D$ from the actual original gluing, with whole
projection equalities. Define $G=C_X^{-1}gC_Y$. The preceding submersion
transport lemma proves it is a submersion, hence holomorphic. Apply the
whole target projection formula at $C_X^{-1}gC_Y(z)$, cancel the inverse,
then apply the finite arrow's projection equality and the whole source
projection formula. The result is exactly $P_MG(z)=fP_{f^*M}(z)$.

For the Cartesian property take an arbitrary complex manifold $W$ and
holomorphic maps $a:W\to Y$, $b:W\to\widetilde X_M$ with $fa=P_Mb$.
The whole target formula shows that $(a,C_Xb)$ is a cone for the actual
finite Cartesian square. Its unique holomorphic lift $w:W\to E$ gives
a lift $C_Y^{-1}w$ into the genuine original global source. The source
projection equality and both inverse equations give the required source
leg; the finite target leg and the target inverse equation give the $G$
leg. Any other holomorphic lift, composed with $C_Y$, is a finite lift
for the same cone, because the two actual whole projection formulas and
$G=C_X^{-1}gC_Y$ identify its legs. Finite uniqueness identifies these
maps; injectivity of $C_Y$ gives uniqueness of the global lift.
\end{proof}
