\section{Compact escape of actual centre footprints}
\begin{theorem}[Compact-exhaustion escape criterion]
\label{hir:disjoint-compact-exhaustion-escape}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.locallyFinite_iff_exhaustion_escape}
\leanok
Let $K_m$ be a compact exhaustion of a topological space and $F_i$ an arbitrary
natural-number-indexed family of subsets. The family is locally finite if
and only if, for every $m$, some $N$ satisfies $F_i\cap K_m=\varnothing$ for
all $i\ge N$. Empty footprints and repeated footprints retain their indices.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the family is locally finite, compactness of $K_m$ gives finitely many
indices meeting $K_m$: choose at each point a neighbourhood meeting finitely
many members, extract a finite subcover, and take the finite union of its
finite index sets. A finite subset of the natural numbers is bounded, so
all indices after a strictly larger bound miss $K_m$. Conversely, at a point
$x$ choose $K_m$ which is a neighbourhood of $x$, as provided by the interior
nesting of a compact exhaustion. Its meeting indices lie in the finite set
of indices strictly below the escaping threshold $N$. Thus this very
neighbourhood witnesses local finiteness.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Actual closed-tail protected opens]
\label{hir:disjoint-compact-escape-protected-opens}
\uses{hir:disjoint-compact-exhaustion-escape}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.protectedOpen,TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.protectedOpen_mono,TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.exists_compact_subset_protectedOpen}
\leanok
Suppose each $F_i$ is closed and the escape condition holds. The literal
sets $U_N=X\setminus\bigcup_{i\ge N}F_i$ are increasing open sets. Every
compact subset of $X$ is contained in some $U_N$, and these opens cover $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding criterion proves local finiteness. Every tail subfamily is
locally finite, so its union of closed members is closed; its complement
is the asserted literal open. Increasing $N$ removes members from the union,
so the complements increase. For a compact set $L$, choose an exhaustion
member $K_m$ containing it. Escape supplies one $N$ for which every member
of the entire tail misses $K_m$. No point of $L$ therefore belongs to that
tail union, proving $L\subset U_N$. Singletons are compact, giving the cover.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Escape produces local finiteness of actual centre images]
\label{hir:disjoint-actual-centre-exhaustion-escape}
\uses{hir:disjoint-compact-exhaustion-escape,hir:disjoint-compact-escape-protected-opens,hir:growing-canonical-sequence-closed-images}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.actual_centres_locallyFinite,TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.actual_centres_compact_protected,TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaDisjointCompactExhaustionEscape.actual_centres_choice_escape}
\leanok
For any genuine growing blowup sequence on the original base, escape of its
actual centre footprints along a compact exhaustion proves their actual
local finiteness and supplies increasing literal closed-tail-complement opens
containing every compact set. No global canonicality, chosen invariant,
resolving-block existence, fair selection, or local-finiteness certificate is
an input. A second-countable locally compact base supplies the compact
exhaustion itself.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the first theorem to the literal images of the actual centres under
the actual finite-prefix projections. Each is closed because the actual
centre is closed and the genuine prefix projection is proper. Apply the
second theorem to those same footprints. In a locally compact second-countable
space, countably many relatively compact neighbourhoods cover the space;
finite accumulation and recursive compact enlargement provide a compact
exhaustion with each member inside the interior of the next. Thus the
compact-exhaustion existence theorem supplies the required exhaustion under
these ordinary topological hypotheses. This argument proves a criterion for
an existing sequence; it does not select centres or construct a fair global
resolution scheduler.
\end{proof}
