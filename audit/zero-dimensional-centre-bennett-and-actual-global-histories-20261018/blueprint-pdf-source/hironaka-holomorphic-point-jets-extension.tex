\section{Faithful finite jets of the entire holomorphic germ ring}
\label{sec:hironaka-holomorphic-point-jets}

Hilbert--Samuel values concern the entire reduced current local ring, rather
than a selected equation or an order-one substitute. In the convention of
\cite{bierstone-milman97-invent-math}, Remark~1.3, the value at $\ell$ is
$\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal O/(I+\mathfrak m^{\ell+1})$. The following genuine
finite-jet construction establishes that this dimension is finite for every
original ideal. It does not assert that these values have a semicoherent
transformation presentation or supply a normally flat centre.

\begin{lemma}[Literal point powers and actual local-ring powers]
\label{hir:whole-point-power-ring-dictionary}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.mem_maximalIdeal_pow_of_point_power,Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.mem_maximalIdeal_pow_iff_point_power}
\leanok
\uses{hir:order-taylor,lag:germ-domain}
Let $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,0}$ be the entire genuine holomorphic germ
ring and $\mathfrak m$ its actual maximal ideal. For every function $f$
holomorphic near zero and every $q\ge0$, its actual germ lies in
$\mathfrak m^q$ if and only if $f$ belongs to the $q$th power of the original
point ideal at zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual maximal ideal consists exactly of germs with value zero. For
one direction, an original point-power expression is a finite holomorphic
sum of products of original point-ideal functions. Passing every actual
representative to its holomorphic germ carries sums, coefficients and
products to the same sums, coefficients and products in $R$. Induction on
$q$ therefore places the resulting germ in $\mathfrak m^q$. At $q=0$
both powers are the whole holomorphic ring.

For the other direction, let $J_q$ consist of germs having an original
representative in that point power. This is an actual ideal: the power is
closed under finite sums and holomorphic coefficients, and membership is
invariant under equality on a neighbourhood. The ideal $J_0$ is $R$.
Every element of $\mathfrak m$ has a holomorphic representative vanishing
at zero and hence belongs to the original point ideal. If
$\mathfrak m^q\subseteq J_q$, products of these representatives lie in the
$(q+1)$st original point power. Since $J_{q+1}$ is an ideal, the full product
ideal $\mathfrak m^q\mathfrak m$ is contained in it. This proves
$\mathfrak m^q\subseteq J_q$ by induction. Equality of two actual germs is
neighbourhood equality of their original representatives, so membership
transfers to the stated original $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The exact whole-jet kernel]
\label{hir:whole-point-power-derivative-kernel}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.mem_maximalIdeal_pow_iff_iteratedFDeriv}
\leanok
\uses{hir:whole-point-power-ring-dictionary,hir:order-taylor}
An actual holomorphic germ lies in $\mathfrak m^q$ exactly when all its
actual iterated complex derivatives of degrees less than $q$ vanish at zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The preceding dictionary identifies membership with the genuine original
point power. The convergent holomorphic Taylor theorem identifies this
power with vanishing of every derivative below degree $q$: the forward
implication differentiates products, and the reverse implication expresses
the convergent Taylor remainder as a sum of degree-$q$ coordinate products
with holomorphic coefficients. The original point-power derivative theorem
therefore gives both implications. In the original Euclidean chart the
preferred chart is the identity, so these derivatives are precisely the
unmodified derivatives of the same original representative at zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The actual finite linear jet]
\label{hir:actual-finite-holomorphic-linear-jet}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.holGermComplexAlgebra,Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.finiteJet,Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.finiteJet_mk}
\leanok
\uses{hir:whole-point-power-derivative-kernel}
Give $R$ its actual complex algebra structure through constant germs. The
$q$th jet of a germ is the finite tuple $(D^j f(0))_{0\le j<q}$, with each
entry an algebraic complex multilinear map on $(\mathbb C^n)^j$. These
are the derivatives of any genuine original holomorphic representative.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Well-defined linearity and the exact kernel]
\label{hir:actual-finite-linear-jet-kernel}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.ker_finiteJet}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-finite-holomorphic-linear-jet,hir:whole-point-power-derivative-kernel}
The actual finite jet is a complex linear map. Its kernel is exactly
$\mathfrak m^q$, considered as a complex subspace of $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Representatives of the same germ agree on an open neighbourhood, so every
iterated derivative agrees at zero. Thus the finite tuple is well defined
on the entire original germ ring. Every holomorphic representative is
analytic near zero and hence has all complex derivatives there. The
iterated derivative of $f+g$ is $D^j f+D^j g$, and the derivative of a
constant scalar multiple $cf$ is $cD^j f$. Constant-germ multiplication is
exactly this same scalar multiplication, proving complex linearity.
The jet vanishes precisely when each actual derivative below degree $q$
vanishes. The preceding whole-jet kernel theorem identifies this condition
with the actual maximal-ideal power, including $q=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite dimension of every actual point quotient]
\label{hir:actual-point-quotient-finite-dimensional}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.finiteDimensional_quotient_maximalIdeal_pow,Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.finiteDimensional_quotient_sup_maximalIdeal_pow}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-finite-linear-jet-kernel}
For every $q\ge0$, the entire actual quotient $R/\mathfrak m^q$ is a
finite-dimensional complex vector space. For every original ideal $I$ of
$R$, the actual quotient $R/(I+\mathfrak m^q)$ is also finite dimensional.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each degree-$j$ multilinear-map space on finite-dimensional $\mathbb C^n$
with values in $\mathbb C$ is finite dimensional: repeated currying
identifies it with iterated finite-dimensional spaces of linear maps.
There are only $q$ entries, so the whole finite jet target is finite
dimensional. The actual linear map's first isomorphism theorem and its
proved exact kernel identify $R/\mathfrak m^q$ with its genuine image,
a subspace of that finite-dimensional target. This proves finite dimension
without a supplied Taylor-polynomial basis or a finiteness certificate.
Since $\mathfrak m^q\subseteq I+\mathfrak m^q$, the actual quotient map
$R/\mathfrak m^q\to R/(I+\mathfrak m^q)$ is surjective. Its image is thus
finite dimensional, proving the second conclusion. At $q=0$ the quotient
is zero; at $n=0$ the same real jet construction includes the degree-zero
value and the higher zero multilinear entries.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Actual Hilbert--Samuel point values]
\label{hir:actual-hilbert-samuel-point-value}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHolomorphicPointJets.hilbertSamuelValue}
\leanok
\uses{hir:actual-point-quotient-finite-dimensional}
For an original ideal $I\subseteq R$, define its actual point value at
$\ell$ by
\[
 H_I(\ell)=\dim_{\mathbb C}R/(I+\mathfrak m^{\ell+1}).
\]
The preceding theorem proves this dimension is finite for every original
ideal; no finite-dimensional quotient hypothesis is part of the definition.
\end{definition}
