\section{Actual pointwise normal crossings give genuine original good neighborhoods}

The preceding genuine trace centre avoids the union of actual original NC
neighborhoods. We now prove the missing pointwise dictionary, from the
literal original adapted-coordinate definition. No neighborhood or
preservation certificate is given by the caller. This proves openness
in the actual complex topology, not global analyticity of the whole bad
locus or termination of the modified driver.

\begin{lemma}[Actual finite closed coordinate families propagate to genuine nearby charts]
\label{hir:sharp-closed-coordinate-family-genuine-neighborhoods}
\source{bierstone-milman97-invent-math:page-0087}
\uses{hir:snc}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.sub_mem_coordSubspace_iff,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.coordinateFamilyAt_of_flattening,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.eventually_incident_subset,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.eventually_coordinateFamilyAt}
\leanok
For a finite family of closed original subsets $A_i$, an original
simultaneous adapted-coordinate witness at $x$ gives a genuine open
neighborhood where the same coordinate-index sets give adapted coordinates
at every actual point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Intersect the actual chart-and-flattening source with the complements of
the finitely many closed members which miss $x$. No unseen member can
appear in this neighborhood. Fix a true nearby point $y$. Its preferred
chart transitions holomorphically to the original chart. Recenter the
original flattening at the actual chart value of $y$ and subtract its
actual flattened value. Compose with that genuine chart transition.
Both directions are holomorphic and have the true inverse equations;
the new source lies in the actual preferred-chart target and its true
center maps to zero. For each member containing $y$, its old membership
dictionary applies because it also contains $x$. That same dictionary
puts the actual flattened value of $y$ in its coordinate subspace.
Translation by a vector in that subspace preserves it. Thus every true
membership equation is retained by the produced centered coordinates at
$y$. This constructs the whole nearby family, with the same actual
finite coordinate sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Every original pointwise normal-crossings witness gives an actual NC open]
\label{hir:sharp-pointwise-normal-crossings-original-open-recognition}
\source{bierstone-milman97-invent-math:page-0087}
\uses{hir:sharp-closed-coordinate-family-genuine-neighborhoods,hir:sharp-single-boundary-trace-avoids-good-nc-region}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.normalCrossingsAt,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.eventually_normalCrossingsAt,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.normalCrossingsAt_singleton_preimage_val_iff,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.normalCrossingsAt_iff_mem_originalSmoothNCRegion}

Let the original smooth carrier $W$ and original boundary member $H$ be
closed. At any point of $W$, the literal original pointwise adapted
coordinate witness for $W$ and $[H]$ is equivalent to membership in the
previously defined union of genuine original NC neighborhoods.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the preceding lemma to the actual two-member family indexed by
the carrier and boundary positions. Its finite closed-member condition
holds for both true original sets. At nearby carrier points the boundary
cardinality-one condition is inherited from the original witness: every
incident member already passed through the original point. Thus the
whole actual neighborhood has the genuine pointwise NC property.
Restrict its coordinates through the true open inclusion; the original
coordinate-family open-restriction dictionary gives normal crossings
of the literal restricted carrier and boundary on this true open.
This is a witness for the original NC-open region. Conversely an actual
NC-open witness supplies coordinates at the true subtype point over
the original carrier point. The genuine open-inclusion coordinate
dictionary identifies them with literal original adapted coordinates;
its boundary positions and cardinalities are the same.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual original good locus is exactly the genuine original NC-open region]
\label{hir:sharp-original-pointwise-good-locus-whole-open-dictionary}
\source{bierstone-milman97-invent-math:page-0087}
\uses{hir:sharp-pointwise-normal-crossings-original-open-recognition}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.originalPointwiseGoodLocus,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.originalPointwiseGoodLocus_eq_originalSmoothNCRegion,Hironaka.HironakaSharpNormalCrossingsOpenLocus.originalPointwiseGoodLocus_isOpen}
\leanok
The complement of $W$, together with its genuine pointwise normal-crossing
points relative to $[H]$, equals the preceding genuine original NC-open
region. In particular the whole original good locus is open.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At points of $W$ use the preceding actual dictionary in both directions.
Outside closed $W$, its open complement has empty restricted carrier,
so its true normal-crossings condition is vacuous and supplies a genuine
NC-open witness. These cases prove the literal whole-set equality.
The already proved openness of the genuine NC-open region gives
openness of this actual original good locus. No new analytic bad-locus
claim is substituted for this topological conclusion.
\end{proof}
