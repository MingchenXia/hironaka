\section{Actual hypersurface maximum-multiplicity phases}

This section proves the literal current-centre alternative of
Bierstone--Milman\cite{bierstone-milman97-invent-math}, Theorem 1.6(1),
for compact hypersurfaces. It uses fresh maximum reduced-multiplicity
phases and the actual controlled transform of marked ideals as in
Bierstone--Milman\cite{bierstone-milman08-functoriality}, Section 2.
The full higher-codimension Hilbert--Samuel invariant assertion and
noncompact locally finite resolution are separate conclusions.
This section follows the fresh multiplicity phases used in the proof of
embedded resolution for a hypersurface. It concerns the actual current
reduced vanishing ideal, not the original marked ideal's nonmonomial
part alone. The geometric and analytic lemmas below identify those
ideals on every actual recorded prefix of one fixed maximum-order phase.

\begin{lemma}[Regular density survives a singular-centre blowup]
\label{hir:singular-centre-regular-density}
\uses{bcalc:strict-geo}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.strictTransform_subset_closure_regularLocus}
\leanok
Let W be closed analytic in a complex manifold, with
W contained in the closure of its dimension-d regular locus. Let C be a
closed smooth centre contained in W minus that regular locus, and let
pi be its actual blowup. Then its actual strict transform S is contained
in the closure of its own dimension-d regular locus.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The centre misses the entire regular locus, so W is contained in the
closure of W minus C. Therefore S is the actual geometric strict transform,
the closure of pi inverse-image of W minus C. Fix a point z of that
defining inverse-image set and an arbitrary open neighbourhood U of z.
Since pi(z) is outside C, z is outside the actual exceptional locus and
the image of U intersected with its open complement is a neighbourhood
of pi(z). Original regular density supplies a point of $\operatorname{Reg}_d$ W in that
image, lifted to a point y of U outside the exceptional locus. Its image
is outside C since it is regular. The original regular flattening transports
through the actual local biholomorphic inverse of pi at y; the local
strict-transform membership germ is the inverse-image germ, so y is in
$\operatorname{Reg}_d$ S. Thus every defining point is in the closure of $\operatorname{Reg}_d$ S. Taking
closures proves the claimed density on all S, including points of the
new divisor. No current purity or transformed regular-density certificate
is supplied.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual derivative maximal-order bound]
\label{hir:actual-reduced-derivative-bound}
\uses{hir:order-taylor,bcalc:giraud-strict}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.isMaximalOrder_vanishingIdeal_of_order_le,Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.order_le_of_isMaximalOrder}
\leanok
For the actual complete vanishing ideal of an analytic set W, a bound
mu on its actual order on W implies that its mu-th iterated derivative
ideal contains 1 at every ambient point. Conversely this derivative-unit
condition for any actual ideal implies the ambient order bound. Under
an actual admissible blowup with I contained in the mu-th centre ideal
power, the genuine derivative-transform inclusion preserves this unit
condition for the actual controlled transform.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Containing 1 after mu derivatives prevents order at least mu+1: the
iterated Giraud inclusion would force that 1 to vanish to order one.
Conversely absence of the order-(mu+1) locus gives actual derivatives
of order at most mu whose values do not vanish at each point. Their
generated coherent derivative ideal therefore contains 1 locally.
Under the actual blowup, the derivative transform inclusion places the
pullback of this iterated derivative ideal inside the mu-th derivative
ideal of the actual controlled transform. Pulling back the actual unit
and taking mark zero gives the new unit. These are the actual previously
proved derivative inclusions and local Taylor witnesses, not a newly
introduced bound-preservation certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole current ideals on actual maximum-order test towers]
\label{hir:maximum-phase-entire-current-ideal}
\uses{hir:singular-centre-regular-density,hir:actual-reduced-derivative-bound,hir:actual-max-order-hypersurface-strict-ideal}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.strictIdeal_and_geometry_geoTransformAlong,Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.stageCentres_singular_and_exact_order}
\leanok
Let W be an analytic hypersurface in dimension m+1, with actual
invertible vanishing ideal and dense m-dimensional regular locus, and
let E be an SNC boundary. Let mu be at least two and bound the actual
order of $I_W$ at every point of W. On every actual admissible test tower
of the original marked ideal (X,X,E,$I_W$,mu), every current strict
transform is analytic, has dense m-dimensional regular locus and an
actual invertible vanishing ideal. Its actual marked transform ideal
is exactly its complete reduced vanishing ideal. Its order is at most
mu everywhere. Every recorded centre is contained in the singular
locus of the actual current strict transform and has order exactly mu.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Outside the closed W its actual ideal contains 1, so the given bound
extends to the whole ambient space. The derivative maximal-order
criterion supplies its actual derivative-unit condition. Induct on the
actual recorded blowup tower. The empty tower gives the original data.
At a successor prefix, genuine admissibility and the preceding entire
ideal equality put its next centre in the order-mu locus of the actual
current reduced ideal. As its current order is bounded by mu everywhere,
it has exact order mu on that centre. Order one at regular points and
mu at least two put the entire centre in the current singular locus,
including points on the accumulated boundary.

Apply the previously proved actual one-step whole strict-ideal theorem
to that current hypersurface, its actual SNC boundary, its true order
bound and the literal next blowup. It proves the next controlled ideal
is the complete reduced strict ideal and is invertible. The actual
derivative-unit transform maintains the global mu bound; regular density
is preserved by the preceding singular-centre lemma. Analyticity comes
from the actual blowup theorem. The actual strict-transform composition
identity identifies this successor with the strict transform by the
original prefix projection. All induction assertions refer to the true
recorded prefix and its actual objects. No equality or centre-position
data for later stages is supplied by the caller.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A genuinely constructed finite multiplicity-drop phase]
\label{hir:constructed-multiplicity-drop-phase}
\uses{hir:maximum-phase-entire-current-ideal,hir:actual-fresh-maximal-multiplicity-centre,hir:resolution-driver}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.exists_maximal_multiplicity_drop_phase}
\leanok
Suppose additionally W is compact and has an actual point of order at
least two. There exist an internally chosen finite maximum mu, at least
two, and a genuinely constructed finite canonical resolution tower of
(X,X,E,$I_W$,mu) such that its entire recorded centres lie in the current
strict-transform singular loci with exact current order mu, and its
final current reduced ideal has order strictly less than mu everywhere.
Its final strict transform is analytic and compact, with dense
m-dimensional regular locus, invertible vanishing ideal and actual SNC
boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the actual finite maximum of the original reduced order internally
by upper semicontinuity and compactness. The original pure hypersurface
geometry supplies the invariant-domain data for the genuine marked
ideal at this mu. Its cosupport is closed and contained in compact W,
so the actual compact-support canonical resolution constructor gives
an actual finite tower with final cosupport empty. The whole-current-ideal
and recorded-centre induction theorem applies to this tower, hence
identifies the final cosupport as the order-mu locus of the actual
final reduced vanishing ideal. Its emptiness says every actual order
is strictly below mu. The actual proper projection gives compactness;
the preceding theorem supplies all the remaining geometric conclusions.
This is a produced multiplicity drop for a true canonical tower, not an
assumed terminating tower or a post-reinitialization comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order one of the actual invertible reduced ideal gives regularity]
\label{hir:actual-order-one-regular-hypersurface}
\uses{hir:order-taylor}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.regularAtDim_of_order_lt_two_of_isInvertible}
\leanok
Let W be closed in dimension m+1 and have an actual invertible vanishing
ideal. At a point x of W where that ideal has order less than two,
W is regular of dimension m.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the actual invertible local generator f on one neighbourhood U of x,
generating every original nearby ideal stalk. It vanishes at x. Its
actual preferred-chart differential is nonzero: otherwise every test
germ in $I_W$ has the form a f, whose value and first differential vanish
at x. The actual Taylor criterion puts every such germ in the square
of the point ideal, forcing order at least two, a contradiction.
The actual local zero set of f is W: membership in W makes the generator
vanish; outside closed W the original ideal contains 1, so local generation
prevents f from vanishing. Transfer this equation to the actual chart.
It is an actual one-equation holomorphic presentation and its single
nonzero differential is linearly independent. The actual holomorphic
inverse function theorem gives a flattening to the codimension-one plane,
which transports back to a genuine RegularAtDim witness for W at x.
No regularity, local equation or post-transform ideal certificate is input.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine finite multiplicity restarts to a smooth hypersurface]
\label{hir:finite-reduced-multiplicity-restarts}
\uses{hir:constructed-multiplicity-drop-phase,hir:actual-order-one-regular-hypersurface,hir:actual-stage-centres-concatenation,hir:singular-phases-preserve-regular-open}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.exists_tower_smooth_of_order_le,Hironaka.HironakaHypersurfaceMultiplicityDescent.exists_tower_smooth_singular_stage_centres}
\leanok
For a compact analytic hypersurface W in dimension m+1 with invertible
actual reduced ideal, dense m-dimensional regular locus, and SNC boundary,
a finite tower exists whose final actual strict transform is smooth of
dimension m and whose every actual centre lies in the current strict
transform's singular locus with normal crossings with the accumulated
boundary. It avoids every original open set on which W is dimension-m
regular.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First assume an integer b bounds the actual original order on W. Induct
strongly on b, universally over the actual ambient manifold and the
original geometric data. If no point of W has order at least two, the
actual order-one lemma proves regularity at every point, and the empty
tower suffices. Otherwise choose the actual attained maximum mu internally
and apply the genuinely constructed finite multiplicity-drop phase. The
attained original maximum satisfies mu at most b. Its final complete
reduced ideal has order strictly below mu everywhere, hence is bounded
by the integer mu-1, strictly less than b. Its actual final hypersurface
is analytic and compact, with invertible actual reduced ideal and dense
m-dimensional regular locus, and its actual final boundary is SNC.
Apply the induction hypothesis to precisely these actual produced objects.
Concatenate the two actual finite towers. The preceding composition lemma
preserves the literal singular containment and normal-crossing condition
at every recorded centre, and identifies the final actual strict transform.
The actual marked-domain and compactness maximum argument supplies an
original finite bound internally when needed; the resulting tower's
existence does not assume a terminating sequence. Finally, any such
singular-centre tower avoids every original regular open by induction:
above that open the prior prefix is genuinely biholomorphic, so current
regularity transports from the original W and excludes the next singular
centre. This proves actual avoidance for the entire constructed tower.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Density outside the original singular part and boundary]
\label{hir:regular-density-outside-boundary-union-singular}

If dimension-m regular points are dense in closed W and W is dense

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Natural compact pure hypersurface embedded resolution]

 (W\setminus\operatorname{Reg}_m W)\cup(W\cap\bigcup E).
\]

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}
