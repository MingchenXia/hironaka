\section{Actual original analytic monic vertex generators}

Fix the genuine original Taylor map $T:R\to S$ at the origin of $\mathbb C^n$.
All original analytic germs below are constructed internally. The monomial
order in the basis statement is the actual degree-lexicographic order.
Canonical reduced formal basis convergence is not asserted.

\begin{theorem}[Every formal extended member has actual original ideal jet representatives]
\label{hir:actual-analytic-ideal-member-finite-jet-lift}
\uses{hir:actual-formal-extension-whole-original-ideal-contraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAnalyticVertexBasis.exists_original_ideal_member_jet_lift}
\leanok
For every original ideal $I$, every $F\in T(I)S$ and every $q\geq0$, there
exists $g\in I$ such that $T(g)$ and $F$ have identical coefficients at
all exponents of total degree less than $q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original ring is Noetherian, so internally choose finite generators
$g_i$ of $I$. Membership in their actual formal extension gives an actual
finite expression $F=\sum_i A_iT(g_i)$. For each formal $A_i$, use its
actual polynomial truncation through degree $q$, evaluated as an original
analytic polynomial germ $a_i$. The already proved genuine approximation
identity says $T(a_i)T(g_i)$ and $A_iT(g_i)$ have the same coefficients
in degrees less than $q$. The original germ $g=\sum_i a_ig_i$ belongs
to $I$ and finite additivity gives the stated whole finite jet equality.
No formal coefficient is assumed convergent and empty families are allowed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A finite actual analytic family realizes all true monic vertices and generates the entire original ideal]
\label{hir:actual-original-analytic-monic-vertex-basis}
\uses{hir:actual-analytic-ideal-member-finite-jet-lift,hir:actual-formal-vertex-division-identity,hir:actual-formal-vertex-series-whole-ideal-generation}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualAnalyticVertexBasis.exists_actual_original_analytic_vertex_basis}
\leanok
For every original ideal $I$, let $V$ be the actual finite set of minimal
vertices of the genuine degree-lex initial diagram of $T(I)S$.
There exists an actual original analytic family $(g_\alpha)_{\alpha\in V}$
with $g_\alpha\in I$, $T(g_\alpha)\ne0$, initial exponent exactly $\alpha$
and coefficient exactly $1$ at $\alpha$. Moreover the entire original ideal
is $I=(g_\alpha)_{\alpha\in V}$. No proposed standard basis, generator
family or analytic convergence certificate is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each actual vertex $\alpha$, use the already constructed genuine monic
formal vertex series of $T(I)S$. Apply the preceding original-ideal jet lift
with $q=|\alpha|+1$. Its original representative $g_\alpha$ has coefficient
$1$ at $\alpha$. Every exponent strictly lower in degree-lex has total
degree at most $|\alpha|$, so its coefficient also agrees with the formal
vertex series and is zero. Thus $T(g_\alpha)$ is nonzero and has exactly
the required original initial exponent and monic coefficient.

Set $J=(T(g_\alpha))S$. Then $J\subseteq T(I)S$. Its initial diagram
contains every vertex of the latter and hence, by upward closure, contains
the whole latter diagram. The reverse inclusion follows from ideal inclusion.
To infer whole ideal equality from these equal diagrams, divide any
$F\in T(I)S$ by the genuine internally constructed vertex family of $J$.
Its complete remainder has zero coefficients at all diagram exponents of
$J$. The remainder still belongs to $T(I)S$ because the subtracted finite
sum belongs to $J$. If nonzero its actual initial exponent belongs to the
diagram of $T(I)S$, hence of $J$, contradicting that coefficient vanishing.
So the remainder is zero and $F\in J$. This proves $J=T(I)S$ using whole
formal division, rather than assuming that a matching finite jet generates.
Finally extension of the original span of the $g_\alpha$ is exactly $J$.
The previously proved contraction identity for every entire original ideal
therefore gives $(g_\alpha)=I$. Empty vertex sets, the zero and unit ideals,
and dimension zero remain covered. This analytic family need not equal the
canonical reduced formal basis in its higher coefficients.
\end{proof}
