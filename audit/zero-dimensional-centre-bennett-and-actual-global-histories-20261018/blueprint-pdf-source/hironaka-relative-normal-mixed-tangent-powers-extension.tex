\section{Actual ambient powers retain every mixed tangent degree}

Let $B=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^d,0}$, $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{d+t},0}$,
let $M=\mathfrak m_B R$ through the original tangent pullback, and let
$J$ be the actual normal-coordinate ideal. The genuine relative Hadamard
decomposition gives $\mathfrak m_R=M+J$. The ambient Hilbert--Samuel
quotient of $R/I$ therefore retains mixed tangent and normal orders.
This chapter makes that weighted quotient explicit, including every
original numerator. It is an algebraic prerequisite for the ambient
dimension calculation; no blowup descent or Hilbert--Samuel constancy
is a conclusion here.

\begin{lemma}[Every power of a whole ideal sum retains its mixed weights]
\label{hir:relative-normal-whole-mixed-ideal-power-expansion}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.ideal_sup_pow_eq_mixed}
\leanok
For ideals $M,J$ in a commutative ring and every $s\geq0$,
\[
 (M+J)^s=\sum_{q=0}^s M^{s-q}J^q.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The binomial theorem in the semiring of ideals writes this power as
the sum of the mixed products multiplied by the binomial coefficients.
Ideal addition is ideal sum and is idempotent. Hence multiplication
of an ideal by a positive natural-number coefficient leaves that
ideal unchanged. For each $0\leq q\leq s$, the binomial coefficient
is positive, so the corresponding term is exactly $M^{s-q}J^q$.
There are no other terms in the binomial sum. This proves the equality,
including $s=0$, when both sides are the unit ideal.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The genuine original ambient maximal power is the mixed ideal]
\label{hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-maximal-power}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-whole-mixed-ideal-power-expansion,hir:relative-normal-original-fibre-maximal-ideal}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalMixedTangentPower,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalMaximalIdeal_power_eq_mixed,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalAmbientIdeal_sup_maximal_power_eq_mixed,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalNormalPower_le_mixed}
\leanok
For every actual original whole ideal $I\subset R$ and every $s$,
\[
 I+\mathfrak m_R^s=I+\sum_{q=0}^s M^{s-q}J^q.
\]
In particular the degree-$q$ term retains its original tangent weight
$M^{s-q}$, rather than replacing the ambient quotient by a fibre quotient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the previously proved whole original identity
$\mathfrak m_R=M+J$ into the first lemma and add the same original
whole ideal $I$ to both sides. This retains the actual original ring,
all its original powers, and all mixed products.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every actual ambient jet is a weighted quotient of the normal truncation]
\label{hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-truncation-quotient}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-maximal-power}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalNormalTruncation,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalMixedTruncationIdeal,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalAmbientMixedTruncation,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalMixedTruncation_kernel_eq,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalAmbientMixedTruncationAlgEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.originalAmbientMixedTruncationAlgEquiv_mk}
\leanok
Put $T_s=R/(I+J^s)$, let $\pi_s:R\to T_s$ be the actual quotient map,
and let $K_s$ be the image in $T_s$ of
$\sum_{q=0}^s M^{s-q}J^q$. There is a genuine complex algebra isomorphism
\[
 T_s/K_s\simeq R/(I+\mathfrak m_R^s)
\]
that sends the double class of every actual original numerator $g$
to its original ambient class $[g]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The mixed sum contains $J^s$ as its $q=s$ term. Thus the actual normal
truncation quotient map is followed by a well-defined map to the
ambient quotient, and the image ideal $K_s$ lies in its kernel.
The third isomorphism theorem for the actual quotient identifies
$T_s/K_s$ with $R/(I+J^s+\sum_q M^{s-q}J^q)$. Containment of $J^s$
in the mixed sum removes precisely that redundant term, and the
preceding identity identifies the remaining ideal with
$I+\mathfrak m_R^s$. The quotient maps and the third-isomorphism map
all send an original numerator to its same class and preserve
complex constants. Their composite therefore has the stated
complex algebra structure and the original numerator formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The existing whole ambient Hilbert--Samuel value uses the same weighted quotient]
\label{hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-hilbert-samuel}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-original-ambient-mixed-truncation-quotient,hir:actual-hilbert-samuel-point-value}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.finiteDimensional_originalAmbientMixedTruncation,Hironaka.HironakaRelativeNormalMixedTangentPowers.hilbertSamuelValue_eq_originalAmbientMixedTruncation}
\leanok
For every $\ell$, the existing whole ambient Hilbert--Samuel value
of the original ideal $I$ is the complex dimension of
$T_{\ell+1}/K_{\ell+1}$. This quotient is finite dimensional for every
original $I$, without a normal-flatness hypothesis.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The preceding complex algebra isomorphism gives a complex linear
isomorphism with the actual original quotient defining the existing
Hilbert--Samuel value. That original quotient is finite dimensional
by the genuine original finite-jet theorem. Transport finite dimension
and equality of complex dimensions through this linear isomorphism.
\end{proof}
