\section{Whole original Taylor expansion commutes with genuine analytic differentiation}

Let $R$ be the original holomorphic germ ring in $n$ coordinates, let
$T:R\to\mathbb C[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$ be its genuine original Taylor map,
and let $D_i$ be actual differentiation of holomorphic representatives
in the true $i$th coordinate direction. This is the existing whole analytic
germ derivative; it is independent of representatives.

\begin{lemma}[Actual analytic polynomial differentiation retains the original complex coefficients]
\label{hir:actual-original-polynomial-coordinate-derivative}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-derivation-evaluation,hir:actual-original-canonical-finite-taylor-polynomial}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalTaylorDerivatives.originalDerivative_polynomialEval}
\leanok
For every genuine complex polynomial $P$, one has
$D_i(P(x))=(\partial_iP)(x)$ in the whole original analytic germ ring.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lift the genuine complex coefficients to their actual constant germs on the
zero-dimensional base. The existing entire relative polynomial derivative
theorem identifies analytic differentiation with formal polynomial
differentiation under evaluation in the normal coordinates. Polynomial
differentiation commutes with this actual coefficient lift: each coefficient
at $\alpha$ on both sides is the actual lifted value of
$(\alpha_i+1)[x^{\alpha+e_i}]P$. Actual zero-base coefficient evaluation is
the original complex polynomial evaluation. Combining these equalities
gives the asserted whole germ identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Entire actual Taylor series commute with original coordinate differentiation]
\label{hir:actual-whole-original-taylor-coordinate-derivative}
\uses{hir:actual-original-polynomial-coordinate-derivative,hir:actual-original-formal-taylor-algebra-embedding,hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-derivation-evaluation}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalTaylorDerivatives.originalTaylorAlgHom_coordinate_derivative}
\leanok
For every whole original analytic germ $g$ and every actual coordinate $i$,
\[
 T(D_i g)=\partial_iT(g)
\]
as equality of the entire genuine formal power series. In particular every
coefficient satisfies
$[x^\alpha]T(D_i g)=(\alpha_i+1)[x^{\alpha+e_i}]T(g)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First, for every complex polynomial $Q$, its genuine evaluated germ has
Taylor coefficient $[x^\alpha]Q$. To verify this without a convergence
assumption, subtract its canonical original Taylor polynomial through degree
$|\alpha|$. The genuine polynomial remainder is in the entire maximal
ideal power $\mathfrak m^{|\alpha|+1}$; original polynomial coefficient
detection forces equality of the coefficients at $\alpha$.

Now fix $\alpha$, put $k=|\alpha|$, and use the genuine original Taylor
polynomial $P$ of $g$ through degree $k+1$. Its whole analytic remainder is
in $\mathfrak m^{k+2}$. Actual analytic coordinate differentiation lowers
that whole power into $\mathfrak m^{k+1}$, so the actual coefficient at
$\alpha$ of $T(D_i g-D_i(P(x)))$ is zero. The preceding polynomial
derivative identity and the verified polynomial coefficient dictionary show
that $[x^\alpha]T(D_i g)=[x^\alpha]\partial_iP$.
The latter coefficient is
$(\alpha_i+1)[x^{\alpha+e_i}]P$.
The degree of $\alpha+e_i$ is $k+1$, so the genuine stabilized Taylor
coefficient dictionary makes this exactly
$(\alpha_i+1)[x^{\alpha+e_i}]T(g)$.
This is the literal coefficient formula for the actual formal derivative.
Equality at every actual exponent proves equality of the entire series.
\end{proof}
