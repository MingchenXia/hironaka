\section{The genuine original multivariate formal Taylor algebra embedding}

Let $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,0}$ with its ORIGINAL complex scalar
structure, and let $c_\alpha(g)$ be the actual stabilized coefficients
already constructed from genuine finite quotient polynomials. Define
$\mathcal T(g)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha(g)X^\alpha$ in the ACTUAL
multivariate formal power-series ring $\mathbb C[[X_1,\ldots,X_n]]$,
whose multiplication is its genuine coefficient convolution. The
zero-tangential-block coordinate identification retains the same original
ambient coordinates. This supplies a faithful convergent-to-formal
producer for \cite[\S7]{bierstone-milman97-invent-math}; completion
surjectivity, faithful flatness, formal division and transformed standard
bases remain separate obligations.

\begin{lemma}[Actual constants and original coordinates have their genuine formal values]
\label{hir:actual-original-formal-taylor-constants-coordinates}
\uses{hir:actual-original-canonical-finite-taylor-polynomial,hir:actual-original-taylor-coefficient-stabilization}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorPolynomial_constant,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorPolynomial_coordinate,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorSeries,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorSeries_coefficient,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorSeries_constant,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorSeries_coordinate}
\leanok
The actual formal series $\mathcal T(a)$ of an original complex constant
is the genuine constant formal series $a$, and the actual formal series
of EACH original coordinate germ is the corresponding formal variable
$X_i$. All coefficients are the SAME original complex numbers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A genuine constant polynomial has the exact original constant germ as its
evaluation and has no coefficient outside degree zero. The already proved
finite Taylor uniqueness therefore gives $T_k(a)=a$ for every $k$.
For an original coordinate, its genuine polynomial $X_i$ has the exact
same evaluation and degree one; uniqueness gives $T_k(x_i)=X_i$ whenever
$k\ge1$. To compute ANY original coefficient in either statement, use
coefficient stabilization at an actual truncation containing that exponent
and, for a coordinate, also degree one. These are precisely the genuine
constant and variable coefficients in the actual formal-series ring.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual whole original products give the genuine formal coefficient convolution]
\label{hir:actual-original-formal-taylor-genuine-convolution}
\uses{hir:actual-original-canonical-finite-taylor-polynomial,hir:actual-original-polynomial-low-coefficient-separation,hir:actual-original-taylor-coefficient-stabilization}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorCoefficientMap_mul,Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.originalTaylorSeries_mul}
\leanok
For ANY original numerators $g,h\in R$ and EVERY actual exponent $\alpha$,
\[
 c_\alpha(gh)=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}c_\beta(g)c_\gamma(h).
\]
Thus $\mathcal T(gh)=\mathcal T(g)\mathcal T(h)$ for the ACTUAL formal
power-series multiplication, rather than pointwise coefficient products.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $k=|\alpha|$ and $A=T_k(g)$, $B=T_k(h)$, $C=T_k(gh)$.
All three true original remainders lie in $\mathfrak m^{k+1}$. The literal
original ring identity
\[
 \operatorname{ev}(C)-\operatorname{ev}(A)\operatorname{ev}(B)
 =-(gh-\operatorname{ev}(C))
   +g(h-\operatorname{ev}(B))
   +(g-\operatorname{ev}(A))\operatorname{ev}(B)
\]
puts the actual evaluation of $C-AB$ in that SAME true maximal power.
Genuine lower coefficient separation gives $[X^\alpha]C=[X^\alpha](AB)$.
The genuine polynomial product formula expresses the right side as its
finite exponent antidiagonal sum. At each actual pair $\beta+\gamma=\alpha$,
nonnegative degree additivity gives $|\beta|,|\gamma|\le k$; true
coefficient stabilization therefore replaces both polynomial coefficients
by $c_\beta(g)$ and $c_\gamma(h)$. This proves the displayed convolution.
Actual formal coefficient extensionality and the genuine formal product
formula give the stated multiplication identity.
\end{proof}

transported or substituted on either ring.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual original maximal powers have precisely the genuine low-coefficient kernel]
\label{hir:actual-original-formal-taylor-maximal-power-kernel}
\uses{hir:actual-original-formal-taylor-algebra-embedding,hir:actual-original-canonical-finite-taylor-polynomial,hir:actual-original-polynomial-low-coefficient-separation}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalFormalTaylor.mem_original_maximal_pow_iff_formal_coefficients_zero}
\leanok
For every original $g\in R$ and $q\ge0$,
\[
 g\in\mathfrak m^q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [X^\alpha]\mathcal T(g)=0\quad\hbox{for all }|\alpha|<q.
\]
This is the actual original filtration dictionary with the genuine formal
coefficients, without a supplied coefficient or kernel certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $g\in\mathfrak m^q$ and $|\alpha|<q$. Put $k=|\alpha|$.
The actual power inclusion gives $g\in\mathfrak m^{k+1}$, and its true
Taylor remainder lies there too. Subtracting yields
$\operatorname{ev}(T_k(g))\in\mathfrak m^{k+1}$. Actual lower
coefficient separation kills its $\alpha$ coefficient, which is the
actual formal coefficient by stabilization. Conversely $q=0$ is the
whole original ring. For $q=k+1$, all lower coefficients of $T_k(g)$
vanish by the assumed actual formal coefficients and stabilization; all
higher ones vanish by its true support bound. Thus $T_k(g)=0$, and the
actual whole remainder gives $g\in\mathfrak m^q$ as required. No
identification with a completed-ideal quotient or flatness is inferred.
\end{proof}
