\section{Whole intersections under genuine analytic Taylor extension}

Let $R$ be the original holomorphic germ ring at the origin of $\mathbb C^n$,
$S=\mathbb C[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$, and $T:R\to S$ the existing original Taylor
homomorphism. Formal coefficients below need not be convergent.

\begin{theorem}[Taylor extension preserves entire original ideal intersections]
\label{hir:actual-formal-extension-whole-ideal-intersections}
\uses{hir:actual-formal-relations-original-analytic-syzygies}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualFormalIdealIntersections.map_inf_originalTaylorAlgHom}
\leanok
For any two original ideals $I,J\subseteq R$, including zero and unit ideals,
\[
 T(I\cap J)S= T(I)S\cap T(J)S.
\]
The statement concerns whole ideals and every formal element, including $n=0$;
finite generators, relation certificates and an intersection-compatibility
hypothesis are not supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Monotonicity gives the forward inclusion. For the reverse, take any formal
$F$ in both extensions. The original analytic ring is Noetherian, so choose
finite original generators $g_i$ of $I$ and $h_l$ of $J$. Membership in their
formal extensions supplies actual finite expressions
$F=\sum_i T(g_i)A_i=\sum_l T(h_l)D_l$.
Apply the preceding analytic syzygy producer to the concatenated original
family $(g_i,h_l)$ and the formal relation vector $(A_i,-D_l)$.
It supplies a finite formal family $B_j$ and original coefficients
$c_{ij},d_{lj}$ with
\[
 A_i=\sum_jT(c_{ij})B_j,\qquad
 \sum_i g_i c_{ij}+\sum_l h_l d_{lj}=0.
\]
Set $k_j=\sum_i g_i c_{ij}$. Then $k_j\in I$ and the second identity gives
$k_j=-\sum_l h_l d_{lj}\in J$. Substituting the first identity into the
expression for $F$ and interchanging the two finite sums gives
$F=\sum_j T(k_j)B_j$. Thus $F$ belongs to the extension of the actual
intersection. All sums may be empty; no convergence of $A_i,D_l,B_j$ is used.
\end{proof}
