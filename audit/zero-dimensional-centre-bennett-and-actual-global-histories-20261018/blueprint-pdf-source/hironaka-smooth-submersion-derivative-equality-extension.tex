\section{Entire derivative ideals under a genuine holomorphic submersion}

The input is the original holomorphic map $f:Y^m\to X^n$, a genuine
submersion at every point, and an original ideal of holomorphic germs.
Jacobian pivots, inverse coefficients and the entire ideal comparison are
constructed internally. This is a prerequisite for full canonical smooth
functoriality; higher invariant equality and blowup base change remain separate.

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-holomorphic-cramer-coefficients}
For an actual square matrix $A$ of scalar holomorphic germs with
$\det A(y)\ne0$, the actual coefficients
$B_{ij}=\det(A)^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(A)_{ij}$ are holomorphic at $y$.
Where $\det A\ne0$, the actual matrices satisfy $AB=1$.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.holomorphicAt_cramer_entry}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.cramer_matrix_rightInverse}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-holomorphic-jacobian-pivot}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The determinant is a finite signed sum of products of the original entries,
hence holomorphic. Its nonzero value makes its reciprocal holomorphic near
the original point. Each adjugate entry is the determinant of the original
matrix with one row replaced by a coordinate vector; each replacement entry
is either an original holomorphic entry or a constant. Thus every adjugate
entry, and its product with the reciprocal determinant, is holomorphic.
The algebraic identity $A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\det(A)1$, followed by
scalar multiplication by $\det(A)^{-1}$, gives $AB=1$ at each point where
the same actual determinant is nonzero. The empty matrix is included.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-actual-coordinate-derivative-recovery}
Let $e,e'$ be original charts through $f(y),y$ and let $a$ be holomorphic
at $f(y)$. Internally choose actual source columns $c_i$ of a nonzero
Jacobian pivot. Every target coordinate derivative has the genuine germ identity
\[
 (\partial_j^e a)\circ f
 =\sum_i B_{ij}\,\partial_{c_i}^{e'}(a\circ f).
\]
All the actual coefficients $B_{ij}$ are holomorphic at $y$.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.exists_coordinate_derivative_recovery}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-holomorphic-cramer-coefficients,hir:submersion-holomorphic-jacobian-pivot,hir:crossdim-chain-rule}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual submersion derivative has a right inverse, so the internally
constructed original-coordinate Jacobian minor is nonzero at $y$ and remains
nonzero near $y$. Keep these actual columns on this neighbourhood and construct
$B$ as above. The genuine chart chain rule, on a further neighbourhood where
the original charts and $a$ are valid, says
$\partial_{c_i}^{e'}(a\circ f)=\sum_k A_{ki}(\partial_k^e a)\circ f$.
Multiply by $B_{ij}$ and sum over $i$. Exchange the two finite sums and use
$\sum_i A_{ki}B_{ij}=\delta_{kj}$ from the actual matrix identity.
Only the $j$th target derivative remains. Every neighbourhood used is produced
from the original point and actual holomorphic germs; no inverse or chain
identity is supplied as an extra hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{hir:submersion-actual-directional-derivative-membership}
For every original ideal member $a$ at $f(y)$, every original chart $e$
through $f(y)$ and every target direction $v$, the actual germ
$(\partial_v^e a)\circ f$ belongs to $\mathcal D(f^*I)$ at $y$.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.pulled_chartDeriv_mem_derivIdeal}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-actual-coordinate-derivative-recovery,hir:derivative-ideal}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an actual source chart through $y$. The holomorphic germ $a\circ f$
is an original generator of $f^*I$, and every actual source coordinate
derivative of this generator belongs to $\mathcal D(f^*I)$ by its definition.
The preceding internal recovery identity puts each pulled target coordinate
derivative in that same entire ideal, since its coefficients are holomorphic.
Write the actual target vector as the finite sum of its coordinate vectors
with coefficients $v_j$. Complex linearity of the actual derivative gives
$\partial_v^e a=\sum_j v_j\partial_j^e a$ on the original chart.
Closure of the entire span under finite sums, constants and germ equality
puts its pullback in $\mathcal D(f^*I)$ as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{hir:submersion-entire-derivative-ideal-equality}
For every ideal $I$ of holomorphic germs on $X$ and every genuine holomorphic
submersion $f:Y\to X$, the entire original germ ideals satisfy
$f^*\mathcal D(I)=\mathcal D(f^*I)$.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.pullback_derivIdeal_le}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.derivIdeal_pullback_eq}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-actual-directional-derivative-membership,hir:crossdim-derivative-inclusion}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At any original point $y$, a member of $f^*\mathcal D(I)$ is a finite
holomorphic combination of pullbacks of actual members of $\mathcal D(I)$.
Each such actual member is itself a finite holomorphic combination of members
of $I$ and original-chart directional derivatives of members of $I$.
Pull this combination back along the original holomorphic map. Its coefficients
remain holomorphic; pulled ideal members belong to $f^*I$ and hence its
derivative ideal, and pulled directional derivatives belong to that derivative
ideal by the previous lemma. Transitivity of the actual entire finite spans
proves $f^*\mathcal D(I)\subseteq\mathcal D(f^*I)$.
The genuine cross-dimensional chain and product rules already prove the
opposite inclusion for every holomorphic map. Apply both inclusions at every
point and every scalar germ and use extensionality of the original germ ideal.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{hir:submersion-entire-iterated-derivative-equality}
For every integer $r\ge0$, the actual entire iterated ideals satisfy
$f^*\mathcal D^r(I)=\mathcal D^r(f^*I)$.
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionDerivativeEquality.iterDerivIdeal_pullback_eq}
\leanok
\uses{hir:submersion-entire-derivative-ideal-equality,hir:derivative-ideal}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $r$. The zero case is the original ideal itself, so reflexivity
suffices. Every member of the actual iterated derivative ideal is holomorphic,
by the defining finite holomorphic span (and the original hypothesis for $r=0$).
Apply the entire first derivative equality to this actual iterated ideal.
Replace its pullback using the induction hypothesis and unfold the successor
definition of the original iteration. This is equality of all members of the
entire original ideals at all points; no selected generator space is substituted.
\end{proof}
