\section{Actual monomial ordering under arbitrary dimensional smooth pullback}

For a genuine holomorphic submersion $f:Y^m\to X^n$, the existing whole
divisor dictionary identifies every original index and exponent. Its
monomial key still uses the genuine ambient dimension: a key with support
$K$ starts with $n-|K|$ on $X$ and $m-|K|$ on $Y$. We prove that this
dimension change preserves and reflects the complete original ordering.
All index cardinality bounds are produced from the actual SNC coordinates.
No equality of keys in different dimensions is asserted. Higher invariants,
canonical blowup base change and complete smooth functoriality remain open.

\begin{lemma}[Actual submersion dimension inequality]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-dimension}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.dimension_le_of_submersionAt}
A genuine submersion at an actual point of $Y^m$ internally gives $n\le m$.
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual submersion coordinates give a continuous complex linear
isomorphism $L:\mathbb C^m\simeq\mathbb C^n\times F$. Compose its linear
map with first projection. For every $v$, $L^{-1}(v,0)$ is a genuine
preimage, so this actual linear map is surjective. The finite-rank
inequality for this map gives $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C^n\le
\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C^m$. Their genuine ranks are $n$ and $m$.
There is no ambient nonemptiness assumption: the actual point supplies it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Entire original key ordering]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-key-order}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.key_lt_key_dimension_iff, Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.key_le_key_dimension_iff}
If $|K|,|L|\le n\le m$, then for arbitrary actual exponent functions $a,b$
the strict and weak comparisons of original keys of $K,L$ agree in
dimensions $n$ and $m$.
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the complete original lexicographic key as
$(n-|K|,\sum_Ka,K)$, with the last entry colexicographically ordered.
Because both cardinalities are at most $n$, subtraction does not truncate.
Thus the strict comparison, and equality, of the first coordinates agree
after replacing $n$ by $m$. The actual exponent sums and colex entries
remain the same. Expanding the two original lexicographic comparisons
proves strict equivalence. Weak comparison is the negation of the reverse
strict comparison in the actual linear order, which proves the weak case.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Same genuine top subset in both dimensions]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-common-top}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.exists_common_top_dimensions}
For a genuine finite member set $S$ with $|S|\le n\le m$ and
$d\le\sum_Sa$, one actual feasible subset attains the original invariant
in both dimensions and dominates every actual feasible key in both.
\uses{hir:submersion-monomial-key-order}
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the actual maximizing subset of the original finite feasible family
in dimension $n$. Every feasible subset is contained in $S$, so the
genuine cardinality bounds needed by the preceding lemma hold. The whole
family of key inequalities therefore transports to dimension $m$.
Taking the actual maximum of its key image shows that the same subset
attains the dimension-$m$ invariant. Feasibility, sums and colex tie
breaking are unchanged, and original key injectivity makes the subset
unique in either dimension. No maximizing subset is supplied by a caller.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Whole invariant ordering, including the bottom value]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-invariant-order}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.inv_le_inv_dimension_iff, Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.inv_eq_inv_dimension_iff}
For two arbitrary real member sets of cardinality at most $n\le m$, with
arbitrary exponent functions and marks, the entire original invariants
have the same weak comparisons and equality in both dimensions.
\uses{hir:submersion-monomial-common-top}
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If either feasible family is empty, the original invariant is literally
bottom, in both dimensions. Empty families do not depend on dimension;
the corresponding bottom/nonbottom comparisons therefore agree. If both
are nonempty, internally take their actual common top subsets from the
preceding lemma. Rewrite the actual maxima as their keys and use the
whole key-order equivalence. Equality follows by the two weak comparisons.
Zero marks, zero dimensions, empty member sets and bottom values are
included in the same argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual original gamma ordering under smooth pullback]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-gamma-order}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.gamma_pullback_le_iff, Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.gamma_pullback_eq_iff}
For an actual coherent ideal and actual SNC boundary on $X$, the original
gamma values of its genuine pullback at any two actual points of $Y$
have exactly the original gamma ordering and equality at their images.
\uses{hir:submersion-monomial-dimension, hir:submersion-monomial-invariant-order}
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the actual submersion point to produce $n\le m$. The genuine SNC
coordinate family injects the actual member indices at each image point
into distinct coordinate hyperplanes, and hence internally bounds both
cardinalities by $n$. The previously proved whole divisor dictionary
identifies all original member indices and the entire exponent function
of the pullback. Apply the preceding ordering lemma to these exact
original data. This proves every comparison and equality without assuming
any pullback value, member-cardinality or ordering certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Literal original marked maximum locus]
\label{hir:submersion-monomial-original-max-locus}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionMonomialOrdering.maxLocus_gamma_markedPullback_eq}
For a surjective genuine holomorphic submersion and an actual coherent
marked ideal whose marked ambient locus is the whole manifold, with genuine SNC boundary, the original gamma maximum locus
on its actual controlled cosupport pulls back to the literal maximum
locus of the original marked pullback.
\uses{hir:submersion-monomial-gamma-order}
\leanok
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The genuine whole marked ambient locus remains whole under preimage.
Whole original ambient order preservation identifies the actual pulled-back
cosupport with the preimage of the original cosupport. If an image point
is in the original maximum locus, every point of the pulled cosupport
has a smaller or equal gamma by the complete ordering equivalence. In
the reverse direction, lift every original cosupport point using actual
surjectivity. Its lift lies in the actual pulled cosupport, and the same
ordering equivalence gives the required original inequality. Membership
and all inequalities give equality of the entire literal sets, including
empty cosupports and unattained maxima. Surjectivity is real geometry;
no maximum certificate is an input. For a nonsurjective submersion, the
pointwise comparison statement remains valid without this global claim.
\end{proof}
