\section{Regrouping actual branches without losing original boundary labels}
\label{sec:nc-labelled-boundary-regrouping}

The groups in this chapter are explicit finite sets of original list
positions. They supply only combinatorial provenance, not any desired
geometric or history conclusion. An empty group remains an empty
labelled position. Retained or born members can be kept individually by
using their singleton position sets.

\begin{theorem}[Disjoint groups of an actual labelled NC boundary remain labelled NC]
\label{hir:labelled-nc-actual-disjoint-regrouping}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundaryRegrouping.isNormalCrossingsBoundary_grouped}
\leanok
Let $F$ be an actual labelled NC boundary, and let $G_i$ be pairwise
disjoint finite sets of positions in $F$, indexed by a finite ordered
set. Form the entire ordered list
\[
                   H_i=\bigcup_{j\in G_i}F_j.
\]
This list, with each group and empty position retained, is labelled NC.
The conclusion includes every group in the given order; the groups
need not cover all positions of $F$.
\uses{hir:labelled-normal-crossings-boundary}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $H_i$ is closed, being a finite union of the original closed
members. At $x$, take the common actual NC coordinates of $F$, with
pairwise disjoint coordinate branch sets $S_j$. Give the new position
$i$ the coordinate set $T_i=\bigcup_{j\in G_i}S_j$. Its local union
equation follows in both directions: a point belongs to $H_i$ exactly
when it belongs to some $F_j$ with $j\in G_i$, exactly when a
coordinate in the corresponding $S_j$ vanishes, exactly when a
coordinate in $T_i$ vanishes.

For distinct new positions $i,k$, suppose a coordinate belonged to both
$T_i,T_k$. There would be $j\in G_i$ and $l\in G_k$ with that
coordinate in both $S_j,S_l$. The disjointness of $G_i,G_k$ implies
$j\ne l$, whereas the original labelled NC condition says that
$S_j,S_l$ are disjoint. This contradiction proves the required
pairwise disjointness of the $T_i$. Thus the very same actual chart
gives all labelled equations and disjoint branch sets for the new
entire ordered list.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Actual SNC branches can be assembled into their original labels]
\label{hir:actual-snc-branches-supply-labelled-nc-groups}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundaryRegrouping.isNormalCrossingsBoundary_grouped_of_snc}
\leanok
If the original finite branch list $F$ is SNC, the ordered finite unions
of any pairwise disjoint position groups are labelled NC. In
particular an original member may group several actual branches,
while any retained or born positions can be kept as singleton groups.
\uses{hir:labelled-nc-actual-disjoint-regrouping,hir:snc-supplies-labelled-nc}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Actual SNC geometry supplies labelled NC geometry for the unchanged
whole list $F$. Apply the disjoint regrouping theorem to that very
list and the given position groups. Its actual finite unions are
exactly the required original labelled members. Singleton groups
return their original member, and empty groups return the empty set;
both statements follow directly from finite union membership.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An injective selection retains labelled NC geometry]
\label{hir:labelled-nc-injective-original-position-selection}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundaryRegrouping.isNormalCrossingsBoundary_reindex}
\leanok
Every injective ordered selection of positions from an actual labelled
NC boundary is labelled NC, with each selected member unchanged.
This includes permutation of its entire ordered index set.
\uses{hir:labelled-nc-actual-disjoint-regrouping}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the selection $\sigma$, use the singleton position group
$G_i=\{\sigma(i)\}$. If two distinct positions shared a group element,
their images under $\sigma$ would be equal, contradicting
injectivity. The groups are therefore pairwise disjoint. Apply the
regrouping theorem. Each resulting finite union over its singleton is
exactly the selected original member, so the entire resulting list
is the stated ordered selection, including any selected empty member.
\end{proof}
