\section{Normal crossings with the original boundary labels retained}
\label{sec:nc-labelled-boundary}

An original boundary member may have several distinct local branches.
The labels of the original finite list are retained, including empty
members. The local condition below permits such selfcrossings, while
requiring that two different labels have no common local branch. It
extends the simple normal crossings boundary convention of
Bierstone--Milman (2008), Definition 2.1, by allowing several coordinate hyperplanes
in a single labelled member. The statements concern actual local
geometry; they do not construct a resolution history or assert its
centre admissibility.

\begin{definition}[A labelled normal crossings boundary]
\label{hir:labelled-normal-crossings-boundary}
\source{bierstone-milman08-functoriality}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundary.IsNormalCrossingsBoundary}
\leanok
A finite ordered list $B=(H_i)$ is a normal crossings boundary if every
$H_i$ is closed and, at every $x$, there are centred holomorphic
coordinates and pairwise disjoint finite coordinate sets $S_i$ such that
on that same coordinate neighbourhood
\[
 H_i=\bigcup_{a\in S_i}\{z_a=0\}.
\]
The equality holds for every original position $i$, including positions
whose member does not contain $x$; their set $S_i$ is empty.
\uses{hir:general-normal-crossings-carrier,hir:snc}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The actual union is a normal crossings carrier]
\label{hir:labelled-nc-union-carrier}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundary.isNormalCrossingsCarrier_union}
\leanok
If $B$ is a labelled normal crossings boundary, then its actual union
$H=\bigcup_iH_i$ is a normal crossings carrier. In any of the common
coordinates in the definition, its coordinate branch set is exactly
$S=\bigcup_iS_i$.
\uses{hir:labelled-normal-crossings-boundary,hir:general-normal-crossings-carrier}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The union is closed because the list is finite and all its members are
closed. Fix the common coordinates at $x$. A point belongs to the union
if and only if it belongs to some $H_i$, if and only if some coordinate
indexed by some $S_i$ vanishes. This is exactly the vanishing of one of
the coordinates indexed by the finite union $S$. The same coordinates
are centred at $x$ and have the required domain, so they give the
asserted normal crossings carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A single label retains arbitrary normal crossings]
\label{hir:labelled-nc-singleton-equivalence}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundary.isNormalCrossingsBoundary_singleton_iff}
\leanok
The one-member boundary $[H]$ has labelled normal crossings if and only
if $H$ is a normal crossings carrier. Thus the labelled condition does
not require each original member to be smooth.
\uses{hir:labelled-nc-union-carrier,hir:general-normal-crossings-carrier}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The union of the one-member list is exactly $H$, so one implication
follows from the union lemma. Conversely, take the actual centred NC
coordinates of $H$ and give the unique label its entire coordinate
branch set. There are no two distinct positions, so pairwise
disjointness is vacuous. The same local equality and closedness give
the required labelled boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Every simple normal crossings boundary has labelled normal crossings]
\label{hir:snc-supplies-labelled-nc}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundary.isNormalCrossingsBoundary_of_snc}
\leanok
Every actual SNC list, with its order and all empty positions unchanged,
is a labelled normal crossings boundary.
\uses{hir:labelled-normal-crossings-boundary,hir:snc}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $x$, take the actual common SNC coordinates and their coordinate
sets $C_i$. For every member not containing $x$, remove that closed
member from the neighbourhood. The finite intersection of these open
complements is an open neighbourhood of $x$. Restrict the same
holomorphic coordinate equivalence to its inverse image in the chart;
its centre and its coordinate functions are unchanged. Put $S_i=C_i$
for members through $x$ and $S_i=\varnothing$ for all others.

For a member through $x$, $C_i$ has exactly one element, so its
coordinate-subspace equation is precisely the union-of-hyperplanes
equation for $S_i$. For an absent member, its trace on the restricted
neighbourhood and the union indexed by its empty $S_i$ are both empty.
Two nonempty $S_i,S_j$ are singletons. If they shared a coordinate they
would be equal, contradicting the injectivity of the actual SNC
coordinate family for distinct through positions. All other pairs
contain an empty set. This proves the required pairwise disjointness
and every labelled local equality on the same actual neighbourhood.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic total branch count one makes the original list simple]
\label{hir:labelled-nc-count-one-snc}
\lean{TranscendentalNakamaye.Pending.HironakaNormalCrossingsLabelledBoundary.isSNCDivisor_of_branchMultiplicity_le_one}
\leanok
Let $B$ be a labelled NC boundary and $H$ its actual union. If the
intrinsic branch multiplicity of $H$ is at most one at every point,
then the entire original list $B$ is an SNC boundary.
\uses{hir:labelled-nc-union-carrier,hir:general-nc-exact-nearby-branch-count,hir:snc}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and the actual labelled NC coordinates. Their total coordinate
branch set is $S=\bigcup_iS_i$, by the union lemma. The actual
coordinate formula for intrinsic branch multiplicity gives
$|S|\le1$. If $x\in H_i$, the labelled local equality at the centre
gives an element of $S_i$. Since $S_i\subset S$, it follows that
$|S_i|=1$. For this singleton, the union-of-hyperplanes equation equals
its coordinate-subspace equation, giving the required common SNC
coordinates for every through member. If two through positions had
equal coordinate sets, those equal nonempty sets would contradict
their pairwise disjointness unless the positions were identical.
Hence the coordinate assignment is injective on the through positions.
Closedness was retained from the original boundary. These are exactly
the SNC conditions for the unchanged entire list $B$.
\end{proof}
