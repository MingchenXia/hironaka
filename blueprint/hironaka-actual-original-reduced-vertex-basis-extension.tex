\begin{theorem}[The actual original ideal has its literal canonical reduced analytic basis]
\label{hir:actual-original-canonical-reduced-vertex-basis}
\uses{hir:actual-original-ideal-reduced-vertex-convergence,hir:actual-convergent-original-taylor-realization,hir:actual-formal-extension-whole-original-ideal-contraction,hir:actual-reduced-formal-standard-basis-generation}

\leanok
For every whole original holomorphic germ ideal $J$, put $I=\widehat J$
and let $V$ be its actual finite degree-lex vertex set. There is an internally
constructed family of actual original holomorphic germs $G_a$ such that
its ORIGINAL Taylor image is exactly the canonical whole reduced vertex
series $F_a$ of $I$, every $G_a$ belongs to $J$, and $J=(G_a:a\in V)$.
No analytic prepared family or convergence certificate is an input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original whole ideal alone internally produces a common positive mass
polyradius for every actual canonical reduced vertex series. Realize each
unchanged series by its actual holomorphic evaluation germ. The proved
inverse Taylor theorem gives $\operatorname{Taylor}(G_a)=F_a$ exactly.
Since $F_a\in\widehat J$, whole original ideal contraction gives $G_a\in J$.
The Taylor extension of the original ideal generated by the $G_a$ is
precisely the formal ideal generated by their actual images $F_a$.
Canonical reduced formal generation identifies this with $\widehat J$.
Injectivity of extension on all whole original ideals therefore gives
$(G_a)=J$. The construction also applies to the empty vertex family.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Entire original analytic division by that same canonical reduced family]
\label{hir:actual-original-canonical-reduced-analytic-division}

\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalReducedVertexBasis.exists_original_reduced_vertex_division}
\leanok

holomorphic germ $g$, there are actual holomorphic quotient germs $h_a$
and an actual holomorphic remainder germ $s$ such that
\[
 g=\sum_{a\in V}G_a h_a+s,

\]
Every actual Taylor coefficient of $h_a$ outside its genuine diagram-owner
region is zero. All holomorphic quotients and remainder are outputs;
no convergence, prepared-family or division identity is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite family of actual original germs consisting of $g$ and the fixed
$G_a$ has an internally produced common positive Taylor coefficient radius.
Consequently each unchanged reduced formal generator has a summable tail
after subtracting its single monic leading monomial. The genuine small-radius
construction produces a common smaller positive radius with the actual tail
operator norm strictly below one. At that radius the unchanged Taylor series
of $g$ remains summable. Genuine complete-space Neumann division therefore
produces actual summable formal quotients and the canonical normal remainder,
with the whole formal division identity and the genuine owner support.
Realize every quotient and remainder by its actual evaluated holomorphic
germ, retaining every Taylor coefficient by the inverse theorem. Taylor
injectivity applied to the entire finite sum gives the asserted original
analytic identity, using the already proved exact Taylor images of the SAME
fixed $G_a$. The literal quotient Taylor identities retain their owner support.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The original whole quotient has a genuine canonical analytic normal projection]
\label{hir:actual-original-analytic-normal-projection}

normal projection $R_J$, whose original Taylor image is the canonical formal

It is idempotent and all its Taylor coefficients on the entire initial diagram
vanish. These properties uniquely determine the original normal representative
in each whole original ideal class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the actual holomorphic remainder supplied by the preceding division.
Its Taylor image is the uniquely specified formal normal remainder, so
Taylor injectivity makes it independent of every analytic radius or quotient
choice. Formal normal reduction preserves addition and complex scalar
multiplication; Taylor injectivity transfers both identities to the original
holomorphic representatives and constructs the asserted complex linear map.
A remainder vanishes exactly when its Taylor image vanishes, equivalently
when the original Taylor series lies in $\widehat J$. Whole original ideal
contraction makes this equivalent to membership in $J$, proving the kernel
identity. Finally, the original Taylor image of $g-R_Jg$ is the formal series
minus its canonical normal representative, which lies in $\widehat J$.
Apply whole ideal contraction again to obtain $g-R_Jg\in J$. Its whole
formal normal image vanishes on the entire initial diagram. Formal
idempotence transfers through Taylor injectivity. If another actual germ
is in the same whole ideal class and has the same diagram vanishing,
formal normal representative uniqueness identifies its entire Taylor
series with that of $R_Jg$, and Taylor injectivity identifies the original germs.
\end{proof}
