\section{The actual initial sharp maximum and the original normal-crossings locus}

The ambient complex dimension is positive. The original ordered boundary has
simple normal crossings, the carrier is a closed smooth submanifold, and its
complement of the whole boundary is dense. Original boundary positions remain
the factors of the chronological product; its initial auxiliary boundary is
empty. The results below compare these independently defined initial objects.
They do not replace the entire sharp bad locus by the first defect.

\begin{lemma}[The entire initial germ quotient and proper-class count]
\label{hir:sharp-actual-initial-germ-quotient-source}
\uses{hir:sharp-actual-chronological-distinct-defect-objects}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_germ_relation_iff_source_trace,Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_distinct_count_eq_source}
At every ambient point, equality of two entire initial chronological member
ideals is equivalent to equality of the corresponding original restricted
trace ideals. Their proper-position sets and the cardinalities of their
actual proper germ-class images therefore agree. This includes points outside
the carrier, unit factors, and coincident proper germ classes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An initial chronological member is the carrier restriction of the vanishing
ideal of its original boundary member. Membership of a function in this
restricted ideal means that it is holomorphic and belongs to the original
restricted trace span. Thus equality of whole member predicates implies trace
span equivalence for every holomorphic function. Conversely, trace span
equivalence preserves the conjunction with holomorphy and hence the entire
member predicate for every function. For the constant function one,
holomorphy always holds, so the two proper-position predicates agree.
The identity on the original finite position set consequently induces a
bijection of the two genuine germ quotients. It maps the image of the proper
positions in one quotient onto the corresponding image in the other. These
finite images have equal cardinality.
\leanok
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The whole initial product is the restriction of the original product]
\label{hir:sharp-actual-initial-product-source-restriction}
\uses{hir:sharp-original-chronological-full-product-geometry-order-sum}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_product_eq_restrictOn_source,Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.source_product_restrictEquiv_actual_initial}
The entire initial chronological product equals the carrier restriction of the
entire original ordered member product. The two marking-one product records
are restricted equivalent, with exactly the same carrier and empty auxiliary
boundary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply finite relative-product normalization to the ordered list of original
vanishing ideals. Coherence of each member follows from the original smooth
SNC member geometry. The normalized relative product of their carrier
restrictions is exactly the carrier restriction of their ambient product.
At the identity history this is the literal chronological member list,
including every original position. Restriction to the carrier is equivalent
to the original ideal in both restricted-span directions. Their carrier,
empty auxiliary boundary and marking one coincide, yielding restricted
equivalence of the full records.
\leanok
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The literal actual initial defect is the original first defect]
\label{hir:sharp-actual-initial-defect-source-equality}
\uses{hir:sharp-actual-initial-germ-quotient-source,hir:sharp-actual-initial-product-source-restriction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_defect_eq_source_first,Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_defect_subset_sharp_bad}
The actual chronological distinct defect at the identity history equals the
original first defect, in every carrier dimension. It is contained in the
original sharp bad locus and therefore avoids every original smooth
normal-crossings point, including good boundary intersections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both defect definitions require membership in the original carrier. At such
a point their proper-class counts agree by the first lemma. Their product
orders agree by the second lemma and invariance of the ordinary trace order
under carrier restriction of a coherent nonzero ideal. Hence the strict
inequalities defining the two defects are identical. Outside the carrier,
neither defect has a point. The proved original first-defect inclusion in the
sharp bad locus now gives the asserted containment without a dimension-one
argument.
\leanok
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Equality of the entire initial invariant and its actual maximum]
\label{hir:sharp-actual-initial-full-maximum-source-equality}
\uses{hir:sharp-actual-initial-defect-source-equality,hir:sharp-actual-initial-product-source-restriction,hir:sharp-original-noncompact-current-product-domain}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.source_full_value_eq_actual_initial_on_first_defect,Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.initial_actual_dynamic_maximum_eq_source}
Suppose also that the original carrier has fixed positive pure dimension
$d+1\leq n$. On the same first defect, every entry of the full original
product invariant equals the corresponding entry of the full actual initial
product invariant. Their maximum loci on the same first defect are equal.
No maximum-attainment or compactness assumption is needed for these
equalities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Original geometry supplies both product records as inputs of the invariant
domain. The preceding restricted equivalence preserves their full invariant
at the identity history. A first-defect point has product order strictly
greater than a nonnegative proper-class count, hence at least one, so it lies
in the marking-one cosupport required by this invariant comparison. All full
values on the same defect therefore agree. A point bounds every full value
of one record on this set if and only if it bounds every value of the other.
Combining this with the actual-defect equality proves equality of their
entire maximum loci, including the case where both are empty.
\leanok
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual initial dynamic maximum has a whole-good-preserving blowup]
\label{hir:sharp-actual-initial-dynamic-whole-good-blowup}
\uses{hir:sharp-actual-initial-full-maximum-source-equality,hir:sharp-actual-initial-defect-source-equality}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSharpActualInitialFullCompatibility.exists_actual_initial_dynamic_sharp_blowup}
If the original carrier is compact of the stated positive pure dimension and
the actual initial distinct defect is nonempty, there is a genuine analytic
blowup of its entire actual full dynamic maximum. The same nonempty centre
is admissible for the original marking-one product, the actual initial
product, and the embedded carrier with the entire original ordered boundary.
It belongs to the original sharp bad locus. The blowup is proper and is an
isomorphism over every original good point. Its strict carrier is smooth and
the entire original boundary after strict transform, together with the new
exceptional member, has simple normal crossings.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Transfer nonemptiness of the actual defect to the identical original first
defect. The original first-defect blowup producer supplies its genuine entire
maximum centre, original-product and embedded-boundary admissibility, the
analytic blowup, properness, whole-good isomorphism, smooth strict carrier,
and the full transformed SNC list. Restricted equivalence gives admissibility
for the actual initial product as well. The preceding full maximum equality
identifies this same already constructed centre with the actual dynamic
maximum, so neither its geometry nor the blowup is an assumed certificate.
This constructs an actual first sharp step. Full-history comparison, strict
decrease of the modified invariant, recursive intersection phases and a
general terminal sharp endpoint remain separate obligations.
\leanok
\end{proof}
