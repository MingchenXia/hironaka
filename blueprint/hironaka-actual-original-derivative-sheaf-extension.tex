\section{The original chart-free derivative sheaf has the actual differential stalk}

Let $\mathfrak a$ be an original coherent analytic ideal sheaf on the actual
complex model space, and let $I$ be its entire original ideal at the origin.
The original sheaf $\mathcal D(\mathfrak a)$ is generated by members of
$\mathfrak a$ and derivatives in every chart of its specified atlas and every
constant chart direction. Its definition is retained, including its actual
finite holomorphic combination witnesses.

\begin{theorem}[The whole original derivative-sheaf stalk is the actual differential ideal]
\label{hir:actual-original-derivative-sheaf-whole-stalk}
\uses{hir:derivative-ideal,hir:relative-normal-deformation-whole-origin-ideal,hir:actual-original-differential-ideal-definition}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalDerivativeSheaf.originalOriginIdeal_derivIdeal}
\leanok
The entire original origin ideal of $\mathcal D(\mathfrak a)$ equals
$\mathcal D_R(I)$, with the genuine analytic coordinate derivatives.
Only the original coherent sheaf is supplied; no derivative-stalk dictionary
or output equality certificate is an input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Coherence gives the actual whole membership dictionary for $I$ and, by the
original derivative coherence theorem, for the derivative sheaf. Every
original member is a derivative-sheaf member, giving the inclusion of $I$.
For an arbitrary $g\in I$, choose an actual holomorphic representative $f$.
The dictionary puts $f$ in the original sheaf. Its genuine coordinate
derivative is a defining derivative-sheaf generator in the actual identity
chart. Passing to its true entire germ puts every $D_i g$ in the origin
ideal of the derivative sheaf. Thus $\mathcal D_R(I)$ lies in that ideal.

For the reverse inclusion, take a true derivative-sheaf member and retain
its actual finite holomorphic combination witness. An original generator
has germ in $I$. For a derivative generator, every chart of the model's
specified identity atlas is literally the identity. Expand its actual
constant direction $v$ in the finite standard coordinate basis. The
continuous-linear differential gives the whole function identity
$D_v f=\sum_j v_jD_jf$. Each coordinate direction is the actual normal
direction with zero tangent dimension. Thus each term's germ lies in
$\mathcal D_R(I)$, as does the whole directional derivative. Finally the
actual finite holomorphic combination becomes a finite combination in the
whole germ ring, so its germ lies in that ideal. The two inclusions prove
the equality of the entire stalk ideals.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every original iterated derivative-sheaf stalk]
\label{hir:actual-original-all-derivative-sheaf-stalks}
\uses{hir:actual-original-derivative-sheaf-whole-stalk,hir:derivative-ideal-generators,hir:actual-original-iterated-differential-ideal-definition}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalDerivativeSheaf.originalOriginIdeal_iterDerivIdeal}
\leanok
For every $q\in\mathbb N$, the entire origin ideal of the original
$\mathcal D^q(\mathfrak a)$ is exactly $\mathcal D_R^q(I)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At zero the two ideals are identical. At a successor apply the preceding
whole-stalk theorem to the actual original $\mathcal D^q(\mathfrak a)$,
whose coherence follows internally by the original iterated derivative
coherence theorem. The induction hypothesis identifies its entire origin
ideal with $\mathcal D_R^q(I)$; the two genuine recursive successors then
agree. This uses every actual original derivative sheaf, rather than a
new replacement sheaf chosen to satisfy the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole original derivative sheaves under actual formal Taylor extension]
\label{hir:actual-original-derivative-sheaf-taylor-extension}
\uses{hir:actual-original-all-derivative-sheaf-stalks,hir:actual-original-all-differential-closure-extension}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualOriginalDerivativeSheaf.map_originalOriginIdeal_iterDerivIdeal}
\leanok
For every $q$, the Taylor extension of the entire original origin ideal
of $\mathcal D^q(\mathfrak a)$ equals $\mathcal D_S^q(T(I)S)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Replace the entire origin ideal on the left by the preceding original
iterated-stalk equality, and apply the proved whole finite differential
closure theorem for the same genuine original Taylor homomorphism. It
includes arbitrary formal members and arbitrary formal coefficients.
\end{proof}
