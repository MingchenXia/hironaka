\section{True smooth products reflect the entire original HS profile}

The product here is exactly the original holomorphic coordinate projection
$\mathbb C^{d+t}\to\mathbb C^d$ and its whole ideal extension. Its HS is the
existing local-ring function, computed by the genuine mixed-power quotient.

\begin{lemma}[The unique degree-zero original normal monomial]
\label{hir:actual-product-unique-degree-zero-monomial}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-finite-exponents}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualNormalExponentDegree_eq_zero_iff,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductZeroIndex,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductZeroIndex_degree}
\leanok
The actual normal exponent degree is zero precisely for the zero exponent.
In the genuine finite exponent family for order $\ell+1$, this zero exponent
is the unique degree-zero index. Every other index has positive degree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every original nonnegative exponent entry is at most their finite sum.
If the sum is zero, each entry is therefore zero, so the entire exponent
is zero; conversely the zero exponent has zero sum. The zero exponent has
degree less than $\ell+1$ and hence defines an actual index. Equality of
whole exponents is equality of these subtype indices. Consequently every
other original index has nonzero, and thus positive, degree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[True product HS comparison at the first differing order]
\label{hir:actual-product-hs-first-difference-comparison}
\uses{hir:actual-product-unique-degree-zero-monomial,hir:actual-product-original-ambient-hs-convolution}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHSRemainder,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHS_zeroTerm_add_remainder,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHSRemainder_eq_of_lower_orders,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHS_comparison_of_lower_orders}
\leanok
For ANY two whole original ideals $I,K\subset\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^d,0}$,
if their actual HS values agree at all orders less than $\ell$, then
comparison of their genuine product HS at order $\ell$ is exactly
comparison of their original HS at that order, for equality, strict order
and weak order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split each genuine convolution sum into its unique zero-exponent term
and the finite sum over all other original indices. The zero term is
exactly $H_I(\ell)$, respectively $H_K(\ell)$. At every other index of
degree $q>0$, the coefficient is the existing original value at order
$\ell-q<\ell$; hence these two terms agree by the hypothesis. Their entire
finite remainder sums are therefore the same actual natural number $c$.
The two product values are $H_I(\ell)+c$ and $H_K(\ell)+c$. Cancellation
of addition in the natural numbers proves each of the three comparisons.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every original finite prefix and the whole original HS profile are reflected by the true smooth product]
\label{hir:actual-product-entire-hs-profile-reflection}
\uses{hir:actual-product-hs-first-difference-comparison}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHS_prefix_eq_iff,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductHSProfileReflection.actualProductHS_entire_profile_eq_iff}
\leanok
For every cutoff $N$, equality of the genuine product HS at all orders
less than $N$ is equivalent to equality of the original HS at all such
orders. In particular the entire genuine product profiles agree at all
orders if and only if the entire ORIGINAL profiles agree at all orders.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the forward finite-prefix assertion, use strong induction on an
original order $\ell<N$. All smaller original values agree by the
induction hypothesis; the preceding equality comparison therefore
recovers equality at $\ell$ from the assumed genuine product equality.
For the reverse assertion, each original convolution argument
$\ell-|\alpha|$ is at most $\ell<N$, so every actual term agrees and the
finite sums agree. For entire profiles, apply the recovered finite-prefix
assertion with cutoff $\ell+1$ at each arbitrary original order $\ell$;
the reverse direction follows by the same actual convolution formula.
These arguments include zero free-coordinate dimension and make no
HS-cancellation or comparison assumption about the desired product.
\end{proof}
