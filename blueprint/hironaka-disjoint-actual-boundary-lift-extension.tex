\section{Actual product extension of an entire labelled boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Whole labelled normal crossings under the actual zero section]
\label{hir:disjoint-product-whole-boundary-lift}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDisjointActualBoundaryLift.hasNormalCrossings_zeroSection}
\leanok
Let $D\subset\mathbb C^d$ have normal crossings with the ordered list
$B=[B_i]$. Write $p:\mathbb C^{d+t}\to\mathbb C^d$ for the actual first-coordinate
projection and $s$ for its zero section. Then $s(D)$ has normal crossings with
the ENTIRE ordered list $[p^{-1}B_i]$, retaining every original position,
including empty members. No ambient normal-crossings certificate is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $s(x)\in s(D)$. Unpack the intrinsic normal-crossings coordinates at
$x$: a genuine local biholomorphism $\Phi$ with true open source and target,
centered at zero, a coordinate mask $S_D$ for $D$, and singleton masks $S_i$
for every member through $x$. Use the existing actual product extension
$F(z)=s(\Phi(pz))+z-s(pz)$, whose inverse is the identical expression with
$\Phi^{-1}$. Its source and target are the inverse images of the actual
source and target of $\Phi$, hence are genuinely open. The inverse equations
follow from $ps=\mathrm{id}$; this uses no product-neighbourhood assertion
about an arbitrary ambient source open.

For each tangent index $j$, $F(z)_{j}=\Phi(pz)_j$; every normal coordinate
is unchanged. Consequently a lifted intrinsic mask is zero exactly when
its original mask is zero after $\Phi p$. The image centre is cut out by
the lifted $S_D$ AND every normal coordinate. Indeed these normal equations
put $z=s(pz)$, and then the original centre equation is exactly $pz\in D$.
Every boundary member is cut out by its lifted singleton mask; casting
indices is injective, so its cardinality remains one. Apply these formulas
to the same $F$ for ALL original positions. This is precisely the actual
ambient normal-crossings definition, not a germwise choice of unrelated
coordinates for individual boundary members.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Generic embedded consumer: exact remaining implementation route.}
The intended consumer has a closed regular native submanifold $N\subset X$,
an entire ordered SNC divisor $E$ transverse to $N$, and an intrinsic closed
regular centre $D\subset N$ having normal crossings with the literal ordered
trace $[\operatorname{val}^{-1}E_i]$. Its conclusion must construct normal
crossings of $\operatorname{val}(D)$ with ALL $E$, then its genuine ambient
blowup and comparison with a prescribed intrinsic blowup. This paragraph is
a source-facing proof route, not an accepted theorem or additional input.

At each centre point, unpack transversality to get ONE ambient coordinate
system in which $N$ is a coordinate plane and ALL active $E_i$ are tangent
coordinate hyperplanes. Permute normal indices last. Restriction gives an
actual intrinsic chart of the existing native atlas. Read the intrinsic
$D$-and-trace chart relative to this chart and extend THAT transition by the
preceding actual product construction. Intersect its true source with the
original ambient chart target and shrink its inverse target accordingly;
no source open is replaced by a global product. On the resulting true
neighbourhood, the boundary equations are independent of normal coordinates,
so their intrinsic equations give precisely the ambient equations. The same
extended chart straightens $\operatorname{val}(D)$, proving its regularity
jointly rather than assuming an image-regularity equivalence. Closedness is
closedness of $D$ followed by the closed embedding of $N$. Empty interior
follows from the intrinsic empty-interior condition when $N=X$; in positive
codimension it follows directly from the normal coordinate equations. Source
mark-one cosupport containment follows from functorial evaluation of the
literal restricted ideal. Only AFTER these geometric facts are proved may
actual blowup existence be invoked, followed by the actual closed-contact
universal comparison with the prescribed intrinsic blowup. This final
embedded native-chart/open-domain transport is not supplied by the product
model theorem and remains to be implemented separately.
