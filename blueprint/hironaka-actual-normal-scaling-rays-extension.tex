\section{Genuine partial-normal scaling and the nonzero translated ray}

\begin{definition}[The actual normal scaling with the original tangent projection fixed]
\label{hir:actual-normal-scaling-linear-map}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-entire-germ-retraction}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScale,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScale_tangent,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScale_normal,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.tangentProjection_actualNormalScale}
\leanok
Let $T$ be the genuine tangent projection followed by the original
tangent inclusion in $\mathbb C^{d+t}$. Define the actual continuous
complex linear map $L_c=T+c(1-T)$. It fixes each tangent coordinate and
multiplies each normal coordinate by the true complex scalar $c$.
The actual entire tangent projection is unchanged by $L_c$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
Both the original projection and inclusion are actual continuous complex
linear maps, so their composite and the displayed linear combination
are such maps. In a tangent coordinate $Tz$ equals $z$, hence the
formula gives $z$. In a normal coordinate $Tz$ is zero, hence it gives
$cz$. Coordinate extensionality in the true tangent model proves the
whole projection identity. Empty coordinate blocks obey the same rules.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The genuine nonzero normal-scaling coordinate and germ equivalences]
\label{hir:actual-normal-scaling-whole-germ-equivalence}
\uses{hir:actual-normal-scaling-linear-map}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScaleLinearEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScaleGermEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.hilbertSamuelValue_actualNormalScale}
\leanok
For $c\ne0$, the actual inverse of $L_c$ is $L_{c^{-1}}$. These actual
maps construct a genuine continuous complex linear equivalence and its
actual pullback equivalence of the entire original complex holomorphic
germ algebras. Mapping any entire original ideal under this genuine
algebra equivalence preserves every original HS value.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
In each actual tangent coordinate both composites are the identity.
In each actual normal coordinate both composites multiply by
$c^{-1}c=cc^{-1}=1$. The preceding continuous linear maps supply both
continuity fields, so they give the actual claimed coordinate
equivalence. Genuine original holomorphic germ pullback along these
actual inverse linear maps gives inverse ring homomorphisms and fixes
the original complex constants, hence a true complex algebra
equivalence. The original whole quotient dimension theorem for genuine
complex germ algebra equivalences preserves the maximal ideal and all
its powers, proving every unchanged original HS equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The finite normal-homogeneous translated polynomial has its actual scalar factor]
\label{hir:actual-normal-scaling-translated-polynomial}
\uses{hir:actual-normal-scaling-whole-germ-equivalence}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScaleGermEquiv_tangentPullback,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScaleGermEquiv_normalCoordinate,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualTranslatedNormalPolynomialEval,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualNormalScaleGermEquiv_translatedPolynomial}
\leanok
Let $P$ be a genuine finite normal polynomial of degree $q$, homogeneous
with coefficients in the entire original tangent holomorphic germ ring.
Evaluate it at the actual normal coordinates translated by $cp$. Its
actual pullback by $L_c$ is $c^q$ times the actual evaluation translated
by $p$. Every whole actual tangent germ is fixed by this pullback.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Represent a genuine tangent germ by a true holomorphic function. The
whole tangent projection identity in the first node makes its actual
composite representative identical after pullback, so the entire
original germ is fixed. The true normal-coordinate expression gives
pullback of a normal germ equal to $c$ times that germ; original complex
constants remain constants. Thus each translated coordinate with
parameter $cp$ pulls back to $c$ times its translate with parameter $p$.
Expand the genuine finite polynomial as its actual sum of monomials.
A monomial of degree $q$ acquires exactly $q$ factors of $c$, while its
whole tangent coefficient remains fixed. Every supported monomial has
that same degree by genuine homogeneity. Summing gives the asserted
actual original germ identity, with the numerical factor $c^q$ retained.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual whole translated normal-family ideal is constant in HS along the nonzero ray]
\label{hir:actual-translated-normal-family-nonzero-ray-hs}
\uses{hir:actual-normal-scaling-translated-polynomial}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualTranslatedNormalFamilyIdeal,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.map_actualNormalScale_translatedFamily,Hironaka.HironakaActualNormalScalingRays.actualTranslatedNormalFamily_nonzero_ray_hs}
\leanok
For any actual indexed family $P_i$ of genuine finite normal-homogeneous
polynomials with entire original analytic tangent coefficients, let
$J_p$ be the entire ideal spanned by their actual evaluations translated
by $p$. For every $c\ne0$, the true scaling germ equivalence maps the
entire $J_{cp}$ onto the entire $J_p$. Hence their unchanged original
Hilbert--Samuel values agree at every order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The image-of-span identity and the preceding actual generator formula
show that every image generator is $c^{q_i}$ times the corresponding
original target generator, proving one entire inclusion. Conversely,
the true complex factor $c^{q_i}$ is nonzero and has its actual inverse
as an original constant germ. Multiply the same image generator by that
inverse to obtain the exact target generator in the entire image ideal.
Span proves the reverse inclusion, allowing different genuine degrees
for different generators. Apply the genuine whole germ algebra HS
invariance from the second node to this exact entire ideal equality.
\end{proof}

This is genuine nonzero-ray invariance. Comparison with the ray origin
still needs an actual jointly holomorphic coefficient family and its
original closed HS superlevel theorem. No such comparison is assumed or
claimed here; general positive-dimensional Bennett remains open.
