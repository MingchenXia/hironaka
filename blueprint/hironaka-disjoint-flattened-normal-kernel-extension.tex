\section{The same actual flattening supplies its whole normal trace kernel}
\begin{theorem}[Original vanishing inclusion gives the entire flattened normal kernel]
\label{hir:disjoint-same-flattening-normal-kernel}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-whole-hadamard}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaDisjointFlattenedNormalKernel.ker_planeTrace_le_adaptedIdeal}
\leanok
Let $S$ be an actual subset of a complex manifold of dimension $d+t$, and
let $\Theta$ be a supplied local holomorphic equivalence in the actual chart
at $y$, sending $y$ to zero. Its source lies in the chart target, and on its
whole actual chart domain membership in $S$ is equivalent to membership of
$\Theta(z)$ in the standard $d$-plane. If an actual germ ideal $A$ contains
the original vanishing ideal of $S$, then the ENTIRE kernel of the actual
normal-plane trace is contained in the entire adapted ideal of $A$ at $y$
in this SAME $\Theta$. No coherence or radicality of $A$, global closedness
or regularity of $S$, or target-kernel certificate is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each of the $t$ normal coordinates define an actual function near $y$
by composing that coordinate with the supplied flattening and the original
manifold chart. It is holomorphic near $y$: the manifold chart and the
flattening are holomorphic on their genuine open domains, and coordinate
projection is linear. On that whole chart domain the flattening assumption
forces this function to vanish at every point of $S$. It is therefore an
original member of the vanishing ideal of $S$ at $y$, and the original ideal
inclusion makes it a member of $A$ there.

The adapted germ of this actual function is precisely the corresponding
original normal coordinate germ. Indeed zero belongs to the genuine open
target of $\Theta$. For all coordinates in that neighbourhood, the inverse
flattening lands in its source, then in the original manifold chart target;
the two actual inverse identities give
$\Theta(e_y(e_y^{-1}(\Theta^{-1}(v))))=v$. Projection to the chosen normal
coordinate proves equality of these ENTIRE holomorphic germs. The definition
of the adapted ideal as the ideal generated by actual adapted members now
places every normal coordinate germ in the whole adapted ideal of $A$.
Closing under all holomorphic multiples and finite sums includes the entire
normal-coordinate ideal. Whole relative Hadamard division identifies this
normal-coordinate ideal with the full kernel of the actual plane-trace
homomorphism. This proves the asserted inclusion while retaining the supplied
flattening throughout. For $t=0$ the normal-coordinate family is empty and
the same proof gives the zero kernel inclusion.
\end{proof}
