\section{Identity and composition of the entire original global smooth arrows}

Write $T(M)$ for the actual global terminal manifold constructed from the
original marked ideal $M$, and $p_M:T(M)\to X$ for its original projection.
For a smooth map $f:Y\to X$, the actual global arrow
$G_f:T(f^*M)\to T(M)$ is already constructed without compactness, together
with its Cartesian holomorphic square and its intrinsic uniqueness.  Here the
identity and composition laws concern those same entire arrows.  They do not
construct a globally scheduled noncompact blowup history.

\begin{theorem}[Actual terminal manifolds transport along equality of whole marked records]
\label{hir:original-global-terminal-whole-record-transport}
\uses{hir:original-input-global-terminal-genuine-complex-manifold}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFunctor.terminalRecordEquiv,
Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFunctor.terminalRecordEquiv_proj,
Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFunctor.terminalRecordEquiv_trans}
\leanok
An equality of entire original marked records induces a genuine
biholomorphism between their actual global terminal manifolds, commuting with
their whole original projections.  These biholomorphisms compose according to
transitivity of equality.  Only the original domain hypotheses are required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Identify the two whole marked records using their given equality. Their
domain proofs are proofs of the same proposition, so the terminal
constructions are the same original construction.  Take the identity
biholomorphism. Its projection equality is reflexive. For three equal records,
identify both successive equalities in turn; both the successive transport
and the direct transport are the same identity biholomorphism. This proves
the composition equality on their entire actual terminal manifolds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The entire original global arrow preserves identity]
\label{hir:original-arbitrary-target-global-smooth-arrow-identity}
\uses{hir:original-global-terminal-whole-record-transport,hir:original-arbitrary-target-global-intrinsic-smooth-cartesian-uniqueness,hir:smooth-terminal-full-square-biholomorphic-transport}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFunctor.originalGlobalSmoothMap_identity}
\leanok
For every original marked ideal in its original invariant domain, the entire
global smooth arrow over the identity of the ambient manifold is exactly the
biholomorphism induced by the actual whole-record equality
$\operatorname{id}^*M=M$.  Thus, after this literal identification of source
records, it is the identity of the same actual global terminal manifold. No
compactness or supplied terminal history is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Whole marked pullback along the identity is the original record: its carrier
and marking are unchanged, its ordered boundary is mapped by the identity,
and its coherent germ ideal pulls back to itself. The first theorem gives the
corresponding actual terminal biholomorphism $e:T(\operatorname{id}^*M)\to
T(M)$ with $p_M e=p_{\operatorname{id}^*M}$.

This biholomorphism has a Cartesian square over the identity. Explicitly, for
a holomorphic cone $(a,b)$ satisfying $p_M b=a$, its unique lift is $e^{-1}b$;
the projection identity and both inverse identities verify the cone equations
and uniqueness. The already constructed global arrow over the identity is
holomorphic, commutes with these projections, and has the same Cartesian
property. Intrinsic uniqueness for the entire original global arrows
therefore identifies it with $e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The entire original global arrows preserve smooth composition]
\label{hir:original-arbitrary-target-global-smooth-arrow-composition}
\uses{hir:original-global-terminal-whole-record-transport,hir:original-arbitrary-target-global-intrinsic-smooth-cartesian-uniqueness,hir:smooth-terminal-whole-holomorphic-pullback-pasting,hir:smooth-terminal-full-square-biholomorphic-transport}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaSmoothSubmersionOriginalGlobalFunctor.originalGlobalSmoothMap_composition}
\leanok
For arbitrary smooth maps $Z\xrightarrow{h}Y\xrightarrow{f}X$ and original
marked input $M$, let $e:T((f\circ h)^*M)\to T(h^*(f^*M))$ be the actual
terminal biholomorphism induced by the whole-record pullback-composition
identity. Then
\[
 G_f\circ G_h\circ e=G_{f\circ h}
\]
on the entire original global source terminal manifold. Neither source nor
intermediate nor target is assumed compact. All arrows and all domain
hypotheses in this equality are constructed from the original smooth maps and
the original marked input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original smooth domain theorem supplies the domain for $f^*M$ and then
for $h^*(f^*M)$; composition of genuine smooth maps supplies the domain for
$(f\circ h)^*M$. Whole coherent pullback identifies their carriers, entire
ordered boundaries, ideals and markings, hence gives the actual full-record
equality. The first theorem constructs $e$, and its projection equality is
$p_{h^*(f^*M)}e=p_{(f\circ h)^*M}$.

The true global projection identities give
$p_M G_fG_he=f p_{f^*M}G_he=fh p_{h^*(f^*M)}e=fh
p_{(f\circ h)^*M}$. The composite is holomorphic. Paste the two actual
Cartesian squares for $G_f$ and $G_h$: a cone first has its unique lift through
$G_f$, and then its unique lift through $G_h$; the two uniqueness statements
give uniqueness of the pasted lift. Transport this pasted square along the
genuine source biholomorphism $e$, using its inverse on every cone lift.
The composite $G_fG_he$ therefore has the same whole Cartesian square and
projection identity as the independently constructed $G_{f\circ h}$.
Intrinsic uniqueness on the entire original global source identifies these
two holomorphic arrows.
\end{proof}
