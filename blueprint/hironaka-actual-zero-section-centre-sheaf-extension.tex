\section{The whole centre ideal sheaf on an actual zero section}

The following statements concern the genuine ideal sheaf of holomorphic
functions vanishing on a subset, at every point of the base. They are not
statements about the origin algebra alone. A subset need not be analytic,
closed, smooth or nonempty for the sheaf dictionary. Its additional centre
geometry, when required for a blowup, is a separate hypothesis on that
blowup. The actual zero-section conclusion has no supplied extension,
coherence, finite-generation or desired ideal-identity certificate.

\begin{theorem}[A holomorphic retraction gives both whole vanishing-sheaf directions]
\label{hir:whole-holomorphic-retraction-centre-sheaf}
\uses{ana:geometric-setting}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualZeroSectionCentreSheaf.pullback_vanishingIdeal_image_of_holomorphic_retraction}
\leanok
Let $X$ and $Y$ be genuine complex manifolds of possibly different
dimensions, and let $i:X\to Y$ and $p:Y\to X$ be holomorphic maps with
$pi=\operatorname{id}_X$. For every subset $C\subset X$, the entire
vanishing ideal sheaves satisfy
\[
 i^*\mathcal I_{i(C)}=\mathcal I_C.
\]
Here $i^*$ is the actual ideal generated by pullbacks of ambient
holomorphic germs, and the equality holds at every $x\in X$, including
points outside $C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in X$ and an actual scalar function $g$ on $X$. If the germ of
$g$ belongs to the left side, it is holomorphic at $x$ and, near $x$,
is a finite sum $\sum_j a_j(b_j\circ i)$, where $a_j$ is holomorphic
near $x$ and $b_j$ is an actual germ-function vanishing on $i(C)$ near
$i(x)$. Continuity of $i$ pulls the true vanishing neighbourhood of
each $b_j$ back to a neighbourhood of $x$. Intersect these finitely
many neighbourhoods with the neighbourhood where the sum identity
holds. At any point $z$ in this neighbourhood that lies in $C$,
$i(z)\in i(C)$, so every $b_j(i(z))$ is zero and $g(z)=0$.
This is precisely actual membership in $\mathcal I_C$ at $x$.

Conversely suppose the actual function $g$ is holomorphic at $x$ and
vanishes on $C$ near $x$. Use the actual ambient function $b=g\circ p$.
Because $p(i(x))=x$ and $p$ is holomorphic, $b$ is holomorphic at
$i(x)$. Continuity of $p$ pulls the true vanishing neighbourhood of
$g$ back to a neighbourhood of $i(x)$. If a point $y=i(z)$ in that
neighbourhood lies in $i(C)$, choose its defining $z\in C$.
The actual retraction identity gives $p(y)=z$, and therefore
$b(y)=g(z)=0$. Thus $b$ itself is an actual germ-function in
$\mathcal I_{i(C)}$ at $i(x)$. The whole equality
$(g\circ p)\circ i=g$ follows pointwise from $pi=\operatorname{id}$,
so $g$ is an actual pulled-back generator. Its coefficient one is
holomorphic. This proves the reverse stalk inclusion. Since $x$ and
$g$ were arbitrary, extensional equality gives the entire ideal sheaf
identity. The argument makes no assertion that an arbitrary bare
ideal-membership predicate is closed under pullback; it uses the
actual finite-span definition and actual vanishing germs directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual zero section supplies the whole centre-sheaf dictionary internally]
\label{hir:actual-zero-section-whole-centre-sheaf}
\uses{hir:relative-normal-original-split,hir:whole-holomorphic-retraction-centre-sheaf}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualZeroSectionCentreSheaf.actual_zeroSection_centre_preimage,Hironaka.HironakaActualZeroSectionCentreSheaf.actual_zeroSection_centre_sheaf}
\leanok
For any nonnegative integers $d,t$, let
$i:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^{d+t}$ be the genuine coordinate inclusion
$z\mapsto(z,0)$. For every subset $C\subset\mathbb C^d$,
\[
 i^{-1}(i(C))=C,\qquad
 i^*\mathcal I_{i(C)}=\mathcal I_C.
\]
The maps, holomorphy and retraction are supplied by the actual coordinate
inclusion and projection. In particular the whole ideal-sheaf equality
requires only $d,t,C$ and holds at all base points and all actual
holomorphic germ-functions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the genuine continuous complex linear coordinate projection
$p:\mathbb C^{d+t}\to\mathbb C^d$. The actual coordinate formulas give
$p(i(z))=z$ for every $z$. If $i(x)=i(z)$ with $z\in C$, applying $p$
gives $x=z\in C$; the reverse set inclusion follows from $i(x)\in i(C)$
when $x\in C$. Both actual linear maps are holomorphic everywhere.
Apply the preceding theorem to these very maps and their internally
proved retraction. Empty $C$, the whole base, a zero-dimensional base,
and an empty added-normal block require no alternate construction.
\end{proof}

This supplies the whole original centre-ideal input for the genuine
all-dimensions blowup universal property. It does not assert that an
arbitrary subset is a legal smooth centre, construct the induced strict
carrier map, or identify canonical centres in two embeddings.
