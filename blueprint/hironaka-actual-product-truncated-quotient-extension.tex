\section{Entire original coefficient quotients for a smooth product}

Let $B=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^d,0}$ and
$R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{d+t},0}$, with the actual pullback by the first
coordinate projection. Let $I\subset B$ be any whole original ideal and
$J\subset R$ the ideal of the last $t$ coordinates. The following are
quotients by $IR+J^s$, retaining the free-coordinate truncation. Computing
ambient HS subsequently requires the mixed powers of the actual maximal
ideal; a uniform normal cutoff alone is not that computation.

\begin{lemma}[Whole extension ideals are exactly original scalar multiples in the true quotient]
\label{hir:actual-product-entire-extension-ideal-module-quotient}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualExtendedIdealImage,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualExtendedIdealQuotientEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualExtendedIdealQuotientEquiv_mk}
\leanok
For any commutative complex algebras $B\to R$, actual ideals $I\subset B$
and $K\subset R$, the image of $IR$ in the whole ring $Q=R/K$, restricted
to its original $B$-module action, is exactly $I Q$. Hence the true
quotient $R/(IR+K)$ is naturally $B$-linearly equivalent to $Q/IQ$.
Every original numerator keeps its actual two quotient classes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An element of the extended ideal is a finite sum of original multiples
of images of elements of $I$. Taking the actual quotient map sends such
a sum to exactly a finite sum $\sum b_iq_i$ with $b_i\in I$ and $q_i\in Q$.
Conversely choose an original representative in $R$ for each $q_i$; the
same sum is then the quotient image of an element of $IR$. These prove
both inclusions with the original scalar action. The kernel of the
composite $R\to Q\to Q/IQ$ is precisely $IR+K$: a zero class means its
first quotient agrees with that of some member of $IR$, so their
original difference lies in $K$. Surjectivity of both actual quotient
maps and this entire kernel identify the quotients. The construction
sends the class of each $g\in R$ to the class of its actual class in $Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine product truncations have all original coefficient quotient coordinates]
\label{hir:actual-product-normal-truncation-entire-coefficient-quotient}
\uses{hir:actual-product-entire-extension-ideal-module-quotient,hir:relative-normal-basis-actual-entire-quotient-basis,hir:relative-normal-actual-free-quotient-coordinate-ideal}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualNormalPowerCoefficientIdeal,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualProductTruncationEquiv,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualProductTruncationEquiv_monomial_sum}
\leanok
Let $A_s=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N^t:|\alpha|<s\}$. For the genuine original
coordinate projection, the entire original product quotient
$R/(IR+J^s)$ is $B$-linearly equivalent to
$\prod_{\alpha\in A_s}B/I$. Its original complex action is retained.
For every finite original polynomial $\sum_{\alpha\in A_s}a_\alpha z^\alpha$,
the equivalence gives exactly the classes $(a_\alpha\bmod I)_\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Actual original relative Hadamard division and true holomorphic
coefficient separation have already constructed the genuine $B$-linear
monomial equivalence $R/J^s\simeq B^{A_s}$; each basis vector is the class
of its original normal monomial. Under this actual equivalence, $I(R/J^s)$
is exactly the families with every coordinate in $I$. Indeed scalar
multiples have their coordinates in $I$, and conversely a finite such
family is the sum of its coordinate entries times the original unit
coordinate vectors. Quotienting therefore gives $\prod_{A_s}B/I$.
Compose with the preceding true extension-ideal quotient comparison.
The existing genuine monomial combination formula shows that every
original finite polynomial has the stated coefficient tuple. Restricting
scalars along the original complex algebra action preserves these same
maps and every original class.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact whole product membership and finite dimensions]
\label{hir:actual-product-normal-truncation-coefficient-membership-dimension}
\uses{hir:actual-product-normal-truncation-entire-coefficient-quotient}
\lean{Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualProductPolynomial_mem_iff,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualProductTruncationDimension,Hironaka.HironakaActualProductTruncatedQuotient.actualProductTruncationFinite}
\leanok
A genuine lower-degree original polynomial
$\sum_{\alpha\in A_s}a_\alpha z^\alpha$ belongs to $IR+J^s$ if and only if
EVERY whole original coefficient $a_\alpha$ belongs to $I$. If $B/I$ is
finite-dimensional over the original complex field, then
$\dim_\mathbb C R/(IR+J^s)=|A_s|\dim_\mathbb C B/I$; the product quotient
is finite-dimensional. The statements include $s=0$ and zero free
coordinate dimension.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The actual quotient class is zero exactly when its image under the
preceding genuine equivalence is zero, and the literal coefficient
formula says exactly that each original coefficient class vanishes.
For the dimension formula, restrict the genuine $B$-linear equivalence
to the original complex scalars and compute the dimension of its finite
product: each factor is the same actual $B/I$, so the dimension is its
true dimension times the number of original indices. Finiteness follows
from the actual finite product equivalence. When $A_s$ is empty both
quotients are zero and all coordinate membership conditions are empty;
no nonempty-index assumption was used.
\end{proof}
