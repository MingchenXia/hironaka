\section{De Jong alterations over arbitrary fields}

This section uses schemes over a field. The earlier complex-analytic
compactness assumptions and the canonical analytic-resolution goals do not
enter these statements. The source is de Jong's alteration theorem
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{dejong96-alterations}. All scheme, alteration and divisor
terms below have their native meanings in \texttt{DeJongAlterations}.
The complete native producer cone is included as a local vendored package.

\begin{theorem}[Alteration with regular projective compactification]
\label{thm:hironaka-dejong-alteration}
\source{de Jong 1996, Theorem 4.1, printed page 66}
\lean{Hironaka.deJongAlterationTheorem,DeJongAlterations.StableModificationBridge.AlterationUnconditional.deJongAlterationTheorem}
Let $k$ be any field, $X$ an integral separated finite-type $k$-scheme,
and $Z\subsetneq X$ a prescribed closed subset. There are an integral
$k$-variety $Y$, an alteration $f:Y\to X$, and an open immersion
$j:Y\hookrightarrow\overline Y$ into an integral regular projective
$k$-variety such that an effective reduced strict normal-crossings divisor
$D$ on $\overline Y$ has exactly the support
\[
 |D|=j\bigl(f^{-1}(Z)\bigr)\cup
       \bigl(\overline Y\setminus j(Y)\bigr).
\]
The alteration and open immersion commute with the original structure
maps to $\operatorname{Spec}k$. No perfectness or geometric-integrality
hypothesis is imposed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $k$ is algebraically closed. The proved stable-modification
construction accepts four ingredients: existence and uniqueness over the
valuation rings arising in that construction, numerical-stability openness
for its actual generic-smooth families, and projectivity of the resulting
stable modification. The valuation-existence and valuation-uniqueness
theorems provide the first two ingredients. Projectivity is proved using
the actual global marked dualizing line bundle: positive degrees on its
native normalized components give good sections on the original closed
fibres, and the closed-fibre projectivity criterion applies. Stability
openness is proved on one original base neighborhood by extending actual
fibre sections, constructing a relative affine map and comparing a
positive power of that same line bundle with the pulled-back hyperplane
bundle. The resulting positive native component degrees imply numerical
stability; the proper bad-image complement gives the required open set.
Thus all inputs to the generic-smooth alteration construction are proved.
Its nodal-family resolution produces $f,j,\overline Y,D$ with the stated
regularity, projectivity and full strict normal-crossings boundary.

Now let $k$ be arbitrary and put $L=\overline k$. The scheme $X_L$ need
not be integral or reduced. Choose a reduced irreducible component $C_L$
dominating $X$ and descend it to an integral component model $C_E$
over a finite intermediate extension $E/k$. The map $C_E\to X$ is a
finite alteration, while $C_L\to X$ is surjective; consequently the
inverse image of the prescribed proper subset $Z$ remains proper.
Apply the algebraically closed case to $C_L$ and this inverse image.

Finite presentation descends the resulting alteration, open immersion
and projective compactification to one finite intermediate field. Descend
the finitely many actual boundary components and all their intersections
to a common further finite extension and refine the whole diagram to
that level. The genuine faithfully-flat Cartier descent theorem, whole
ideal pullback and sum identities, and descent of regularity and pure
codimension reconstruct the effective reduced strict normal-crossings
divisor there. The original open-immersion Cartesian square and the
surjective scalar projection descend the entire support equality,
including both the original prescribed inverse image and the
compactification complement.

Finally compose the descended alteration with the finite component
alteration to $X$, and compose the structure maps with the finite map to
$\operatorname{Spec}k$. The result is still an alteration, and finite
base-field restriction preserves projectivity of the same
compactification. Its underlying scheme is regular. The retained whole
coefficient triangles and support identity give exactly the native
output over $k$. This is the proved arbitrary-field endpoint imported by
\texttt{Hironaka.deJongAlterationTheorem}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The prescribed-boundary public API]
\label{cor:hironaka-dejong-prescribed-boundary}
\uses{thm:hironaka-dejong-alteration}
\lean{Hironaka.exists_alterationWithBoundary}
For a field $k$, an actual structure map
$p:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ satisfying the native variety condition,
and a closed $Z\ne X$, the type of native
\texttt{AlterationWithBoundary} outputs for the original $p,Z$ is
nonempty. This retains every alteration, structure-map, regularity,
projectivity and strict normal-crossings field, as well as the exact
boundary support above.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the preceding theorem at the original field $k$, then specialize
its universal quantifiers to the given original $X,p$, its variety proof,
and the prescribed $Z$ with its properness proof. The resulting term has
exactly the native nonempty-output type; no replacement of the target,
boundary or coefficient maps is made.
\end{proof}
